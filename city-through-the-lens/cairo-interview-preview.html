📹 Holiday Video Camera

Cairo is a city of ancient wonders and chaotic energy – the Pyramids, the Nile, and bazaars that have thrived for centuries. But filming here comes with unique challenges: intense heat, dust storms, and crowded streets that test your gear and patience. We sat down with Ahmed Hassan, a Cairo‑based videographer who has shot documentaries and travel content across Egypt, to get the real story on filming in the world's largest Arab city. From the Giza Plateau to the Khan el‑Khalili bazaar, Ahmed shares his favourite locations, the gear that survives the dust, and the permits you need to know about.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: The Pyramids of Giza, the Nile, Khan el‑Khalili, and the Citadel.
  • Gear for dust & heat: Why Ahmed favours weather‑sealed bodies and dust‑proof lenses.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need a permit.
  • Golden hour in the desert: His go‑to settings for that warm, dusty light.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out the golden tones of Cairo.

🏛️ Ahmed's Top Filming Locations

"Everyone shoots the Pyramids from the main viewpoint," Ahmed says. "But the real magic is elsewhere." His favourites:

  • Pyramids of Giza – at sunrise: The best light is at 6 AM when the sun rises behind the pyramids. The desert viewpoint offers a classic composition. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) compresses the scene beautifully.
  • Khan el‑Khalili bazaar: One of the world's oldest markets – a maze of alleys, shops, and historic architecture. Visit early morning (9 AM) before the crowds arrive. A fast prime lens (35mm f/1.4) is essential for low‑light interiors.
  • Nile River – at sunset: The felucca boats and the city skyline create a stunning silhouette. Blue hour (after sunset) is the best time for moody shots. A gimbal is essential for smooth footage on the water.
  • Citadel & Mosque of Muhammad Ali: The historic fortress offers panoramic views of the city. Morning is the best time for clear skies and soft light.

📸 The Kit He Actually Uses

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight and weather‑sealed for dusty conditions.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide and detail shots.
  • Second lens: Sony 70‑200mm f/4 – for compressing the pyramids and cityscape.
  • Compact gimbal: DJI RS 3 Mini for smooth walking shots.
  • Dust cover & blower: Essential for keeping dust off the sensor and lens.

⚡ Quick Cairo Filming Tips

  • Protect from dust: Use a weather‑sealed camera and carry a dust blower.
  • Heat management: Keep gear in the shade when not shooting.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep shutter angle correct in bright sunlight.
  • Shoot in flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in religious sites.
