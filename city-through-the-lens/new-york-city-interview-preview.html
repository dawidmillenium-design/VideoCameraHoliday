Local videographer Marcus Rivera reveals the best spots to film Manhattan, Brooklyn, and the hidden corners of NYC – plus the gear that survives the chaos and energy.

New York City is the most filmed city in the world – and for good reason. The energy, the architecture, the diversity – it's a filmmaker's dream. But filming in NYC comes with challenges: crowds are relentless, permits are strict, and the city never sleeps. We sat down with Marcus Rivera, a New York‑based videographer who has shot everything from music videos to travel documentaries, to get the real story on filming in the Big Apple.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: Manhattan skyline, Brooklyn Bridge, and the hidden backstreets of the Lower East Side.
  • Gear for chaos & crowds: Why Marcus favours compact setups and fast primes.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need a permit (and how to get one).
  • Night settings: His go‑to settings for NYC's famous neon-lit streets.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out NYC's gritty, cinematic energy.

🗽 Marcus's Top Filming Locations

  • Manhattan Bridge – at sunset: The view from the pedestrian walkway offers incredible shots of the bridge and the city skyline. Sunset is the best time – the warm light creates a stunning atmosphere.
  • Brooklyn Bridge – at dawn: The iconic bridge is best shot at 6 AM when the light is soft and the crowds haven't arrived. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) captures the full scale.
  • Lower East Side – night streets: The neon-lit streets of the Lower East Side offer endless opportunities for atmospheric night shots. A fast prime lens (f/1.8 or faster) is essential.

📸 The Kit He Actually Uses

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV-1 II – 1-inch sensor, compact, and excellent autofocus.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide and detail shots.
  • Compact gimbal: DJI RS 3 Mini for smooth walking shots through the city.
  • Audio: RØDE Wireless Go II for capturing interviews in noisy environments.
  • Backup: An iPhone 16 Pro for quick B‑roll.

⚡ Quick NYC Filming Tips

  • Check permit requirements – NYC is strict. Always check before filming in public spaces.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep shutter angle correct in bright daylight.
  • Shoot in flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in residential areas.
