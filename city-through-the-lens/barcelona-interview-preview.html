📹 Holiday Video Camera

Barcelona is a city of Gaudí, Gothic architecture, and Mediterranean energy – a filmmaker's dream. But capturing its magic comes with challenges: intense summer sun, crowds at every landmark, and strict permit rules at some locations. We sat down with Marc Serra, a Barcelona‑based videographer who has shot everything from travel content to commercial campaigns across the city, to get the real story on filming in Catalonia's capital. From the unfinished masterpiece of Sagrada Familia at sunrise to the winding alleys of the Gothic Quarter at dusk, Marc shares his favourite locations, the gear that handles the bright Mediterranean light, and the permits you need to know about.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: Sagrada Familia, Park Güell, Gothic Quarter, and Barceloneta beach.
  • Gear for bright sun & crowds: Why Marc favours fast primes, ND filters, and a compact setup.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need a permit (Sagrada Familia interior).
  • Golden hour in the city: His go‑to settings for that warm, Mediterranean light.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out Barcelona's vibrant colours.

🏛️ Marc's Top Filming Locations

  • Sagrada Familia – at sunrise: Gaudí's masterpiece is best shot at 8 AM when the morning sun illuminates the Nativity Façade. The pond on the east side offers a reflection shot. A wide‑angle lens (16–35mm) is essential – and a polarizing filter helps cut through the glare.
  • Park Güell – the serpentine bench: The colourful mosaics and city views are iconic. Early morning (8 AM) is best to avoid crowds. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) lets you isolate details and compress the cityscape.
  • Gothic Quarter – narrow alleys at dusk: The medieval streets come alive at night. Blue hour (just after sunset) is magical – the warm lights in the windows and the narrow alleys create a cinematic atmosphere. A fast lens (f/1.8 or faster) is essential for low‑light handheld shooting.

📸 The Kit He Actually Uses

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight and weather‑sealed for the Mediterranean sun.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 16‑55mm f/2.8 G – perfect for both wide cityscapes and street scenes.
  • Second lens: Sony 70‑200mm f/4 – for compressing the cityscape.
  • Variable ND filter: Essential for keeping shutter angle correct in bright sunlight.

⚡ Quick Barcelona Filming Tips

  • Protect from the sun: Use a lens hood and ND filters.
  • Beat the crowds: Shoot landmarks at sunrise – the light is better and the crowds are thinner.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep shutter angle correct in bright light.
  • Shoot in flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in residential areas.
