Lisbon Through the Lens

Local videographer João Martins on filming cobblestones, trams, and golden hour in Portugal's capital.

3
Years Filming
DJI Pocket 4P
Primary Camera
Alfama
Favorite Spot

Lisbon is a videographer's dream — and nightmare. The golden light bouncing off azulejo tiles is unmatched, but the steep cobblestone streets will shake any handheld footage to pieces. We sat down with João Martins, a local videographer who has filmed Lisbon professionally for three years, to learn his real-world setup and favorite hidden locations.

🎬

João Martins

Lisbon-based videographer · Travel & documentary work · YouTube: @joaolisbon

What's your go-to camera setup for filming Lisbon?

I use the DJI Osmo Pocket 4P for 90% of my work. Lisbon's streets are steep and narrow — a bulky mirrorless rig would be a nightmare. The Pocket 4P's gimbal handles the cobblestones perfectly, and the 1-inch sensor handles the low-light alleys of Alfama beautifully. For B-roll I pair it with my iPhone 16 Pro for ultrawide shots.

What's your #1 tip for filming Lisbon's cobblestones?

Use a gimbal, period. Electronic stabilization (like on the GoPro Hero 13 Black) can't handle the high-frequency vibrations of calçada portuguesa. I also recommend shooting at golden hour — the low angle of light creates beautiful shadows that make the cobblestones look textured rather than just bumpy.

Top 3 locations every visiting videographer should film?

📍 The Lisbon Essentials

  1. Miradouro da Senhora do Monte at sunrise — best panoramic view, no crowds before 7am
  2. Tram 28 in Alfama — shoot from the parallel street, not on the tram itself
  3. LX Factory at golden hour — industrial textures mixed with warm light

How do you handle the harsh midday sun?

Lisbon's white buildings reflect insane amounts of light. I always use a variable ND filter (ND2-400) on the Pocket 4P. Without it, I can't maintain cinematic motion blur. I also shoot vertical (9:16) for Instagram Reels during midday — the tall buildings frame better vertically.

Show us some of your Lisbon footage

Any advice on filming locals and street scenes?

Portuguese people are generally friendly but private. I always ask before pointing my camera at someone's face. For street scenes, I use the Pocket 4P's ActiveTrack to lock onto a subject and let the gimbal do the work. I also shoot lots of detail shots — tiles, trams, food — that don't require permission.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Lisbon safe for solo videographers?

Yes, very safe. Pickpocketing exists in tourist areas like Tram 28, but violent crime is rare. Use a crossbody camera strap and stay aware in crowds.

Best time of year to film Lisbon?

September-October. Fewer tourists, perfect light, and temperatures around 25°C. Avoid August — it's hot and crowded.

Do I need a permit to film in Lisbon?

For personal/travel content, no. For commercial shoots with tripods and crews, you need a permit from Lisbon City Council. Handheld gimbal shooting is fine everywhere.

What lens focal length works best in Lisbon?

20-24mm equivalent. The narrow alleys of Alfama demand wide angles. The Pocket 4P's native field of view is perfect — no adapter needed.
