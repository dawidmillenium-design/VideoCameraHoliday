🇹🇭 City Through the Lens: Bangkok

Local videographer Somchai Tansiri reveals the best spots to film Wat Arun, Chatuchak Market, and Chinatown – plus the gear that survives the heat, humidity, and crowds.

Bangkok is one of the most chaotic, colourful, and photogenic cities on Earth. But filming here is tough – the heat is relentless, the humidity is brutal, and the crowds are everywhere. We sat down with Somchai Tansiri, a Bangkok‑based videographer who has been filming the city for over a decade, to get the real story on capturing Thailand's capital.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: Wat Arun at sunrise, Chatuchak Market, and Chinatown at night.
  • Gear that survives heat & humidity: Why Somchai favours weather‑sealed bodies and fast primes.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need a permit (temples).
  • Night market settings: His go‑to settings for Bangkok's famous neon-lit street food scenes.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out the vibrant colours of Bangkok.

🏯 Somchai's Top Filming Locations

  • Wat Arun – at sunrise: The iconic temple on the Chao Phraya River is best shot at sunrise when the porcelain tiles catch the golden light. Arrive at 6:00 AM for the best light.
  • Chatuchak Market – at dawn: One of the world's largest weekend markets – 15,000 stalls across 35 acres. Visit on a Saturday or Sunday morning – 7:00–9:00 AM is the sweet spot.
  • Chinatown (Yaowarat) – at night: Neon signs, street food, and endless energy. 7:00–10:00 PM is the best time – the neon lights are on, and the street food vendors are in full swing.

📸 The Kit He Actually Uses

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight, great low light, and reliable autofocus.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide and detail shots.
  • Compact gimbal: DJI RS 3 Mini for smooth walking shots through the markets.
  • Audio: RØDE Wireless Go II for capturing interviews in noisy environments.
  • Backup: An iPhone 16 Pro for quick B‑roll.

⚡ Quick Bangkok Filming Tips

  • Protect from heat: Keep gear in the shade when not shooting – heat kills battery life.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep shutter angle correct in bright sunlight.
  • Shoot in flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in temples and markets.
