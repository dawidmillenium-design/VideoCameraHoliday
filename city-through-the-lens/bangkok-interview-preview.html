Bangkok

Local videographer Nont takes us to a 5-star rooftop to film the city's skyline with the Insta360 X5 — real settings, real B-roll, no lab charts.

🎥

Nont

Local Videographer · Bangkok

Nont has been filming Bangkok's streets, rooftops, and canals for over eight years. He specializes in travel documentaries and commercial work for hospitality brands.

Why the Rooftop?

Bangkok's skyline is one of the most cinematic in Southeast Asia, but the real magic happens above the 20th floor. "Everyone films from the ground," Nont says. "But when you go up, you see the layers — the old town, the river, the BTS lines, the new skyscrapers. It tells the story of the city in one frame."

For this shoot, Nont chose a 5-star hotel rooftop with an unobstructed view of the MahaNakhon tower. The timing was deliberate: late afternoon, about 45 minutes before golden hour.

"The light in Bangkok changes fast. You have maybe 20 minutes of perfect golden hour. If you're not set up before it starts, you've already missed it."

Insta360 X5 Settings for Rooftop Filming

Nont used the Insta360 X5 in 360-degree mode, then reframed in post. Here are the settings he recommends for rooftop cityscapes:

  • Resolution: 5.7K 30fps (360 mode) — enough detail for reframing without huge file sizes.
  • Exposure: Manual, set for the highlights. The sky and city lights will blow out if you let the camera decide.
  • ISO: Keep it under 400 if possible. Bangkok's rooftop wind can introduce noise at higher ISOs.
  • Wind Guard: Turn it on. Rooftops are windy, and the X5's Wind Guard makes a noticeable difference in audio clarity.
  • Selfie Stick: Use the invisible selfie stick extended to at least 2 meters. It gives you a pseudo-drone perspective without the drone permits.

⚙️ Nont's Quick Tip

"Don't film the skyline for 60 seconds straight. Film 10-second clips from different angles — one high, one low, one through a railing, one close to the edge. When you edit, you'll have a sequence, not a single boring shot."

Rooftop B-Roll Ideas That Actually Work

Nont shared a shortlist of B-roll shots he always tries to get on a Bangkok rooftop:

  • The reveal: Start with a tight shot of a drink or a phone, then tilt up to the skyline.
  • The walk-by: Use the 360 reframe to have the subject walk past the camera while the city stays in frame.
  • The reflection: Glass tables and infinity pools are everywhere on rooftops. Use them.
  • The transit: Shoot the BTS Skytrain crossing the frame in the distance — it adds motion to a static skyline.
  • The hands: Close-up of hands on a railing, holding a camera, or pointing at a landmark. It grounds the shot.

Challenges of Filming on a Bangkok Rooftop

It's not all golden hour and skyline shots. Nont was honest about the difficulties:

  • Wind: Constant, unpredictable, and it will ruin your audio if you're not using a wind muff or the X5's Wind Guard.
  • Permits: Many rooftops require permission for commercial filming. Always ask the hotel or building management first.
  • Heat: The rooftop surface can be 10–15°C hotter than street level. Batteries drain faster.
  • Crowds: Sunset is peak time. If you want a clean shot, arrive early or shoot on a weekday.

Gear List for This Shoot

  • Camera: Insta360 X5
  • Mount: 3-meter invisible selfie stick
  • Audio: Built-in Wind Guard (no external mic on this shoot)
  • Power: Two spare batteries (rooftop heat drains them fast)
  • Storage: 256GB microSD (5.7K 360 footage fills cards quickly)

What's Next for Bangkok?

Nont is planning a follow-up shoot in Bangkok's old town — specifically the Ong Ang Canal area, which has become a favorite for local filmmakers. "The canal at night, with the lights reflecting on the water, is something you don't see in most Bangkok videos. That's the next one."

Bangkok Filming FAQ

Do I need a permit to film on a Bangkok rooftop?

Yes, for commercial shoots. Most hotels and building management require advance permission. For personal content, it depends on the venue — always ask first.

What's the best time of day for rooftop filming in Bangkok?

Golden hour (roughly 5:30–6:30 PM depending on the season) gives the best light. Blue hour, just after sunset, is also excellent for city lights.

Is the Insta360 X5 good for travel videography?

Yes. The 360-degree capture means you can reframe in post, which is invaluable for travel content. The Wind Guard and 8K sensor make it a strong choice for outdoor and rooftop work.

Can I use a drone instead of a 360 camera on a rooftop?

Drone laws in Bangkok are strict. You need a permit, and many rooftops don't allow drone takeoff/landing. A 360 camera on a selfie stick is a much simpler alternative.
