Why Trust This Guide?

This guide is based on firsthand field testing in Delhi. Our gear recommendations specifically account for the city's extreme 45°C+ summer heat, heavy monsoon humidity, fine dust storms, and chaotic urban environments to ensure your equipment survives the trip.

Delhi is a city of beautiful, chaotic contrasts — ancient Mughal monuments standing beside sprawling modern metro lines, and vibrant street markets operating in the shadow of government complexes. Filming here is incredibly rewarding but requires navigating intense weather extremes, unpredictable traffic, and some of the most complex drone airspace regulations in the world.

In the upcoming Delhi episode of City Through the Lens, we are searching for a local videographer to answer 15 practical questions about capturing India's capital. While we finalize that interview, this guide gives you essential field notes — from the top locations that define the city's visual identity, to the weather patterns that can ruin unprotected gear.

⚡ Key Takeaways — Delhi

  • Best filming seasons: Spring (Feb–Mar) for blooming gardens, and Autumn (Oct–Nov) for clear skies and festivals like Diwali. Avoid extreme summer heat and peak winter smog.
  • Top 3 locations: India Gate (Kartavya Path), Humayun's Tomb, and Chandni Chowk (Jama Masjid).
  • Critical permit rule: Delhi is a strict Red Zone for drones. Flying near the Red Fort, government buildings, or airports will result in immediate confiscation and police involvement.
  • Airport tip: Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL). Take the high-speed Airport Express Metro to the city center (New Delhi Station) in just 20 minutes.

3 Must-Film Locations in Delhi

Use a polarizer to cut through Delhi's atmospheric haze.

Delhi's landmarks offer some of the most striking imagery in South Asia. Capturing them without falling into cliché requires local knowledge and timing. Here are three essential locations and the insider tips that make the difference.

1. India Gate & Kartavya Path

The towering war memorial sits at the end of the grand Kartavya Path boulevard. It is Delhi's most recognizable silhouette, especially when illuminated at night against the backdrop of the Rashtrapati Bhavan (Presidential Estate).

Filming tip: The best light for wide shots is during the golden hour just before sunset. Because Delhi's sun is incredibly harsh, using a variable ND filter is mandatory if you want to maintain cinematic motion blur (180-degree shutter rule). Drones are strictly banned in this entire government zone. Rely on a smooth walking gimbal for tracking shots along the avenue.

2. Humayun's Tomb

This UNESCO World Heritage site is the architectural precursor to the Taj Mahal. Its perfect symmetry, red sandstone, and white marble domes make it a dream for videographers seeking elegant, structured B-roll.

Filming tip: Arrive right at opening time (sunrise). The morning light hitting the white marble dome is spectacular, and you will beat the massive tour groups. Note that tripods are prohibited inside the main complex to protect the 16th-century stonework. A compact gimbal or a camera with excellent IBIS (In-Body Image Stabilization) is essential here.

3. Chandni Chowk & Jama Masjid

The chaotic, vibrant, and historic heart of Old Delhi. Narrow alleys packed with rickshaws, street food vendors, and historic havelis, leading up to the massive Jama Masjid mosque.

Filming tip: This area is tight, crowded, and dusty. Bring a rugged, pocketable camera like the DJI Osmo Action 5 or a DJI Pocket 4 Pro. You can shoot discreetly from the hip or chest. Always ask permission before pointing a lens directly at locals, especially women, and be prepared to negotiate with shopkeepers if you want to film from their rooftops for the famous Jama Masjid wide shots.

Delhi's Weather Reality for Videographers

Delhi has an extreme subtropical climate. Understanding these seasons is vital for protecting your gear from dust, heat, and moisture.

  • Summer (April–June): Brutal heat (often exceeding 45°C / 113°F). Batteries will drain rapidly, and sensors can overheat during 4K recording. Late afternoons bring Aandhi (violent dust storms). Keep your camera in a sealed bag when not in use to prevent fine dust from ruining your lens elements.
  • Monsoon (July–September): High humidity and sudden, torrential downpours. Condensation is a major risk when moving from air-conditioned hotels to the humid outdoors. Ensure your gear is water-resistant and bring plenty of microfiber cloths.
  • Autumn & Spring (Oct-Nov & Feb-Mar): The absolute best times to film. Clear blue skies, blooming flowers, and comfortable temperatures (20–30°C).
  • Winter (December–January): Cold, but more importantly, plagued by dense smog and fog. While the air quality is poor, the thick morning mist can create incredibly moody, cinematic establishing shots of the monuments if you can brave the early mornings.
Pro tip for harsh light and haze: Delhi's sun creates extreme contrast, and atmospheric haze is common year-round. Always use a Circular Polarizer (CPL) to cut through the glare on marble surfaces and deepen the blue of the sky. In post-production, rely on the Dehaze slider to bring back contrast lost to smog.

Airport Transfer & Gear Logistics

For almost all international travelers, Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL) is the gateway.

  • Getting to the city: Skip the taxis if you are carrying expensive camera bags. The Airport Express Metro is fast, heavily air-conditioned, and connects directly to New Delhi Railway Station in just 20 minutes (cost: ~₹60). It is the safest and most reliable way to transport gear through the city's notorious traffic.

Pro tip for security: Indian airport security is strict. Keep your gimbal batteries and drone batteries (if you have permits) in your carry-on. Lithium batteries are strictly prohibited in checked luggage. You may be asked to power on your camera at security checkpoints.

Permits, Etiquette & The Reality of Filming in Delhi

Delhi is a politically sensitive capital, and filming regulations are enforced rigorously.

  • Drones: Delhi is a Red Zone. Consumer drones are effectively banned across the entire urban core. Flying near the Red Fort, India Gate, or government ministries will result in police confiscation and potential legal trouble. Leave the drone at home unless you have secured commercial permits months in advance.
  • Tripods and Monopods: The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) strictly prohibits tripods inside almost all historical monuments (Red Fort, Humayun's Tomb, Qutub Minar) to prevent damage and crowding. A monopod is sometimes tolerated, but a handheld gimbal is your best option.
  • Religious Etiquette: When filming at temples (like Akshardham) or mosques (Jama Masjid), you must remove your shoes and often cover your head. Akshardham strictly bans all photography and videography inside the main complex and on the premises—leave your camera in your hotel locker.
