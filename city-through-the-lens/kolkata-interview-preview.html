Kolkata Filming Guide: Nikon Z6 III in Festival

One question, asked in the 50 largest metro areas on Earth: how do you really film your hometown? Local shooters answer with gear, settings, light, permits, and etiquette — no lab charts, no stock footage.

Kolkata is India's cultural capital — a city of poets, filmmakers, and the grandest festival on Earth: Durga Puja. The city is a sensory overload of colonial-era architecture, trams clattering through tree-lined avenues, and the relentless energy of its people. Filming here is a test of your ability to capture chaos beautifully. The iconic landmarks — the Victoria Memorial, Howrah Bridge, and the sprawling Dakshineswar Kali Temple — are surrounded by a sea of humanity, especially during the Durga Puja season when the city transforms into an open-air carnival of pandals (temporary temples), lights, and revelry. The heat can be intense, the humidity is relentless, and the festival crowds are unlike anything else on Earth.

In the upcoming Kolkata episode of City Through the Lens, we are searching for a local videographer to answer 15 practical questions about filming in India's most culturally rich city. While we finalize that interview, this guide gives you the essential field notes — from the three locations that capture Kolkata's soul, to the festival strategies that help you navigate the chaos, and the local etiquette that can make or break your shoot.

⚡ Key Takeaways — Kolkata

  • Best filming seasons: October–March offers the most comfortable weather (15–30°C, low humidity).
  • Top 3 locations: Victoria Memorial, Howrah Bridge, and Dakshineswar Kali Temple.
  • Critical permit rule: Drones are banned in most areas. Tripods are allowed but can be problematic in crowds.
  • Airport tip: Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport (CCU) is about 30–45 minutes from the city center.

3 Must-Film Locations in Kolkata

Kolkata's landmarks are visually stunning and culturally rich. These three locations offer the best variety — and they're the ones we'll be asking our local interviewee to break down in detail.

1. Victoria Memorial

The Victoria Memorial is Kolkata's most iconic landmark — a stunning white marble building built in memory of Queen Victoria. It's a blend of Mughal and British architectural styles, set in lush gardens that are a favorite gathering spot for locals. The building's grand domes, angel statues, and reflective pools make it a visual treasure trove.

Filming tip: The best time to film the Victoria Memorial is sunrise or golden hour, when the white marble glows in the warm light. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) is essential for capturing the full scale of the building. Tripods are allowed in the gardens but are prohibited inside the museum. The reflective pools in the front garden offer a great opportunity for symmetrical shots. Be prepared for crowds — this is one of Kolkata's most popular sites.

2. Howrah Bridge

Howrah Bridge is an iconic cantilever bridge that spans the Hooghly River, connecting Kolkata with its twin city of Howrah. It's a symbol of the city and one of the busiest bridges in the world. The bridge's massive steel structure and the bustling riverbanks make it a favorite subject for filmmakers and photographers.

Filming tip: The best time to film Howrah Bridge is sunset or night when the bridge and the city lights create a dramatic silhouette. For a unique perspective, take a ferry on the Hooghly River — you'll get stunning shots of the bridge from the water. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) is ideal for capturing the full span of the bridge. Tripods are allowed along the riverbanks, but be aware of the crowds and traffic. For a less crowded angle, cross the bridge and shoot from the Howrah side.

3. Dakshineswar Kali Temple

Dakshineswar Kali Temple is a riverside temple complex dedicated to Goddess Kali. It's one of the most important Hindu pilgrimage sites in India, known for its spiritual energy and the famous saint Ramakrishna Paramahamsa who once served as a priest here. The temple's red-and-white spires and the views of the Hooghly River make it a visually stunning location.

Filming tip: The best time to film Dakshineswar is early morning (before 9 AM) when the crowds are thinner and the light is soft. A fast prime lens (like a 35mm f/1.8) is ideal for capturing the intricate details of the temple architecture. Tripods are allowed outside the temple but are prohibited inside the sanctum. Be respectful — this is an active place of worship. Always ask permission before filming priests or devotees. The riverside ghats offer great views of the temple complex and the Hooghly River.

Kolkata's Weather Reality for Videographers

Kolkata has a tropical wet-and-dry climate, with extreme heat, high humidity, and a dramatic monsoon season. The city's weather is a defining factor for filmmakers — both a challenge and an opportunity.

  • Winter (October–March): The most pleasant season. Temperatures range from 15–30°C with low humidity. The skies are clear, and the light is beautiful. This is the best time to film — you'll avoid the worst of the heat and rain. Durga Puja (usually in October) is the most photogenic time — the city is alive with pandals, lights, and processions.
  • Summer (April–June): Extremely hot and humid, with temperatures often exceeding 35°C. The heat is brutal on gear and people alike. Shoot early morning (before 8 AM) or late evening (after 5 PM) to avoid the midday sun. A weather-sealed camera is essential — the dust and heat can damage non-sealed gear. Keep spare batteries cool — heat drains them faster.
  • Monsoon (July–September): The city receives heavy rainfall, often leading to flooding. The humidity is near 100%, and the skies are often dramatic. This can create stunning visuals — rain-soaked streets, flooded markets, and dramatic cloud formations — but you need rain covers and anti-fog wipes for your lenses. Silicon rain covers are essential.
Pro tip for Durga Puja shooting: If you're filming during Durga Puja, the pandals (temporary temples) are illuminated with colorful lights — a visual feast for videographers. Use a fast lens (f/1.8 or faster) to handle the mixed lighting. A gimbal is essential for following the crowds. Be prepared for massive crowds — arrive early and stake out your spot. And always keep your gear secure — the crowds are intense.

Airport Transfer & Gear Logistics

Kolkata's Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport is well-connected to the city. Here's how to get your gear safely to your hotel:

  • Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport (CCU): Located about 30–45 minutes from the city center, depending on traffic. The best way to get to the city is by prepaid taxi (available at the airport) or ride-hailing apps like Uber and Ola. The Airport Express bus is a budget option but can be crowded and slow. Avoid the local trains — they're overcrowded and difficult with camera bags.

Pro tip for traffic: If you're flying in, schedule your transfer to avoid peak hours (8–11 AM and 5–8 PM). The roads are often clogged, so build extra time into your plans. Keep your camera gear securely packed — the bumpy roads can jostle bags.

Permits, Etiquette & The Reality of Filming in Kolkata

Kolkata is a bustling, culturally rich city where filming regulations are often loosely enforced — until they're not. Here's what you need to know to avoid trouble:

  • Drones: Drones are strictly banned in most of Kolkata due to security concerns. You need permits from the DGCA and local police — a process that can take weeks and is rarely approved for tourists. Do not fly your drone — you risk confiscation and fines.
  • Tripods and gimbals: Tripods are generally allowed in public spaces like the Victoria Memorial grounds and the riverbanks, but they're difficult to use in crowded areas like festivals and markets — they block pedestrian flow. A compact gimbal (like the DJI RS 3 Mini) is a much more practical choice for street and festival filming. Be prepared to move quickly — the crowds don't stop for anyone.
  • Filming people: India has robust privacy laws. Always ask permission before filming individuals, especially in close-up shots. The best approach is to be friendly and respectful — a smile and a "Can I film you?" (in Bengali or English) goes a long way. Many locals are happy to be filmed, but some will refuse, and you must respect that. In busy festivals, it's often best to shoot the crowds as a whole rather than singling out specific people.
  • Temple etiquette: When filming at Dakshineswar or other temples, remove your shoes before entering the sanctum. Avoid filming inside the main shrine — it's considered disrespectful. Ask permission before filming priests or devotees. Be mindful of your behavior — these are active places of worship.
