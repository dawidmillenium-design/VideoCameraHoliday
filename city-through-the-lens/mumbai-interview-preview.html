Mumbai is the maximum city — a relentless, overcrowded, monsoon-soaked metropolis that never sleeps. It is the heart of Bollywood, the financial capital of India, and one of the most visually chaotic places on Earth. Filming here is a test of endurance, gear, and street smarts. The city's iconic landmarks — the Gateway of India, Marine Drive, and the Victorian Gothic railway stations — are surrounded by a sea of humanity that never stops moving. The monsoon rains transform the city into a dramatic, waterlogged stage, while the heat and dust test the limits of your camera's weather-sealing.

In the upcoming Mumbai episode of City Through the Lens, we are searching for a local videographer to answer 15 practical questions about filming in India's most photogenic — and challenging — city. While we finalize that interview, this guide gives you the essential field notes — from the three locations that define Mumbai's visual identity, to the monsoon strategies that keep your gear safe, and the Bollywood production hacks that locals swear by.

⚡ Key Takeaways — Mumbai

  • Best filming seasons: November–February offers the most comfortable weather (18–32°C, low humidity).
  • Top 3 locations: Gateway of India, Marine Drive, and Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CST).
  • Critical permit rule: Drones are banned in most areas. Tripods are allowed but can be problematic in crowds.
  • Airport tip: Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM) is within the city itself — factor in traffic.

3 Must-Film Locations in Mumbai

Mumbai's landmarks are iconic, but capturing them without the usual tourist clichés requires local knowledge. These three locations offer the best variety — and they're the ones we'll be asking our local interviewee to break down in detail.

1. Gateway of India

The Gateway of India is Mumbai's most famous monument — a basalt arch built to commemorate the visit of King George V and Queen Mary in 1911. It overlooks the Arabian Sea and is a popular gathering spot for locals and tourists alike. The monument itself is photogenic, but the real magic is the surrounding chaos: the street vendors, the boatmen, and the endless flow of people.

Filming tip: The best time to film the Gateway is just before sunset when the golden light warms the basalt. For a unique perspective, take one of the ferry boats to Elephanta Island — you'll get a dynamic, moving shot of the Gateway from the water. Tripods are allowed in the open plaza, but you'll need to be aware of crowds — early morning (before 8 AM) is the best time for a clean shot. Security is present, but they generally allow tripods if you're not blocking the flow.

2. Marine Drive

Marine Drive is a 3 km promenade along the Arabian Sea, curving around Back Bay. It's famously known as the "Queen's Necklace" because of the streetlights that look like a string of pearls when viewed from above. It's a favorite spot for filmmakers — the sea, the sunset, and the Art Deco buildings create a stunning visual.

Filming tip: The classic Marine Drive shot is sunset to blue hour, capturing the transition from orange to deep blue. Set up near the Taraporewala Aquarium end for a wide view of the bay. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) is essential for capturing the sweep of the crescent. Tripods are allowed along the promenade, but be aware of the crowds — especially on weekends. The sea-facing hotels (like the Oberoi) often allow photographers on their rooftops with permission, offering a bird's-eye view.

3. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CST)

Formerly known as Victoria Terminus, CST is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most stunning railway stations in the world. Its Victorian Gothic architecture, with spires, turrets, and stained glass, is a masterpiece of design. It's also a functioning railway station — one of the busiest in India — so the chaos of commuters adds an energetic backdrop.

Filming tip: The best time to shoot the exterior is early morning or sunset, when the light hits the stone facade. For the interior, you'll need to be respectful — it's a working station. Tripods are not allowed inside during peak hours, so use a fast prime lens (like a 24mm f/1.4) and a gimbal for handheld work. The footbridge opposite the station offers a great elevated perspective. Be mindful of security — they're used to tourists, but they will ask you to leave if you're disruptive.

Mumbai's Weather Reality for Videographers

Mumbai has a tropical climate with distinct wet and dry seasons. The monsoon (June–September) is the most challenging and dramatic time for filming — it transforms the city into a waterworld, with flooded streets and intense downpours. Here's what to expect:

  • Winter (November–February): The most pleasant season. Temperatures range from 18–32°C with low humidity. The skies are clear, and the light is beautiful. This is the best time to film — you'll avoid the worst of the heat and rain.
  • Summer (March–May): Extremely hot and humid, with temperatures often exceeding 35°C. The heat and dust are brutal on gear. Weather-sealed cameras and a dust blower are essential. Shoot early morning or late evening to avoid the midday sun.
  • Monsoon (June–September): The city is drenched in rain. The humidity is near 100%, and the streets flood regularly. This is a high-risk, high-reward season — the dramatic clouds, reflections, and waterlogged streets create incredible visuals, but your gear is at constant risk. A weather-sealed camera (like the Canon C70) and a silicon rain cover are non-negotiable. Bring multiple microfiber cloths for drying lenses.
Pro tip for monsoon shooting: Embrace the rain — it's part of Mumbai's identity. Use slow shutter speeds (1/15 – 1/30) to create motion blur in the rain streaks, and push your ISO to 800-1600 to maintain exposure. A circular polarizer (CPL) can cut through glare on wet surfaces. After the shoot, dry your gear thoroughly with a soft cloth and silica gel packs in your camera bag to prevent mould.

Airport Transfer & Gear Logistics

Mumbai's airport is within the city limits, but traffic is legendary. Plan your transfer carefully to avoid sitting in gridlock with your gear.

  • Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM): Located in the western suburbs, about 30–45 minutes from the city center (South Mumbai) depending on traffic. The best way to get to the city is by prepaid taxi (available at the airport) or ride-hailing apps like Uber and Ola. Avoid the local trains during peak hours — they're overcrowded and difficult with camera bags. The Airport Express bus (operated by BEST) is a budget option, but it's slower and less comfortable.

Pro tip for traffic: If you're flying in, schedule your transfer to avoid peak hours (8–11 AM and 5–8 PM). The Western Express Highway is often clogged, so consider taking the local train from the airport's nearby station (Vile Parle) to Churchgate if you're traveling light — it's an experience in itself, but not recommended with a full camera kit.

Permits, Etiquette & The Reality of Filming in Mumbai

Mumbai is a bustling, chaotic city where filming regulations are often loosely enforced — until they're not. Here's what you need to know to avoid trouble:

  • Drones: Drones are strictly banned in most of Mumbai due to security concerns (the city houses the Navy and other sensitive installations). You need permits from the DGCA (Directorate General of Civil Aviation) and local police — a process that can take weeks and is rarely approved for tourists. Do not fly your drone — you risk confiscation and fines.
  • Tripods and gimbals: Tripods are generally allowed in public spaces like Marine Drive and the Gateway plaza, but they're not permitted inside CST during peak hours. A compact gimbal (like the DJI RS 3 Mini) is a much more practical choice for street filming. Be mindful of crowds — you will be constantly jostled.
  • Filming people: India has robust privacy laws. Always ask permission before filming individuals, especially in close-up shots. The best approach is to be friendly and respectful — a smile and a "Can I film you?" (in Hindi or English) goes a long way. Many locals are happy to be filmed, but some will refuse, and you must respect that.
  • Security and public places: Mumbai has a visible police presence, especially around monuments and railway stations. They are generally tolerant of tourists with cameras, but they will intervene if you're blocking pedestrian flow or behaving disruptively. If in doubt, ask a security guard — they're used to tourists and can often guide you to permitted filming spots.
