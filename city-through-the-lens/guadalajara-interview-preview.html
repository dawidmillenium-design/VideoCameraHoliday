📹 Holiday Video Camera

Guadalajara is a city of mariachi, tequila, and colonial architecture – the cultural heart of western Mexico. But filming here comes with challenges: intense heat, bright sun, and crowds at the most famous landmarks. We sat down with Carmen Vázquez, a Guadalajara‑based videographer who has shot travel content and documentaries across the city, to get the real story on filming in Mexico's second‑largest city. From the twin towers of the Cathedral at sunrise to the colourful streets of Tlaquepaque at golden hour, Carmen shares her favourite locations, the gear that survives the heat, and the permits you need to know about.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: Cathedral, Hospicio Cabañas, Tlaquepaque, and the historic centre.
  • Gear for heat & sun: Why Carmen favours weather‑sealed bodies, ND filters, and a good camera strap.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need a permit (Hospicio Cabañas interior).
  • Golden hour in the city: Her go‑to settings for that warm, Mexican light.
  • Editing style: How she grades her footage to bring out the vibrant colours of Guadalajara.

🏛️ Carmen's Top Filming Locations

  • Cathedral – at sunrise: The twin towers are Guadalajara's most iconic landmark. 7 AM is the best time – the soft morning light illuminates the golden sandstone. A wide‑angle lens (16–35mm) captures the full scale of the plaza.
  • Hospicio Cabañas – the murals: The UNESCO‑listed building houses stunning murals by José Clemente Orozco. Mid‑morning (10 AM) is the best time – the light streams through the windows and illuminates the murals. A fast prime lens is essential for the low‑light interior.
  • Tlaquepaque – the colourful streets: The historic neighbourhood is famous for its artisanal shops and colourful buildings. Golden hour (just before sunset) is the best time for warm light. A fast prime lens (35mm or 50mm) is perfect for street scenes.

📸 The Kit She Actually Uses

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight and weather‑sealed for the heat.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide and detail shots.
  • Second lens: Sony 85mm f/1.8 – for portraits and isolating details.
  • Variable ND filter: Essential for keeping shutter angle correct in bright sunlight.

⚡ Quick Guadalajara Filming Tips

  • Protect from heat: Keep gear in the shade when not shooting.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep shutter angle correct in bright light.
  • Shoot early morning and late afternoon to avoid the harsh midday sun.
  • Shoot in flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in markets and religious sites.
