📹 Holiday Video Camera

Beijing is a city of imperial grandeur and rapid modernization – the Forbidden City, ancient hutongs, and futuristic skyscrapers standing side by side. But filming here comes with unique challenges: spring dust storms, thick winter smog, strict drone bans, and crowds that can overwhelm even the most patient shooter. We sat down with Chen Wei, a Beijing‑based documentary filmmaker who has shot everything from historical features to travel content across the capital, to get the real story on filming in China's political and cultural heart. From the golden roofs of the Forbidden City at dawn to the quiet alleyways of the hutongs at sunset, Chen shares his favourite locations, the gear that survives the dust, and the permits you absolutely need to know about.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: The Forbidden City, Temple of Heaven, and the historic hutongs of Shichahai.
  • Gear for dust & smog: Why Chen swears by weather‑sealed bodies, dust blowers, and a polarizing filter to cut through haze.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely (hutongs), where you need a permit (Forbidden City), and why drones are a non‑starter.
  • Golden hour in the capital: His go‑to settings for that crisp autumn light that cuts through the pollution.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out the warm, historic tones of Beijing's architecture.

🏯 Chen's Top Filming Locations

"Everyone shoots the Forbidden City from the Meridian Gate," Chen says. "But the real magic is in the details – and the timing." His favourites:

  • The Forbidden City (Gugong) – at sunrise: The 72‑hectare palace complex is Beijing's most iconic location. Arrive at 8:00 AM and be first through the Meridian Gate. Head straight to the Hall of Supreme Harmony for that classic elevated shot before the crowds flood in. Drones are strictly banned – you'll be stopped by security immediately. A wide‑angle lens (16–35mm) is essential for capturing the grand scale of the courtyards.
  • Temple of Heaven – morning tai chi: The circular Temple of Heaven is surrounded by a vast park where locals practice tai chi and play traditional instruments. 7:00 AM to 9:00 AM is the sweet spot – the light is soft and the park is alive with activity. Ask permission before filming individuals – most locals are happy to be filmed but appreciate the courtesy. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) lets you capture candid moments from a respectful distance.
  • Hutongs – Shichahai & Nanluoguxiang: The traditional alleyways are the soul of old Beijing. Early morning (6 AM) is best for quiet, atmospheric shots of courtyard life and street vendors. The Shichahai lake area offers reflections of the ancient buildings – a polarizing filter helps cut through the glare and deepen the blue of the sky.

"If you want a drone shot, forget it. Drones are banned in most of Beijing – the entire city center is a no‑fly zone. You can get similar aerial views from Jingshan Park, just north of the Forbidden City – the hill offers a panoramic view that's perfect for a telephoto shot."

📸 The Kit He Actually Uses

Chen is a Sony shooter, but his kit is built for Beijing's unique conditions:

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight, weather‑sealed, and excellent dynamic range for Beijing's contrasty light.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide cityscapes and detail shots of imperial architecture.
  • Second lens: Sony 70‑200mm f/4 – for compressing the cityscape from Jingshan Park and isolating details in the hutongs.
  • Compact gimbal: DJI RS 3 Mini for smooth walking shots through the hutongs.
  • Dust blower & lens wipes: Absolutely essential for Beijing's spring dust storms and winter smog.
  • Variable ND filter: Keeps shutter angle correct in the bright, hazy light.

"The ZV‑E10 II is my favourite for travel because it's small enough to carry all day, but the image quality is professional – especially when you grade the S‑Log footage properly. The weather‑sealing is a lifesaver during the dust storms."

⚡ Quick Beijing Filming Tips

  • Protect from dust: Never change lenses outdoors during a dust storm. Use a dust blower on your sensor and lens every evening.
  • Deal with smog: Shoot in S‑Log or a flat profile and use a Dehaze tool in post‑production to restore contrast.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep your shutter angle correct in Beijing's bright, hazy light.
  • Check the air quality index (AQI) before heading out – if it's over 150, consider indoor locations like the National Museum.
  • Charge your batteries – the cold winter air drains them quickly.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in religious sites and residential hutongs.
