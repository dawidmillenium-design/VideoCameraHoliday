City Through the Lens: Seoul, South Korea

Local videographers reveal how they film their hometown — the real locations, permit hurdles, and techniques you won't find in guidebooks.

Quick Takeaways for Filmmakers

  • Best filming season: Cold winters (-10°C), hot humid summers
  • Top 3 landmarks: Gyeongbokgung Palace, Myeongdong Night Market, Bukchon Hanok Village
  • Main airport: Incheon
  • Drone rules: banned in Seoul without permit.
  • Featured camera: Fujifilm X-T5

3 Must-Film Locations in Seoul

1. Gyeongbokgung Palace

Filming Tip: Changing guard 10 AM/2 PM; rent hanbok for free entry; tripods OK in courtyards.

2. Myeongdong Night Market

Filming Tip: 6 PM-midnight; street food neon; handheld gimbal essential.

3. Bukchon Hanok Village

Filming Tip: Residential area; quiet filming only; no tripods blocking alleys.

Weather Reality Check

Cold winters (-10°C), hot humid summers. Best Apr-Jun and Sep-Nov.

Pro tip: Plan your shoot dates around these conditions. The "best" months mean better light, fewer crowds, and more predictable weather windows.

Airport Transfer & Arrival Tips

Incheon (ICN) 60min AREX or Gimpo (GMP) 30min metro.

Factor in transfer time when booking flights. Arriving early gives you buffer for gear checks and acclimatization before shooting.

Permits, Rules & Local Etiquette

Palace filming OK for personal use. Drones banned in Seoul without permit.

Remember: Always ask before filming people, especially in religious or residential areas. Respect local customs — it's the difference between getting great footage and being asked to delete it.
