Paris is the most filmed city in the world – but capturing its magic without the clichés takes local know‑how. We sat down with Sophie Moreau, a Paris‑based videographer who has shot campaigns for luxury brands and travel channels, to get the real story on filming in the City of Light. From the Trocadéro at sunrise to the hidden alleyways of Montmartre, Sophie shares her favourite locations, the gear that handles the unpredictable weather, and the permits you need to avoid a fine.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: Lesser‑known angles of the Eiffel Tower, Seine riverbanks, and Montmartre.
  • Gear that survives rain & crowds: Why Sophie favours weather‑sealed bodies and fast primes.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely, where you need a permit, and how to get one in 24 hours.
  • Golden hour secrets: Her go‑to settings for that warm, dreamy Parisian glow.
  • Editing style: How she grades her footage to enhance the city's romantic, timeless feel.

🗼 Sophie's Top Filming Locations

"Everyone shoots the Eiffel Tower from Trocadéro," Sophie says. "But the real gems are a few streets away." Her favourites:

  • Rue de l'Université – view of the tower: A quiet street that frames the Eiffel Tower perfectly, especially with the cobblestones in the foreground.
  • Pont Alexandre III – sunrise: The ornate bridge with the Grand Palais in the background – best shot at dawn when the light is soft and the streets are empty.
  • Montmartre – backstreets: Skip the Sacré‑Cœur steps; head to Rue des Abbesses for authentic Parisian charm and fewer tourists.
  • Seine riverbanks – blue hour: The reflections from the bridges and the glow of the city lights create a magical atmosphere – handheld with a fast lens is all you need.

📸 The Kit She Actually Uses

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight, great low light, and reliable autofocus.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide cityscapes and detail shots.
  • Compact gimbal: DJI RS 3 Mini for smooth walking shots without drawing attention.
  • Audio: RØDE Wireless Go II for capturing clean interviews even in crowded squares.
  • Backup: An iPhone 16 Pro for quick B‑roll and behind‑the‑scenes content.

⚡ Quick Paris Filming Tips

  • Check the weather – rain can come suddenly; always carry a rain cover or a clear plastic bag.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep your shutter angle correct during bright days.
  • Shoot in S‑Log or C‑Log to get the most dynamic range for grading that Parisian mood.
  • Bring extra batteries – you'll be walking all day, and cold weather drains them faster.
  • Be polite – ask permission before filming people, especially artists and vendors.
