🇨🇴 City Through the Lens: Bogotá

Local videographer Carlos Rueda reveals the best spots to film La Candelaria, Monserrate, and Bogotá's vibrant street art – plus the gear that handles the altitude and the rain.

Bogotá is a city of contrasts – colonial architecture, soaring mountains, and a street art scene that rivals any in the world. But filming here comes with unique challenges: an altitude of 2,640 metres, unpredictable rain, and bustling crowds that can make or break your shoot. We sat down with Carlos Rueda, a Bogotá‑based videographer who has shot everything from music videos to travel campaigns, to get the real story on filming in Colombia's capital. From the cobblestone streets of La Candelaria to the panoramic views from Monserrate, Carlos shares his favourite locations, the gear that survives the altitude, and the permits you need to avoid a fine.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: La Candelaria, Monserrate, and the street‑art alleys of the city centre.
  • Gear for altitude & rain: Why Carlos favours weather‑sealed bodies and fast lenses for Bogotá's variable light.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely, where you need a permit, and how to navigate the city's security.
  • Golden hour at 2,640 metres: His go‑to settings for that crisp, high‑altitude light.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out Bogotá's vibrant colours and dramatic skies.

🏙️ Carlos's Top Filming Locations

"Everyone shoots the view from Monserrate," Carlos says. "But the real magic is in the streets below." His favourites:

  • La Candelaria – colonial streets: The historic centre is a maze of cobblestone streets, colourful walls, and colonial balconies. The best light is in the early morning (6–8 AM) when the streets are quiet and the sun hits the facades at a perfect angle.
  • Plaza de Bolívar: The main square is surrounded by the Cathedral, the Capitol, and the Palace of Justice. Shoot from the southwest corner for a composition that includes both the cathedral and the mountain backdrop. Weekday mornings are less crowded.
  • Street art alleys (Carrera 3 & 4): Bogotá is famous for its street art – entire walls covered in murals by local and international artists. The alleys around La Candelaria and San Felipe are the best spots. A wide‑angle lens (16–35mm) is essential to capture the full scale of the murals.
  • Monserrate – at sunrise: The cable car or funicular takes you to the top of the mountain (3,152 metres) for a panoramic view of the entire city. Sunrise is the best time – the light is soft and the smog hasn't rolled in yet. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) lets you isolate details in the city below.

"If you want a drone shot, be careful – Bogotá's altitude and wind patterns can make flying unpredictable. Check local restrictions and always keep your drone in visual line of sight."

📸 The Kit He Actually Uses

Carlos is a Sony shooter, but his kit is built for Bogotá's unique conditions:

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight, weather‑sealed, and excellent dynamic range for Bogotá's contrasty light.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide cityscapes and detail shots.
  • Second lens: Sony 70‑200mm f/4 G – for compressing the city from Monserrate and isolating details.
  • Compact gimbal: DJI RS 3 Mini for smooth walking shots through La Candelaria's cobblestones.
  • Audio: RØDE Wireless Go II for capturing interviews and ambient sound in the bustling streets.
  • Rain cover: A clear rain cover is essential – Bogotá's rain can appear out of nowhere.

"The ZV‑E10 II is my favourite for travel because it's small enough to carry all day, but the image quality is professional – especially when you grade the S‑Log footage properly. The weather‑sealing is a lifesaver in Bogotá's unpredictable rain."

⚡ Quick Bogotá Filming Tips

  • Altitude matters: You'll feel the thin air – take it slow, especially when carrying heavy gear.
  • Check the weather: Rain can appear suddenly – always carry a rain cover and lens wipes.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep your shutter angle correct during bright mountain light.
  • Shoot in S‑Log or flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading.
  • Charge your batteries – the cold at altitude drains them faster.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in religious sites and residential areas.
