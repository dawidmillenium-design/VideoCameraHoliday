📹 Holiday Video Camera

Berlin is a city of layered history – imperial monuments, Cold War relics, and cutting‑edge modern architecture standing side by side. But filming here comes with unique challenges: unpredictable rain, freezing winters, and strict permit rules around government buildings. We sat down with Lena Schmidt, a Berlin‑based documentary filmmaker and travel content creator who has shot everything from historical features to music videos across the city, to get the real story on filming in Germany's capital. From the Brandenburg Gate at sunrise to the colourful murals of the East Side Gallery at golden hour, Lena shares her favourite locations, the gear that handles the cold and rain, and the permits you need to know about.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: Brandenburg Gate, East Side Gallery, Museum Island, and the Reichstag.
  • Gear for cold & rain: Why Lena favours weather‑sealed bodies, fast primes, and a good rain cover.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need a permit (Reichstag, government district).
  • Golden hour in the capital: Her go‑to settings for that soft, moody Berlin light.
  • Editing style: How she grades her footage to bring out the gritty, historic character of Berlin.

🏛️ Lena's Top Filming Locations

"Everyone shoots the Brandenburg Gate from Pariser Platz," Lena says. "But the real magic is in the details – and the right light." Her favourites:

  • Brandenburg Gate – at sunrise: Berlin's most iconic landmark is best shot at dawn (around 6 AM in summer) when the soft golden light hits the sandstone and the crowds haven't arrived. A wide‑angle lens (16–35mm) is essential to capture the full scale of the gate and the Quadriga. A tripod or monopod is recommended for sharp low‑light shots – just be mindful of security guards who may ask you to move.
  • East Side Gallery – the longest open‑air gallery: The 1.3‑km stretch of the Berlin Wall is covered in colourful murals. Golden hour (just before sunset) is the best time – the warm light brings out the vibrant colours. Visit on a weekday morning to avoid the crowds. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) lets you isolate details in the murals, while a wide‑angle captures the sense of scale.
  • Museum Island – the Pergamon and Alte Nationalgalerie: The UNESCO‑listed museum complex offers stunning neoclassical architecture and river views. Blue hour (just after sunset) is magical – the museums are beautifully lit and the Spree River reflects the lights. A fast lens (f/1.8 or faster) is essential for low‑light handheld shooting – tripods are often restricted on the bridges.

"If you want to film the Reichstag, you need advance permission – the government district is heavily secured. But you can get a fantastic view from the Dome (free entry with registration) – just bring a polarizing filter to cut through the glare on the glass."

📸 The Kit She Actually Uses

Lena is a Sony shooter, but her kit is built for Berlin's unpredictable weather:

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight, weather‑sealed, and excellent dynamic range for Berlin's moody light.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide cityscapes and detail shots.
  • Second lens: Sony 85mm f/1.8 – for portraits and isolating details in the murals and architecture.
  • Compact gimbal: DJI RS 3 Mini for smooth walking shots along the Spree and through the streets of Mitte.
  • Rain cover & lens wipes: Absolutely essential for Berlin's sudden showers.
  • Variable ND filter: Keeps shutter angle correct in the bright, overcast light.

"The ZV‑E10 II is my favourite for travel because it's small enough to carry all day, but the image quality is professional – especially when you grade the S‑Log footage properly. The weather‑sealing is a lifesaver during the rain."

⚡ Quick Berlin Filming Tips

  • Check the weather: Berlin weather is notoriously changeable – always carry a rain cover and lens wipes.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep your shutter angle correct in overcast conditions.
  • Shoot in S‑Log or flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading Berlin's moody skies.
  • Charge your batteries – the cold winter air drains them quickly.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in residential areas and museums.
  • Register for the Reichstag Dome in advance – it's free but requires prior booking.
