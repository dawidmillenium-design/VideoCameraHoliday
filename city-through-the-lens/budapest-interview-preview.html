📹 Holiday Video Camera

Budapest is a city of stunning architecture, thermal baths, and the majestic Danube River – a filmmaker's dream. But filming here comes with its own challenges: freezing winters, frequent mist, and crowds at the most famous landmarks. We sat down with András Kovács, a Budapest‑based videographer who has shot travel content, documentaries, and commercial campaigns across the city, to get the real story on filming in Hungary's capital. From the panoramic views of Buda Castle at dawn to the glittering lights of Parliament at dusk, András shares his favourite locations, the gear that handles the cold and damp, and the permits you need to know about.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: Buda Castle, Hungarian Parliament, Chain Bridge, and the thermal baths.
  • Gear for cold & mist: Why András favours weather‑sealed bodies, fast lenses, and a good rain cover.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need a permit (Parliament interior).
  • Golden hour on the Danube: His go‑to settings for that dramatic Budapest light.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out the warm, historic tones of Budapest.

🏛️ András's Top Filming Locations

  • Buda Castle – at sunrise: The castle hill offers panoramic views of the Danube and Parliament. 6 AM is the best time – the soft morning light illuminates the city and the mist often hangs over the river. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) compresses the Parliament and Chain Bridge beautifully.
  • Hungarian Parliament – at night: The Parliament building is spectacularly lit after dark. Blue hour (just after sunset) is the best time – the building's lights reflect in the Danube. A tripod is essential for long‑exposure shots.
  • Chain Bridge – at golden hour: The bridge connects Buda and Pest. Sunset offers warm light and a stunning silhouette. A wide‑angle lens (16–35mm) captures the full scene.

📸 The Kit He Actually Uses

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight and weather‑sealed for the cold.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide cityscapes and detail shots.
  • Second lens: Sony 85mm f/1.8 – for portraits and isolating details in the architecture.
  • Rain cover & lens wipes: Essential for the damp, misty weather.

⚡ Quick Budapest Filming Tips

  • Protect from cold: Keep spare batteries in your inner pocket to keep them warm.
  • Deal with mist: Use a Dehaze tool in post‑production to restore contrast.
  • Use a tripod for long‑exposure night shots of Parliament and the bridges.
  • Shoot in flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in residential areas.
