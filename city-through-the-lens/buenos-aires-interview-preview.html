📹 Holiday Video Camera

Buenos Aires is a city of passion – tango, football, and architecture that blends European elegance with Latin American energy. But filming here comes with its own challenges: intense summer heat, sudden rainstorms, and bustling crowds that can overwhelm your shoot. We sat down with Mateo Ferraro, a Buenos Aires‑based videographer who has shot everything from music videos to travel documentaries, to get the real story on filming in Argentina's capital. From the colourful houses of La Boca to the tree‑lined streets of Palermo, Mateo shares his favourite locations, the gear that survives the humidity, and the permits you need to know about.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: La Boca, Palermo, Recoleta Cemetery, and the historic centre.
  • Gear for heat & humidity: Why Mateo favours weather‑sealed bodies and fast primes for Buenos Aires' variable light.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need a permit.
  • Golden hour in the city: His go‑to settings for that warm, golden light.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out Buenos Aires' vibrant colours.

🏙️ Mateo's Top Filming Locations

"Everyone films the Caminito in La Boca," Mateo says. "But the real magic is in the details." His favourites:

  • La Boca – Caminito: The colourful street museum is iconic, but visit early morning (8 AM) before the crowds arrive. The best light hits the corrugated iron buildings at 9 AM. A wide‑angle lens (16–35mm) is essential for capturing the full scene.
  • Palermo – Soho & Hollywood: The trendy neighbourhoods offer tree‑lined streets, street art, and fashionable locals. Weekday mornings are quietest. The Botanical Garden and the Rosedal (Rose Garden) are perfect for nature‑inspired B‑roll.
  • Recoleta Cemetery: One of the world's most beautiful cemeteries – a maze of ornate mausoleums. Sunrise is the best time for soft light and fewer tourists. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) lets you isolate details in the architecture.
  • Plaza de Mayo & Casa Rosada: The political heart of Argentina. The pink palace is best shot at sunset when the light warms the façade. Handheld shooting is recommended – tripods may attract security attention.

📸 The Kit He Actually Uses

Mateo is a hybrid shooter, but his kit is built for Buenos Aires' unique conditions:

  • Primary camera: Sony ZV‑E10 II – lightweight, weather‑sealed, and excellent dynamic range.
  • Go‑to lens: Sony 24‑70mm f/2.8 G Master – versatile for both wide cityscapes and detail shots.
  • Second lens: Sony 85mm f/1.8 – for portraits and isolating details.
  • Compact gimbal: DJI RS 3 Mini for smooth walking shots.
  • Audio: RØDE Wireless Go II for capturing interviews and ambient sound.
  • Rain cover: Essential for the sudden summer storms.

⚡ Quick Buenos Aires Filming Tips

  • Hydrate: Summer temperatures can hit 35°C – drink water and protect your gear from heat.
  • Check the weather: Rain can appear suddenly – carry a rain cover.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep your shutter angle correct in bright sunlight.
  • Shoot in flat profile to get the most dynamic range for grading.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in residential areas.
