Baghdad is a city of ancient history and resilient spirit – the Tigris, the Old City, and markets that have thrived for centuries. Filming here comes with unique challenges: intense heat, dust, and security considerations that require careful planning. We sat down with Ahmed al‑Rashid, a Baghdad‑based videographer who has shot documentaries and cultural content across Iraq, to get the real story on filming in one of the world's oldest cities. From the Tigris riverbanks to the Mutanabbi Street book market, Ahmed shares his favourite locations, the gear that survives the dust, and the permits you need to know about.

🎯 What You'll Learn in This Interview

  • Best cinematic spots: The Tigris River, Mutanabbi Street, the Old City, and the historic bazaars.
  • Gear for dust & heat: Why Ahmed favours weather‑sealed bodies and dust‑proof lenses.
  • Permits & etiquette: Where you can film freely and where you need security clearance.
  • Golden hour on the Tigris: His go‑to settings for that warm, golden light.
  • Editing style: How he grades his footage to bring out the historic tones of Baghdad.

🏛️ Ahmed's Top Filming Locations

  • Tigris River – at sunset: The riverbanks offer stunning views of the city skyline. Blue hour (after sunset) is the best time for moody shots.
  • Mutanabbi Street: The historic book market is a cultural treasure. Visit Friday morning when the street is alive with readers and vendors.
  • Old City – bazaars: The narrow alleys and historic architecture offer endless compositional opportunities. A fast prime lens is essential for low‑light interiors.

⚡ Quick Baghdad Filming Tips

  • Protect from dust: Use a weather‑sealed camera and carry a dust blower.
  • Security awareness: Always check local guidelines before filming sensitive areas.
  • Use a variable ND filter to keep shutter angle correct in bright sunlight.
  • Ask permission – always ask before filming people, especially in religious sites.
