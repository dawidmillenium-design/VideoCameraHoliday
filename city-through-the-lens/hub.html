City Through the Lens: A Global Series on Travel Videography

City Through the Lens is a growing interview series built around a simple idea: the best travel footage comes from people who actually live where they film. Local videographers in 50+ cities reveal their exact camera settings, permit hacks, and hidden filming locations — the practical knowledge that never makes it into a generic travel guide.

Whether you are planning a shoot in a city you have never visited or you are a local creator looking to share what you know, this hub is the starting point. Below you will find how the series works, what each city profile covers, and how to take part.

What Each City Profile Covers

Every interview in the series follows the same structure, so you can compare notes between cities and pull the details you need quickly. Each profile is built around the following sections:

  • Exact camera settings — the settings local videographers actually use in that city's light, streets, and architecture.
  • Permit hacks — how local creators navigate filming permissions, and the practical workarounds they rely on.
  • Hidden filming locations — spots that locals return to, beyond the obvious landmarks.
  • Local context — the conditions, rhythms, and quirks of filming in that specific city.

Why Local Knowledge Matters

Travel videography is often taught in general terms: golden hour, wide establishing shot, move to the detail. That advice travels well, but it does not tell you how a city actually behaves. Local videographers know when a street empties out, which side of a building catches light in the afternoon, and where a permit is genuinely required versus where it is not.

That is the gap this series fills. Instead of one creator's perspective applied everywhere, City Through the Lens collects the on-the-ground knowledge of people who film their own city week after week. The result is a reference library that grows city by city.

How the Series Is Structured

The hub connects individual city interviews. Each one is a standalone profile, but together they form a comparison across cities — useful if you are deciding where to shoot next, or if you want to see how different creators approach similar problems in different places.

Are You a Local Videographer?

We are interviewing creators in every major city. If you film where you live — whether professionally or as a dedicated hobbyist — your local knowledge belongs in this series. Share your local knowledge and get featured to thousands of travel filmmakers.

Contributors are featured directly in the series, with their city profile reaching an audience of travel filmmakers actively looking for exactly this kind of grounded, first-hand information. It is a chance to put your city on the map for the people most likely to film there.

What We Ask Contributors

  • Your city and how long you have been filming there.
  • The camera settings you rely on most in local conditions.
  • Your permit hacks and how you handle permissions.
  • The hidden filming locations you return to.
  • Anything a visiting videographer would get wrong without local guidance.

Join the Series

Joining is straightforward. If you are a local videographer with knowledge to share, the series is open to creators in every major city. Your contribution becomes part of a growing global reference for travel filmmakers — and your work is featured to an audience that is already looking for it.

Explore the city profiles, compare how different creators work, and if you film your own city, add your voice to the series.

FAQ

Who can join City Through the Lens?

Any local videographer filming in a major city. We are interviewing creators in every major city, and contributors are featured to thousands of travel filmmakers.

What do I need to share to be featured?

Your local knowledge: your exact camera settings, permit hacks, and hidden filming locations, along with your perspective on filming in your city.

What do featured videographers get?

Contributors get featured in the series, reaching an audience of thousands of travel filmmakers who are actively seeking local, on-the-ground guidance.
