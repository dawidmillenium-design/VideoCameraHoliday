Karachi is Pakistan's maximum city — a sprawling, chaotic, heat-soaked metropolis on the Arabian Sea coast. It is the country's financial and industrial capital, a city of stark contrasts: colonial-era architecture stands beside modern skyscrapers, while the coastline offers a welcome respite from the urban frenzy. Filming here is a test of endurance, patience, and cultural sensitivity. The city's iconic landmarks — Clifton Beach, the Mazar-e-Quaid (Jinnah's Mausoleum), and the historic Mohatta Palace — are surrounded by a relentless flow of people, traffic, and the ever-present dust. The heat can be brutal, the crowds are unrelenting, and the sea-front light at sunset is nothing short of magical.

In the upcoming Karachi episode of City Through the Lens, we are searching for a local videographer to answer 15 practical questions about filming in Pakistan's largest city. While we finalize that interview, this guide gives you the essential field notes — from the three locations that capture Karachi's unique character, to the heat and dust management strategies that keep your gear safe, and the local etiquette that can make or break your shoot.

⚡ Key Takeaways — Karachi

  • Best filming seasons: November–February offers the most comfortable weather (20–30°C, low humidity).
  • Top 3 locations: Clifton Beach, Mazar-e-Quaid (Jinnah's Mausoleum), and Mohatta Palace.
  • Critical permit rule: Drones are banned in most areas. Tripods are allowed but can be problematic in crowds.
  • Airport tip: Jinnah International Airport (KHI) is about 30 minutes from Clifton Beach — factor in traffic.

3 Must-Film Locations in Karachi

Karachi's landmarks are visually diverse, offering everything from coastal views to Mughal-inspired architecture. These three locations give you the best variety — and they're the ones we'll be asking our local interviewee to break down in detail.

1. Clifton Beach

Clifton Beach is Karachi's most famous coastline — a long stretch of golden sand along the Arabian Sea. It's a popular gathering spot for locals and tourists alike, especially at sunset when the sky turns shades of orange and pink. The beach is lively, with camel rides, horse carriages, and street food vendors adding to the vibrant atmosphere.

Filming tip: The best time to film Clifton Beach is golden hour to sunset — the warm light on the water and the silhouettes of camels and horses create stunning visuals. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) is ideal for capturing the sweep of the beach. Tripods are allowed, but be aware of the tides — you don't want your gear soaked. For a unique perspective, film from the cliffs on the eastern end looking west. Be mindful of the crowds — they peak around 6-7 PM.

2. Mazar-e-Quaid (Jinnah's Mausoleum)

The Mazar-e-Quaid is the final resting place of Pakistan's founder, Muhammad Ali Jinnah. The white marble mausoleum, with its iconic curved arches and massive chandelier, is an architectural masterpiece and a symbol of national pride. The surrounding gardens offer a peaceful retreat from the city's chaos.

Filming tip: The best time to film the Mazar is early morning (before 9 AM) or late afternoon when the light is soft and the crowds are thinner. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) is essential for capturing the full scale of the mausoleum. Tripods are allowed in the gardens but are prohibited inside the mausoleum itself. Be respectful — this is a highly revered site. Security is present and may ask you to keep your distance from the main entrance.

3. Mohatta Palace

Mohatta Palace is a stunning sandstone mansion built in the 1920s, combining Rajasthani and Indo-Saracenic architectural styles. It was originally built for a Hindu businessman and later served as the residence of Jinnah's sister. Today, it's a museum and cultural center. The palace's intricate carvings, jharokhas (overhanging balconies), and lush gardens make it a visual treasure trove.

Filming tip: The best time to film the palace is golden hour, when the warm light brings out the rich colors of the sandstone. A fast prime lens (like a 35mm f/1.8) is ideal for capturing the intricate details and shallow depth of field. Tripods are allowed in the gardens but not inside the building. Be aware of the crowds — the palace is a popular spot for weddings and events, so check the schedule before you go.

Karachi's Weather Reality for Videographers

Karachi has a tropical climate with two distinct seasons — a hot, dry season and a monsoon season. The heat and dust are the most significant challenges for filmmakers.

  • Winter (November–February): The most pleasant season. Temperatures range from 20–30°C with low humidity. The skies are clear, and the light is beautiful. This is the best time to film — you'll avoid the worst of the heat and humidity.
  • Summer (March–June): Extremely hot, with temperatures regularly exceeding 35°C (sometimes hitting 40°C). The heat is brutal on gear and people alike. Shoot early morning (before 8 AM) or late evening (after 5 PM) to avoid the midday sun. A weather-sealed camera is essential — the dust and heat can damage non-sealed gear. Keep spare batteries cool — heat drains them faster.
  • Monsoon (July–September): The city receives moderate to heavy rainfall, leading to flooding in low-lying areas. The humidity is high, and the skies are often dramatic. This can create stunning visuals — but you need rain covers and anti-fog wipes for your lenses. Silicon rain covers are essential.
Pro tip for summer heat and dust: If you're filming in Karachi's summer, never leave your camera in a car — the heat can damage the sensor and battery. Carry a microfiber cloth and a dust blower to clean your gear regularly. In post-production, use a Dehaze tool to cut through the atmospheric haze and restore contrast.

Airport Transfer & Gear Logistics

Karachi's Jinnah International Airport is modern and well-connected to the city. Here's how to get your gear safely to your hotel:

  • Jinnah International Airport (KHI): Located about 30 minutes from Clifton Beach and the city center. The best way to get to the city is by prepaid taxi (available at the airport) or ride-hailing apps like Uber and Careem. The journey can take 30–60 minutes depending on traffic. Avoid the local buses — they're overcrowded and difficult with camera bags.

Pro tip for security: Karachi airport security is thorough. Keep your camera bodies, lenses, and gimbal batteries in your carry-on. Lithium batteries are not allowed in checked luggage. You may be asked to power on your camera, so keep your batteries charged.

Permits, Etiquette & The Reality of Filming in Karachi

Karachi is a bustling, vibrant city where filming regulations are often loosely enforced — until they're not. Here's what you need to know to avoid trouble:

  • Drones: Drones are strictly banned in most of Karachi due to security concerns (the city houses sensitive installations and is near the coast). You need permits from the Civil Aviation Authority and local police — a process that can take weeks and is rarely approved for tourists. Do not fly your drone — you risk confiscation and fines.
  • Tripods and gimbals: Tripods are generally allowed in public spaces like Clifton Beach and the Mazar gardens, but they're difficult to use in crowded areas like the markets — they block pedestrian flow. A compact gimbal (like the DJI RS 3 Mini) is a much more practical choice for street filming. Be prepared to move quickly — the crowds don't stop for anyone.
  • Filming people: Pakistan has privacy laws. Always ask permission before filming individuals, especially in close-up shots. The best approach is to be friendly and respectful — a smile and a "Can I film you?" (in Urdu or English) goes a long way. Many locals are happy to be filmed, but some will refuse, and you must respect that. In busy markets, it's often best to shoot the crowds as a whole rather than singling out specific people.
  • Security and public places: Karachi has a visible police and security presence, especially around government buildings and sensitive sites. They are generally tolerant of tourists with cameras, but they will intervene if you're blocking pedestrian flow or behaving disruptively. If in doubt, ask a security guard — they're used to tourists and can often guide you to permitted filming spots.
