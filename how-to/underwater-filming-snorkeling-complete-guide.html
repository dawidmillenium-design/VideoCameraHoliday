Underwater & Snorkeling Filming: The Complete Travel Guide

After 4 years testing underwater cameras in Thailand, Indonesia, Mexico & the Maldives — here's every setting and gear choice you need

By Dawid Millennium | Published August 31, 2026 | 14 min read | 🤿 How-To

Here's the truth nobody tells you about underwater filming: your $3,000 camera will produce worse footage than a $300 GoPro if you don't understand how light, color, and pressure work underwater.

⚡ Key Takeaways

  • IP ratings are misleading — never exceed 80% of rated depth
  • Best settings: 4K 30fps, shutter 1/60, ISO max 800, custom WB
  • Winner: Insta360 X5 with Aquavision Mode — best color without post-processing
  • Post-dive: Rinse 5 minutes, dry thoroughly, lubricate O-rings every 10 dives

What Makes a Camera Truly Underwater-Ready

Real underwater filming involves pressure changes, temperature shock, salt corrosion, and physical impact.

⚠️ Critical Depth Rule

Never exceed 80% of your camera's rated depth. A camera rated for 10m should only go to 8m.

Best Settings for Underwater Video

  1. Resolution: 4K 30fps or 2.7K 60fps
  2. Shutter Speed: 1/60 or 1/125
  3. ISO: Auto (max 800)
  4. White Balance: Custom or 5500K
  5. Color Profile: Flat/Log if available

Snorkeling vs. Scuba

Snorkeling (0-5m): GoPro Hero 13 with floaty grip, red filter for 3m+, anti-fog inserts.

Scuba (5-40m): Need external video lights below 10m, neutral buoyancy, air management.

💡 The 5-Meter Color Rule

Every 5 meters removes a color. At 5m: no red. At 10m: no orange. At 15m: no yellow. At 20m: everything is blue.

7 Techniques for Stunning Footage

  1. Get close, then closer
  2. Shoot into the light for sun rays
  3. Use the reef as a frame
  4. Follow fish, don't chase
  5. Capture surface-to-underwater transitions
  6. Film at 60fps, edit at 24fps
  7. Film B-roll of entry/exit

Field Test: GoPro Hero 13 vs. Insta360 X5

For pure underwater quality, Insta360 X5 wins thanks to Aquavision. For durability and action, GoPro Hero 13 is unbeatable.

Post-Dive Care

  1. Rinse in fresh water 5 minutes
  2. Dry thoroughly
  3. Check seals
  4. Lubricate O-rings every 10 dives
  5. Store with silica gel
