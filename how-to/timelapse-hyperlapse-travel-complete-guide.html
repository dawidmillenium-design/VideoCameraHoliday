By Dawid Millennium | Published August 31, 2026 | 15 min read | 🌅 How-To

Timelapse is the single most underrated technique in travel filmmaking. It requires zero extra gear — just patience, the right settings, and understanding how time compresses.

⚡ Key Takeaways

  • Timelapse = static, Hyperlapse = moving, Long Exposure = one frame
  • Base settings: Manual mode, 4K, 24fps, fixed WB, manual focus
  • Interval guide: Fast clouds 1s, sunsets 3s, stars 20s
  • Hyperlapse tip: Count 240 steps for 10-second clip at 24fps

Timelapse vs. Hyperlapse vs. Long Exposure

  • Timelapse: Camera stays still, time speeds up. Best for sunsets, clouds, stars.
  • Hyperlapse: Camera moves through space AND time. Best for city walks.
  • Long Exposure: Camera stays still, one shot captures time within a single frame.

Camera Settings That Work

  • Mode: Manual (M)
  • Resolution: 4K minimum
  • Frame Rate: 24fps final output
  • Shutter: 1/2× your interval
  • Aperture: f/8-f/11 landscapes, f/2.8 stars
  • ISO: As low as possible (100-400 daytime)
  • White Balance: Fixed (never Auto)
  • Focus: Manual, locked before starting

⚠️ The #1 Timelapse Killer: Flicker

Flicker happens when auto-exposure adjusts between frames. Fix: Lock exposure, WB, and focus.

Interval Guide by Subject

  • Fast clouds / traffic: 1 second (4 minutes for 10s clip)
  • Sunset / sunrise: 3 seconds (12 minutes)
  • Slow clouds / shadows: 5 seconds (20 minutes)
  • Stars / Milky Way: 20 seconds (80 minutes)
  • Flowers blooming: 60 seconds (4 hours)

7 Travel Timelapse Ideas

  1. Golden Hour Compression (30 min before sunset)
  2. City Walk Hyperlapse (straight line, 240 steps)
  3. Cloud Waterfall (2-second interval)
  4. Day-to-Night Transition (1 hour before sunset)
  5. Star Rotation (point at North Star, 2+ hours)
  6. Tide Change (2-3 hours at beach)
  7. Rooftop Reveal (close-up to wide)

Shooting Hyperlapses Solo Without a Gimbal

  1. Pick a straight line (sidewalk cracks, building edges)
  2. Count your steps (240 for 10-second clip)
  3. Lock composition on subject
  4. Walk heel-to-toe, one photo per step
  5. Stabilize in post (warp stabilizer)

Field Notes: Northern Lights in Iceland

  • Interval: 8 seconds
  • Shutter: 4 seconds
  • Aperture: f/1.8
  • ISO: 1600-3200
  • Focus: Manual, infinity
  • Duration: 1-2 hours

Cold kills batteries — at -15°C, keep spares in your jacket.
