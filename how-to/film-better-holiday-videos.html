12 Pro Tips for Better Holiday Videos

1. 🌅 Shoot During Golden Hour

The hour after sunrise and before sunset provides warm, directional light that flatters everything. Midday sun creates harsh shadows and blown-out highlights.

2. 🎬 Use the Rule of Thirds

Enable grid lines on your camera. Place subjects at intersection points, not dead center. This creates more dynamic, professional-looking frames.

3. 🔊 Audio is 50% of Video

Bad audio ruins good footage. Use an external microphone when possible. At minimum, shield the built-in mic from wind with your hand or a dead cat.

4. 📏 Film in 4K, Deliver in 1080p

Shooting 4K gives you room to crop, stabilize, and reframe in post. Deliver in 1080p for smoother playback and smaller files.

5. 🎥 Capture B-Roll Obsessively

For every talking-head shot, capture 5 detail shots: hands, feet, surroundings, textures, wide shots. B-roll is what makes videos cinematic.

6. ⏱️ Hold Shots for 10 Seconds

Count to 10 before moving the camera. This gives you usable clips for editing and prevents rushed, unusable footage.

7. 🔄 Film Multiple Angles

Shoot the same moment from wide, medium, and close-up. This gives editors (or future you) options to cut between.

8. 🌊 Use Slow Motion Sparingly

120fps slow motion is beautiful but overused. Save it for impactful moments — a wave crashing, a jump into water, a confetti drop.

9. 🎯 Focus on Eyes

In any shot with people, ensure eyes are in focus. Viewers instinctively look at eyes first. Soft eyes make footage feel amateur.

10. 🏔️ Show Scale with Wide Shots

Include a person or object for scale in landscape shots. A mountain looks more impressive with a tiny hiker in the foreground.

11. 🎵 Think About Music Early

Choose your music before editing. Cut to the beat. Music drives pacing more than visuals do.

12. ✂️ Edit Ruthlessly

Cut every shot that doesn't advance the story. Better a 2-minute great video than a 10-minute boring one.
