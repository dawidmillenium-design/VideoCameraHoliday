Dawid Millennium — Travel Videographer

Shot vertical-first through Vietnam and Thailand with a phone, Pocket 3 and GoPro — these are the settings that survived.

🕐 Last updated: August 25, 2026

📋 In This Guide

  • Core Settings for Reels and TikTok
  • Vertical Settings by Camera Type
  • Avoiding Overheating on Long Shooting Days
  • Framing and Composition for 9:16
  • FAQ

For travel Reels and TikTok, the settings that actually work are 4K vertical (2160x3840) at 30fps for talking clips and 60fps for anything with motion — recorded natively vertical, not cropped from horizontal. Native vertical keeps full sensor quality, framing stays honest while you shoot, and 60fps gives you the option of smooth half-speed slow motion in the edit. Below are the exact settings I use per camera, plus the heat management that keeps a Pocket 3 or GoPro alive through a full shooting day.

I switched to vertical-first filming on a two-week trip through Vietnam in spring, when I realized my carefully composed horizontal clips were being cropped into mush by every platform anyway. That trip I shot everything vertically on a phone, a DJI Osmo Pocket 3, and a GoPro Hero 13 Black — and learned where each one shines and where each one melts. Literally, in one case.

⚡ Key Takeaways

  • Shoot native vertical 4K — 2160x3840 — instead of cropping horizontal footage.
  • 30fps for talking and scenery, 60fps for motion, food, and anything you might slow down.
  • Keep shutter near double the frame rate; use ND filters outdoors to hold it there.
  • Overheating is the real vertical-video killer: drop screen brightness, avoid 4K/60 in direct sun, and film in shorter takes.
  • Compose for the center of the frame — platform UI covers the edges.

Why Native Vertical Beats Cropping

Every camera I travel with can shoot vertical natively now, and every one of them does it better than a crop. When you crop horizontal 4K (3840 wide) into a 9:16 frame, you keep only about 1215 pixels of width — barely more than 1080p. It survives on a phone screen, but it falls apart the moment you punch in, stabilize, or add a slight zoom transition in the edit.

Native vertical uses the full sensor height, so your 4K stays 4K. Just as important: you compose correctly while shooting. Cropped shooters constantly discover at the edit desk that someone's head or the interesting part of a street scene sits outside the vertical slice. I lost a lovely Hanoi train-street clip that way. Never again.

Field tip: Rotate the camera before you hit record, not after. On the Pocket 3, flip the screen and it powers on already in vertical mode. On a phone, actually hold it upright — iOS bakes orientation in at record time, and a "vertical-ish" tilt gives you tilted footage.

Core Settings for Reels and TikTok

Whatever the camera, this baseline hasn't failed me yet:

  • Resolution: 4K (2160x3840 vertical). Platforms compress aggressively; starting at 4K means you land around good-looking 1080p after their compression instead of mushy 720p.
  • Frame rate: 30fps for talking-to-camera, architecture, food close-ups. 60fps for walking shots, scooters, waves, cooking action — and anything you might want at half speed.
  • Shutter: roughly double the frame rate (1/60 at 30fps, 1/120 at 60fps). Outdoors this means ND filters; see our harsh sunlight guide for the full midday strategy.
  • ISO: as low as the light allows, and cap auto ISO (400 outdoors, 800-1600 indoors) so the camera doesn't crank gain in dim markets.
  • Stabilization: on, always, for vertical. Handheld vertical footage shows micro-shake more than horizontal because the frame is narrow.
Warning: Don't shoot everything at 60fps "just in case." 60fps files are roughly double the size, need more light per frame, and generate more heat. A week of careless 4K/60 filled a 256GB card on me in Da Nang — and filled it with footage that was mostly talking heads that never needed slow motion. Choose 30 or 60 per shot, on purpose. Card sizing math lives in our memory card guide for 4K video.

Vertical Settings by Camera Type

Phone (iPhone 16 Pro and similar)

Set 4K/30 or 4K/60 in Settings, and record in the native camera app for the most reliable stabilization — or a manual app if you want shutter control. Lock AE/AF with a long press before walking shots so exposure doesn't pump as you pass shadows. The front camera is fine for quick reaction clips but the rear cameras are dramatically better in low light; I flip to rear for anything I care about.

DJI Osmo Pocket 3

This is my main vertical tool. Flip the screen sideways, and it boots straight into vertical shooting with the gimbal reconfigured for 9:16. I run 4K/30, shutter 1/60, ISO capped at 400 outdoors, and ActiveTrack for walk-and-talk clips — it keeps my face framed while I back through a market. The rotating screen is genuinely the best vertical UX of any camera I've used, which is a big reason it scored so well in my DJI Osmo Pocket 3 review.

GoPro Hero 13 Black

The Hero 13's 8:7 sensor mode is the vertical sleeper feature: record in 8:7 and you can pull both vertical and horizontal crops from one file at near-full quality. If you know it's vertical-only, the dedicated 9:16 mode is simpler and easier to frame. I run 4K/60, HyperSmooth on, and keep the lens at Linear for walk-and-talks so buildings don't bow. More field notes in the GoPro Hero 13 review.

Mirrorless (Sony ZV-1 II and up)

Turn the camera on its side — seriously. Most mirrorless bodies record whatever orientation you hold them in, and a vertical grip or simple L-bracket makes it comfortable. Set 4K/30, shutter 1/60, and use a wide lens; a 20mm-equivalent feels tight once you're holding vertically at arm's length. The look is the best of any option here, but for quick travel clips I rarely bother unless the light is bad.

Avoiding Overheating on Long Shooting Days

Vertical filming is usually stop-start — dozens of short clips across a day — which is actually kinder to cameras than one long record. But direct sun plus 4K/60 will still cook small cameras. My rules after losing an afternoon of footage to a heat shutdown in Hoi An:

  • Film in takes under 3-4 minutes and power the camera off (or at least sleep it) between setups. Off-time is cooling time.
  • Drop screen brightness to 50-60%. The screen is a major heat source, and you can't see full brightness in sun anyway.
  • Keep the body shaded: a hat brim, your own shadow, or a light-colored pouch between takes. Never a black case in direct sun.
  • Prefer 4K/30 over 4K/60 in heat unless the shot genuinely needs slow motion.
  • Skip recording while charging in the sun. Charging adds heat on top of recording heat; if you must, use a power bank in the shade with a long cable.
Field tip: If a camera does overheat, don't open doors or pull the battery immediately in humid climates — let it cool slowly in shade to avoid condensation forming inside. Yes, I learned that the hard way too, stepping from an air-conditioned café into Bangkok humidity.

Framing and Composition for 9:16

Vertical changes what "good framing" means. Heads go near the top third, not the center. Horizon lines work best very high or very low — a centered horizon in 9:16 feels like two half-frames stacked. And lead room flips: for walking subjects, leave space above and below rather than ahead.

Most importantly, keep the subject in the central 60% of the frame. Reels and TikTok overlay usernames, captions, like buttons and progress bars along the edges and bottom; anything near the frame edge will be covered by UI. I frame all my food and market clips — the ones in our street food filming guide — dead center for exactly this reason. And when clips come home, a mobile workflow like the one in our phone editing guide takes them from camera roll to posted in minutes.

It's the same discipline the city videographers in our City Through the Lens series describe: shoot for the platform's frame, not the one you wish you had. Vertical isn't a compromise anymore — for travel clips in 2026, it's the main event.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 4K or 1080p better for TikTok and Reels?

Shoot 4K. Both platforms compress uploads hard, and starting from 4K means your video still looks sharp after compression. Uploading 1080p often results in visibly softer footage once the platform re-encodes it.

Should I shoot 30fps or 60fps for vertical travel video?

30fps for talking-to-camera, scenery and food close-ups; 60fps for motion, walking shots and anything you might play at half speed. Shooting everything at 60fps doubles file sizes, demands more light and makes cameras run hotter.

Can I just crop horizontal footage into vertical?

You can, but you lose roughly two-thirds of the horizontal resolution and often discover the subject sits outside the vertical slice. Native vertical recording keeps full 4K quality and forces correct framing while you shoot.

How do I stop my camera overheating when shooting 4K vertical?

Keep takes under 3-4 minutes, lower screen brightness, shade the body between setups, prefer 4K/30 in direct sun, and avoid recording while charging. Small cameras like the Pocket 3 and GoPro cool quickly if you actually power them down between clips.
