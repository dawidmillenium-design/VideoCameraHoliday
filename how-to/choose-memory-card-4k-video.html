For 4K holiday video, buy a V30-rated microSD from a reputable brand (SanDisk, Samsung, or Lexar) purchased from an official store — that covers GoPro, DJI, and most cameras up to about 100–200 Mbps. You only need V60 or V90 if your mirrorless camera shoots high-bitrate All-I codecs above 200 Mbps. Capacity-wise, plan for roughly 100–200 GB per heavy shooting day at 4K. That's the whole answer; the details below will save you from both overpaying and from the fake-card trap that burns thousands of travelers every year.

I learned this in a Marrakech market, where a "512GB SanDisk Extreme" for a suspiciously good price turned out to be a 16GB card in disguise. It reported 512GB to the camera, recorded happily over itself, and silently destroyed three days of footage. More on spotting those fakes below — it nearly ended this blog before it started.

Key Takeaways

  • V30 (30 MB/s minimum sustained write) is enough for nearly all consumer 4K cameras, including GoPro Hero 13 and DJI Pocket 3.
  • V60/V90 only matter for mirrorless cameras recording high-bitrate All-I codecs (200–600+ Mbps).
  • Bitrate math: Mbps ÷ 8 × 60 = MB per minute. A 100 Mbps camera eats about 750 MB per minute, ~45 GB per hour.
  • Carry two 256GB cards rather than one 512GB — a dead card then costs half a trip, not all of it.
  • Buy only from official stores, and test every new card with a full-capacity checker tool before your trip.

Speed Classes Explained in Plain English

Memory card labels are an alphabet soup (C10, U3, V30, A2...), but for video only one matters: the V rating, or Video Speed Class. It guarantees a minimum sustained write speed — the number that determines whether your camera can keep writing a 4K stream without dropping frames or stopping mid-clip.

RatingMinimum WriteWhat It HandlesTypical Price (256GB)
V3030 MB/s (240 Mbps)4K up to ~200 Mbps: GoPro, DJI Pocket 3, most mirrorless Long GOPAround $20–35
V6060 MB/s (480 Mbps)High-bitrate 4K, some All-I codecs, 4K/120 on some bodiesAround $60–90
V9090 MB/s (720 Mbps)600+ Mbps All-I, 8K, burst RAW on flagship bodiesAround $150–250+

Two things the marketing won't tell you. First, the giant "200MB/s" printed on the front is the read speed — it matters only when offloading to your computer, not for recording. Second, an "A2" app-performance rating means nothing for video. Ignore both; find the little "V" number in the specs.

Tip: On cameras with CFexpress slots (many full-frame mirrorless), the SD slot is usually the bottleneck for high-bitrate modes. Check your manual: some cameras silently disable their best codecs when only an SD card is present.

Capacity Planning: How Much Per Trip Day?

Using the same math, a 100 Mbps stream records about 750 MB per minute — call it 45 GB per hour of footage. Real-world daily totals from my own trips:

  • Light shooter (photos + short clips, ~20 min of video/day): 20–30 GB/day. A 128GB card covers a week.
  • Typical vlogger (~1 hour of 4K/day): 45–60 GB/day. A 256GB card covers 4–5 days.
  • Heavy shooter (4K/60, lots of slow motion, 2+ hours/day): 100–150 GB/day. Carry multiple 256GB cards.

My standard travel loadout: two 256GB V30 cards in rotation plus nightly offload to a phone or SSD (the full system is in backing up without a laptop). Two mid-size cards beat one huge one: cards fail, get lost, and get stolen — splitting your footage splits the risk. And make sure the cards you already own aren't slowing down your edit: slow cards also bottleneck transfers when you edit travel videos on your phone.

Fake Memory Cards: The Warning Nobody Heeds

Counterfeit cards are epidemic on big marketplaces and in tourist-area electronics shops. The scam: a cheap 16–32GB card is reprogrammed to report 256GB or 512GB. It works fine until the real capacity fills — then it silently overwrites your oldest footage. You won't know until you try to watch it.

Warning: Red flags of a fake card: a price far below other sellers, blurry or misaligned printing on the label, missing holographic packaging, and a marketplace seller with no brand authorization. If a 512GB card costs $25, it is not a 512GB card.

Protect yourself three ways. First, buy only from the brand's official store or authorized retailers — boring advice that would have saved my Marrakech footage. Second, test every new card before your trip with a free full-capacity tool (h2testw on Windows, F3 on Mac/Linux): it writes and verifies the entire card and takes a couple of hours, run it overnight. Third, watch for cards that run hot or randomly stop recording mid-clip — classic counterfeit symptoms.

Common Memory Card Mistakes

  • Buying V90 for a camera that maxes at 120 Mbps. That's $150 for nothing. Read your camera's bitrate spec first.
  • Formatting cards in the computer instead of the camera. Always format in-camera to avoid file-system quirks that cause recording errors.
  • Filling cards to 100%. Keep 10% free; completely full cards are more prone to corruption and failed final writes.
  • Reusing a card for years. Flash wears out. Cards on heavy rotation for 2–3+ years get retired to backup duty.
  • Buying at your destination. Airport and tourist-shop cards are overpriced at best, fake at worst. Stock up before you fly — and test them, as covered above.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is V30 fast enough for 4K video?

Yes for nearly all consumer cameras. V30 guarantees 30 MB/s (240 Mbps) sustained write, while cameras like the GoPro Hero 13 and DJI Osmo Pocket 3 record at around 120-130 Mbps. You only need V60 or V90 for high-bitrate All-I codecs above roughly 200 Mbps on mirrorless cameras.

How much memory card capacity do I need for a week of travel?

It depends on how much you film. Light shooters need about 20-30 GB per day, typical vloggers 45-60 GB per day at 4K, and heavy shooters 100-150 GB per day. Two 256GB cards with nightly offloading to a phone or SSD covers almost any trip.

How can I tell if a memory card is fake?

Fake cards report a large capacity but only contain a small amount of real storage. Red flags include prices far below other sellers, blurry label printing, and recording that stops randomly. Test every new card with a free full-capacity checker like h2testw or F3 before your trip.

Should I buy one big card or several smaller ones?

Several smaller ones. Two 256GB cards cost about the same as one 512GB card, but if a card fails, is lost, or is stolen, you only lose half your footage. Rotate cards daily and offload to a second device every night.
