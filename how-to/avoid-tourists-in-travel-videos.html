How to Avoid Tourists in Travel Videos (7 Pro Techniques)

By Dawid Millennium | Updated September 2, 2026

🏆 Quick Answer

Shoot at golden hour (sunrise/sunset) when crowds are 80% smaller. Use ND filters for long exposure that blurs moving tourists. Tight framing excludes background crowds. Visit off-season (November-February for Europe). Arrive at 6 AM—you'll have popular spots completely empty for 90 minutes before tour groups arrive.

The Problem: Iconic Locations Are Never Empty

Trevi Fountain, Times Square, Angkor Wat—these places define travel dreams but deliver crowd nightmares. Professional travel filmmakers use specific techniques to capture empty-looking footage even in peak season. Here's how.

7 Techniques to Eliminate Tourists from Your Footage

1. Golden Hour Magic (Sunrise & Sunset)

Most tourists sleep until 9 AM and quit by 5 PM. Shooting during golden hour (first/last hour of daylight) gives you:

  • 80% fewer people at major attractions
  • Warmer, cinematic light that flatters skin tones
  • Longer shadows that add depth to compositions
  • Empty streets in cities like Rome, Paris, Barcelona

Pro tip: Download PhotoPills app to calculate exact golden hour times for any location worldwide.

2. ND Filters + Long Exposure Technique

Neutral Density (ND) filters reduce light entering your lens, allowing slower shutter speeds even in bright conditions. Set shutter to 1-2 seconds, and moving tourists disappear into ethereal blur while static architecture stays sharp.

ND Strength Shutter Speed Best For Tourist Blur Effect
ND4 (2-stop) 1/30 sec Slight motion blur Minimal (people still visible)
ND16 (4-stop) 1/8 sec Walking movement Moderate (ghosting effect)
ND64 (6-stop) 1/2 sec Crowd elimination Strong (people blur significantly)
ND1000 (10-stop) 2+ sec Complete erasure Total (moving subjects vanish)

3. Tight Framing & Telephoto Compression

Instead of wide shots showing entire crowded plazas, zoom in on architectural details:

  • Use 50mm+ focal length to isolate subjects
  • Shoot upward at buildings against sky (no tourists above 10ft)
  • Focus on details: door knockers, statues, textures
  • Frame through archways that block background crowds

4. Off-Season Travel Strategy

Timing matters more than technique. Visit popular destinations during shoulder/low seasons:

Destination Peak Season Off-Season Crowd Reduction
Western Europe June-August November-February 70-80% fewer tourists
Japan (Tokyo/Kyoto) March-April, October January, July-August 60% fewer tourists
Bali, Indonesia July-August, December January-March, October 50% fewer tourists
New York City September-December January-February 40% fewer tourists

5. The 6 AM Rule

Arrive at iconic locations by 6 AM (earlier in summer). You'll get:

  • 90 minutes of solitude before first tour groups (typically 7:30-8 AM)
  • Soft morning light perfect for video
  • Local atmosphere—residents going about daily routines
  • Parking availability and no traffic delays

Real example: Trevi Fountain at 6:15 AM = completely empty. At 10 AM = 500+ people shoulder-to-shoulder.

6. Drone Alternatives (When Drones Are Banned)

Many cities ban drones (Rome, Paris, most national parks). Get elevated perspectives legally:

  • Rooftop bars/restaurants: Buy a drink, shoot from balcony
  • Observation decks: Empire State Building, Eiffel Tower second floor
  • Hilltop viewpoints: Sacré-Cœur (Paris), Gellért Hill (Budapest)
  • Bridges: Ponte Vecchio (Florence), Charles Bridge (Prague) at dawn

7. Patience & Multiple Takes

Sometimes you just wait. Set up your shot, record continuously for 5-10 minutes, then edit out moments when tourists walk through frame. This works best for:

  • Static tripod shots (buildings, monuments)
  • Talking-head segments in public squares
  • Time-lapse sequences (tourists become invisible blur)

Equipment Checklist for Crowd-Free Footage

  • Variable ND filter: PolarPro or Freewell ($80-150)
  • Telephoto lens: 50mm f/1.8 or 70-200mm zoom
  • Compact tripod: Joby GorillaPod for low-angle shots
  • PhotoPills app: Golden hour calculator ($9.99)
  • Alarm clock: For those 6 AM shoots!

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I remove tourists in post-production?

Yes, with limitations. Adobe After Effects Content-Aware Fill can remove small groups. For large crowds, shoot a "clean plate" (empty scene) separately and composite. Time-consuming but effective for hero shots.

What shutter speed eliminates walking tourists?

Minimum 1 second for noticeable blur, 2+ seconds for complete disappearance. Use ND1000 filter and tripod. Works only for moving subjects—stationary people remain visible.

Is it ethical to hide tourists from travel videos?

It's standard industry practice. Viewers want aspirational content, not documentary reality. Always disclose if footage is time-lapse or heavily edited when asked directly.

Do these techniques work for photos too?

Absolutely! Long exposure photography uses identical ND filter principles. Many photographers stack multiple exposures and blend them in Photoshop to remove tourists entirely.

What's the single best tip for avoiding crowds?

Wake up early. Every professional travel filmmaker will tell you: 6 AM beats any gear or technique. You'll have iconic locations to yourself while others sleep.
