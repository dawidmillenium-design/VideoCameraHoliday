DJI Osmo Pocket 3 vs GoPro Hero 13: Which Wins Your Holiday?

The DJI Osmo Pocket 3 is the better holiday camera for most travelers — its gimbal-stabilized 1-inch sensor footage simply looks a class above. But if your trip revolves around water, sand or adventure sports, the GoPro Hero 13 Black wins outright. These two cameras are not really rivals; they are specialists, and picking the wrong specialist is the most common mistake we see traveling videographers make.

We carried both through a Thailand beach week, a Lisbon city break and a hiking loop in the Dolomites to find out exactly where each one earns its place in your bag. This head-to-head is part of our action camera vs gimbal camera series — here we focus on the two best-sellers. Full field notes are in our DJI Osmo Pocket 3 review and GoPro Hero 13 review.

Key Takeaways

  • Image quality: Pocket 3 wins — the 1-inch sensor and real gimbal produce visibly more cinematic footage, day and night.
  • Durability: GoPro wins — waterproof to 10m without a case and genuinely drop-proof; the Pocket 3 needs care.
  • Vlogging: Pocket 3 wins — rotating screen, face tracking and wireless mic integration are built for talking to camera.
  • Action & POV: GoPro wins — mounts anywhere, survives anything, and HyperSmooth stabilization is superb in daylight.
  • Ideal kit: if budget allows, carry both — they cover each other's weaknesses perfectly.

Quick Verdict

Best for most travelers: DJI Osmo Pocket 3. Its footage looks like a crew shot it, and it fits in a jacket pocket. Best for beach holidays, families and adventure: GoPro Hero 13. It is the camera you never have to worry about. Best for creators who vlog daily: DJI Osmo Pocket 3 — ActiveTrack plus the wireless mic ecosystem is unbeatable at this size.

There is no tie here. If your holiday is 70% pool, sea and boat trips, buy the GoPro. For everything else — cities, food, sunsets, walking tours — buy the Pocket 3.

Beach & Water: GoPro Wins, No Contest

On a longtail boat off Railay Beach, spray coming over the bow, the Pocket 3 stayed in its case and the GoPro did all the work. That single afternoon sums up this category. The Hero 13 is waterproof to 10 meters with no housing, shrugs off sand, and can be rinsed under a tap at the end of the day. Snorkeling, kayaking, waterfall hikes, kids cannonballing into the pool — the GoPro films all of it without a second thought.

The Pocket 3 is not waterproof at all, and its gimbal motors actively dislike sand and salt spray. You can film near the beach with care, but you will always be managing the camera instead of enjoying the moment. For any trip where water is a headline activity, the decision is made for you.

Watch out: salt water is the Pocket 3's enemy. We have seen gimbals seized by a single beach day of sea spray. If you take it to the coast, keep it in a zip bag between shots — or check our waterproof cameras for beach holidays guide instead.

Night Markets & Low Light: Pocket 3 Wins Big

Chiang Mai's Sunday night market is where this comparison stops being close. The Pocket 3's 1-inch sensor produced clean, atmospheric footage of lantern stalls and sizzling woks that we happily used in finished edits. The GoPro's small sensor, fighting electronic stabilization that crops and struggles in the dark, turned the same scenes grainy and smeared. GoPro's night performance has improved over the generations, but physics is physics: the Pocket 3's sensor gathers roughly several times more light.

Evenings are when holidays look their best — golden hour rooftops, restaurant terraces, lit-up old towns. If those moments matter to you, the Pocket 3 is the only choice of the two. The gimbal also earns its keep after dark: slow, gliding walk-through shots that electronic stabilization renders jerky.

Vlogging & Audio: Pocket 3 Wins on Every Count

Filming yourself is the Pocket 3's home turf. Twist the rotating screen, and ActiveTrack locks onto your face and follows you around like a patient cameraman — we recorded an entire walking tour of Lisbon's Alfama district hands-free. Pair the DJI wireless mic and you get crisp voice over crowd noise, the camera perched on a bench across the square. It is the closest thing to a one-person film crew we have ever used, and a big reason locals we interview for our City Through the Lens series keep recommending it.

The GoPro vlogs acceptably — the front screen helps with framing and the built-in mics are decent in calm conditions — but there is no comparable face tracking, and wind noise on a cliff path still needs an aftermarket mic to tame. For sit-down travel diaries and talking-head content, it is simply outgunned.

Travel tip: if you vlog solo, the Pocket 3's face tracking matters more than any spec on the table. Frame yourself, hit record, walk and talk — no tripod gymnastics, no asking strangers to hold the camera.

Battery & Travel Days: GoPro Edges It

This is the GoPro's quiet victory. Its Enduro battery is swappable — carry two spares in a pocket and you can film a 14-hour sightseeing day without ever hunting for an outlet. It also charges from any USB-C bank while mounted, tolerates cold far better, and needs zero babying: throw it in a bag with keys and it comes out fine.

The Pocket 3's built-in battery is genuinely good — a full day of mixed 4K shooting usually leaves us 20-30% in reserve, and a 20-minute café top-up adds hours. But the battery is sealed in, so a heavy shooting day means planning around a power bank, and the gimbal demands its protective cover in transit. Neither is a dealbreaker; one just asks for more thought. For treks, ski trips and festivals, the GoPro's swap-and-forget approach wins.

Who Should Buy Which

Buy the DJI Osmo Pocket 3 if: you want the best-looking footage per gram, you film yourself, your trips are city-food-sunset shaped, or you shoot after dark. It is the camera that makes ordinary holidays look cinematic.

Buy the GoPro Hero 13 if: your holiday involves water, snow, bikes, boats or small children; if you want mountable POV angles; or if you are hard on gear. Also consider the Insta360 X5 if reframing 360° footage later appeals more than choosing angles on the spot.

Buy both if: you travel often and shoot seriously. Around $850-$1,000 combined buys a kit with no weak scenario — Pocket 3 for beauty, GoPro for chaos. That two-camera setup is exactly what lives in our own hand luggage.

FAQ

Is the DJI Pocket 3 better than the GoPro Hero 13 for travel?

For most travelers, yes — the Pocket 3's 1-inch sensor and mechanical gimbal produce visibly better footage in cities, restaurants and low light. The GoPro only wins when water, impact or mounting is involved. Choose by trip type, not by brand.

Can the DJI Pocket 3 survive a beach holiday?

It can, but it should not be your beach camera. It is not waterproof and its gimbal dislikes sand and salt spray. For swim, snorkel and boat days, the GoPro Hero 13 is the correct tool; keep the Pocket 3 for evenings and city days.

Which is better for vlogging, Pocket 3 or GoPro Hero 13?

The Pocket 3, clearly. Its rotating screen, face tracking and seamless wireless mic pairing are designed for filming yourself. The GoPro can vlog in a pinch, but without tracking or strong audio options it is the weaker vlogging tool.

Is the GoPro Hero 13 good in low light?

It is usable in dim conditions but it is the weakest scenario for any action camera — the small sensor produces noisy, soft footage after dark. If night markets and evening cityscapes matter to you, the Pocket 3 is dramatically better.

Should I buy Hero 12 or Action 4 instead to save money?

If you find a deal, yes — both are still excellent. The Hero 12 lacks the new Magic Lens features but keeps 5.3K video and HyperSmooth. The Action 4 has a larger sensor than either Hero 12 or 13, making it better in low light. For scuba, all three work equally well with proper housing; the Hero 13's main advantage is longer battery life and better cold-weather performance.
