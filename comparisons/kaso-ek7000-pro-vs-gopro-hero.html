Akaso EK7000 Pro vs GoPro Hero: Budget King vs Premium?

We took both cameras diving in cenotes and hiking in the Alps. Is the Akaso really 90% as good as the GoPro for half the price?

The Budget King

Akaso EK7000 Pro

$89

Perfect for beginners, backup cams, or rough situations where you don't want to risk expensive gear.

  • ✅ Incredible value
  • ✅ Comes with tons of mounts
  • ❌ Weak low-light performance
  • ❌ Audio is unusable in wind
OVERALL WINNER
The Premium Standard

GoPro Hero (11/12/13)

$350-$400

The best image quality, unbeatable stabilization, and rugged reliability for serious creators.

  • ✅ 5.3K resolution & 10-bit color
  • ✅ HyperSmooth is magic
  • ✅ Waterproof to 33ft bare
  • ❌ Expensive accessories

Key Takeaways

  • Image Quality: GoPro wins easily. The dynamic range and color science are generations ahead. Akaso footage looks "digital" and flat in comparison.
  • Stabilization: Akaso's "EIS" crops the image heavily and still jitters. GoPro's HyperSmooth looks like a gimbal.
  • Value: If you just need a dashcam or a helmet cam for occasional use, Akaso is fine. For vlogging or social media content, get the GoPro.
  • Audio: Neither is great without a mic, but GoPro is usable in calm weather. Akaso is windy noise only.

Video Quality Test

We shot side-by-side footage in bright sunlight (Alps) and low light (Cenote cave). The difference was stark.

Bright Light: Akaso footage is sharp but colors look oversaturated and fake. GoPro footage has natural skin tones and better highlight retention (sky doesn't blow out).

Low Light: Akaso falls apart. Noise grain is heavy, and details turn into mush. GoPro keeps detail surprisingly well thanks to larger sensor processing.

Pro Tip: If you buy the Akaso, shoot in "Flat" profile if available and color grade it. Do not use the standard "Vivid" mode unless you like neon greens.

Stabilization Battle

We mounted both cameras on a mountain bike handlebar. The GoPro footage looks like it was shot on a drone—smooth as butter. The Akaso footage has a noticeable "jello effect" where vertical bumps make the image wobble horizontally. Plus, the Akaso EIS crops your field of view by about 20%, making wide shots less wide.

Build & Usability

Akaso: Feels plasticky. Buttons are mushy. The screen is small and hard to see in sunlight. However, it comes with a box full of mounts, cases, and remotes, which is great value.

GoPro: Rugged tank. Touchscreen is responsive even with wet fingers. The "QuikCapture" feature (press button to start recording instantly) is a lifesaver for spontaneous moments.

Is GoPro Worth Double the Price?

Yes, for creators. If you plan to edit videos for YouTube or Instagram, the GoPro saves you hours of fixing bad audio, stabilizing shaky clips, and color correcting weird skin tones. Time is money.

No, for casual users. If you just want to record your kid's soccer game or have a dashcam, the Akaso does the job perfectly fine at 1/4th the price.

FAQ

Can Akaso use GoPro mounts?

Yes! Akaso uses the standard GoPro 3-prong mount system. All your existing GoPro accessories will fit the Akaso perfectly.

Does Akaso have slow motion?

Yes, up to 1080p at 60fps. It's okay for basic slow-mo, but don't expect the buttery smooth 240fps of the GoPro.

Which SD card for Akaso?

You don't need expensive V90 cards. A standard V30 SanDisk Extreme is perfect and saves you money. See our SD Card Guide →
