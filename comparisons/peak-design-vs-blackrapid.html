Peak Design's Slide Lite is the better all-round travel strap — its quick-release anchor system and tidy design make it the smarter choice for most photographers. But BlackRapid's sling straps still win for speed: if you shoot wildlife, street or action, nothing gets a camera to your eye faster. These are two different philosophies of carrying a camera, and the right one depends on how you shoot.

We've used both systems for years — from dusty trails in the Dolomites to crowded markets in Marrakech — and this comparison is about which one earns a permanent place in your travel kit. For a broader look at hiking-specific straps, see our best camera strap for hiking guide.

Key Takeaways

  • Speed: BlackRapid wins — the sling design swings the camera up instantly.
  • Versatility: Peak Design wins — switch between neck, shoulder and sling carry in seconds.
  • Security: Peak Design wins — Anchor Links are rated to 200 lbs and quickly detach.
  • Comfort: Tie — both offer padded options that handle a full-frame camera + lens.
  • Price: BlackRapid often cheaper, but Peak Design's modular ecosystem adds long-term value.

Quick Verdict

Best for most travelers: Peak Design Slide Lite. The anchor system alone is worth the price — clip your camera to any strap in seconds, and the whole thing packs flat. Best for speed and action: BlackRapid Sport Breathe. If you shoot moving subjects and need the camera ready in half a second, the sling is unbeatable. Best for heavy setups: BlackRapid Curve or Double. The cross-body design distributes weight better for long days with a big zoom.

There's no wrong answer — only the wrong user. Match the strap to how you actually shoot, not how you imagine you'll shoot.

Anchors vs Sling Design

The single biggest difference is how each system connects to your camera and how you deploy it.

Peak Design: The Anchor System

Peak Design's genius is the small, coin-sized Anchor Link. You attach one to each camera strap lug (or a base plate), and the strap clicks on and off in a second. This means you can:

  • Switch between multiple cameras instantly
  • Remove the strap entirely when using a tripod
  • Attach the strap to a backpack strap or belt for alternate carry
  • Pack the strap flat without tangling

Anchors are rated to 200 lbs, and we've never had one fail in years of hard use. The system is so good that many third-party accessories now support it.

BlackRapid: The Sling Speed

BlackRapid straps screw into the tripod socket (or use a plate) and hang the camera upside-down at your hip. To shoot, you grab the camera and slide it up the strap to your eye — the whole motion takes under a second. It's the fastest way to deploy a camera, and it keeps the camera stable while walking.

The downside: the camera hangs lens-down, which can be worrying in crowds, and the screw mount can loosen over time (though BlackRapid includes a locking carabiner and rubber washer to help).

Travel tip: If you choose BlackRapid, add a small safety tether from the camera's strap lug to the strap itself — cheap insurance against the screw working loose.

Comfort & Padding

Both companies know that comfort matters on long days, and both offer well-padded options.

  • Peak Design Slide Lite: The shoulder pad is removable and repositionable. It's adequate for a mirrorless camera with a standard zoom, but for a heavy full-frame kit, the wider Slide (not Lite) is more comfortable.
  • BlackRapid Sport Breathe: The pad runs the full length of the strap and uses breathable mesh — excellent for hot climates. It spreads weight across the shoulder and back, which is great for long hikes with a heavy lens.

For pure comfort with a heavy setup, BlackRapid has an edge. For versatility and lighter kits, Peak Design is more than enough.

Travel Practicality

When you're living out of a carry-on, every gram and every second matters.

  • Packing: Peak Design straps fold flat and weigh almost nothing; BlackRapid straps are bulkier due to the full-length pad and metal hardware.
  • Airport security: Peak Design anchors allow you to quickly remove the strap when putting the camera in a bin; BlackRapid's screw mount takes a few seconds longer.
  • Switching between cameras: Peak Design wins hands-down — just add anchors to every camera. BlackRapid requires a separate strap or adapter for each.
  • Using a tripod: Peak Design anchors pop off in a second; BlackRapid's screw mount blocks the tripod socket, requiring an extra plate or adapter.

For most travel scenarios, Peak Design's modular approach is simply more convenient.

Durability & Weather

Both are built to last, but different failure points exist:

  • Peak Design: Anchors are the weak point, but they're replaceable and rated high. The strap itself is seatbelt-grade nylon — virtually indestructible. Weather-resistant.
  • BlackRapid: The metal carabiner and screw are strong, but the screw can strip if cross-threaded. The strap material is tough, but the mesh pad can absorb sweat and dust over time.

For harsh conditions (sand, salt, rain), Peak Design's simpler design has fewer places for grit to cause trouble. But with basic care, both survive years of travel.

Who Should Buy Which

Buy Peak Design Slide Lite if: you travel with one or two cameras, you value quick switching between neck and sling carry, you use a tripod often, or you just want the most elegant, packable strap available. It's the default choice for modern travel photographers.

Buy BlackRapid Sport Breathe if: you shoot wildlife, sports or street where speed matters, you carry a heavy camera + telephoto lens and want the weight off your neck, or you prefer a dedicated sling and don't need to switch configurations often.

Buy both? Some photographers do — Peak Design for everyday, BlackRapid for action days. But for most, one will cover 90% of needs.

FAQ

Are Peak Design anchors really secure enough for a heavy camera?

Yes. Each Anchor Link is rated to 200 lbs (90 kg) — far beyond any camera setup. We've used them for years with full-frame bodies and 70-200mm lenses without a single failure. Just check the anchor cord for wear every few months and replace if frayed.

Will a BlackRapid strap damage my camera's tripod socket?

Not if used correctly. BlackRapid includes a rubber washer to protect the base and recommends not over-tightening. However, the screw can slowly loosen with movement, so check it periodically or add a drop of thread-lock. A safety tether to the strap lug is also wise.

Can I use Peak Design anchors with a BlackRapid strap?

No — the two systems are not directly compatible. Peak Design anchors attach to small loops; BlackRapid uses a screw into the tripod socket or a specific plate. You could use an adapter, but it's simpler to choose one ecosystem.

Which strap is better for hiking?

For hiking, many prefer BlackRapid's sling because it keeps the camera at your hip, reducing neck strain and bouncing. But Peak Design's Slide Lite can also be worn as a sling and offers quicker tripod access. Our hiking strap guide goes deeper.

Is the Peak Design Slide worth the extra money over BlackRapid?

For most travel photographers, yes. The anchor system, packability and versatility justify the price. But if you're on a tight budget and shoot primarily action, BlackRapid gives excellent performance for less.
