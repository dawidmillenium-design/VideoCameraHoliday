DJI Osmo Pocket 4P Settings for Night Markets: A Field Guide to Neon, Steam, and Motion

There is no location on Earth that tests a camera’s low-light capability, autofocus, and color science quite like a night market. Whether you are weaving through the crowds of Smorgasburg in Los Angeles, navigating the lantern-lit aisles of Honolulu’s Night Market, or capturing the sizzling woks at a 626 Night Market in Arcadia, California, the environment is a sensory overload. The challenge? Mixed lighting (sodium vapor, neon, LED), high contrast, and constant motion.

I have spent the last three years shooting these events with the DJI Osmo Pocket series, and I can tell you this: the DJI Osmo Pocket 4P (the fourth-generation "Pro" model) is arguably the best compact gimbal camera for this specific task, provided you configure it correctly. Out of the box, the camera defaults to auto settings that will leave your footage looking like a muddy, overexposed mess. But with the right manual overrides, you can capture buttery-smooth 4K footage that rivals full-frame mirrorless cameras.

This guide is not about theoretical specs. It is a field-tested, step-by-step configuration manual based on my experiences shooting in the humid, chaotic, and beautifully lit streets of American night markets. We will cover everything from frame rate selection to white balance locking, ensuring you walk away with cinematic, usable footage.

Prerequisites: What You Need Before You Go

Before we dive into the menu settings, let’s talk about gear. The Osmo Pocket 4P is a capable camera, but it needs a few accessories to survive a night market environment.

  1. The DJI Osmo Pocket 4P (Obviously): Ensure your firmware is updated to the latest version. DJI frequently releases low-light optimization patches.
  2. DJI Osmo Pocket 4P ND Filter Set (Specifically the ND16 and ND32): You might think you don't need ND filters at night, but you do. We will use them to control shutter speed for motion blur. These are available at B&H Photo or Adorama in the US.
  3. DJI Osmo Pocket 4P Wireless Microphone (or a wired 3.5mm TRS mic): Night markets are loud. If you want to capture vendor interviews or ambient "sizzle," you need a directional mic. The DJI Mic 2 is my go-to, but a Rode VideoMicro works well if you have the USB-C adapter.
  4. Portable Power Bank (Anker 10,000mAh): The 4P battery drains fast when using the active tracking and high brightness screen. A USB-C power bank is essential for a 3-hour shoot.
  5. SanDisk Extreme Pro 256GB (V30) MicroSD: You will be shooting in high bitrate modes. Do not use a slow card; you will get buffer errors.

Pro Tip: Wear dark clothing. The gimbal's face tracking might lock onto a bright white shirt in the crowd. Dark clothing helps the camera prioritize your subject if you are vlogging.

The Step-by-Step Guide to Perfect Night Market Footage

Let’s get into the meat of the matter. We are going to configure the DJI Osmo Pocket 4P from the ground up. I recommend doing this before you enter the market, as fiddling with menus while holding a skewer of grilled meat is a recipe for disaster.

Step 1: Set Exposure Mode to Manual (M)

This is the single most important step. Night markets are filled with bright bulbs and deep shadows. If you use Auto Exposure (AE), the camera will constantly hunt, brightening and darkening your video as you pan across the stalls. This is called "exposure pumping," and it ruins footage.

  1. Swipe down on the touchscreen to access the Quick Settings menu.
  2. Tap the "A" (Auto) icon on the right side of the screen.
  3. Switch it to "M" (Manual).

You now have full control over ISO, Shutter, and Aperture (the 4P has a fixed f/1.8 aperture, but you control the other two).

Step 2: Lock Your Shutter Speed to 1/50 (or 1/60)

Here is where the 4P shines. To get that cinematic "film look" where motion blur is natural (and not jittery), you must follow the 180-degree shutter rule. If you are shooting at 24 frames per second (fps), your shutter speed should be double that—1/50 of a second. If you are shooting at 30fps, use 1/60.

  1. In Manual mode, tap on the "Shutter" value (it looks like a fraction).
  2. Rotate the dial on the side of the gimbal or use the slider to set it to 1/50.

Why this matters at a night market: When you pan across neon signs, a fast shutter speed (like 1/200) creates a strobe effect on the lights. A 1/50 shutter allows the lights to blur naturally, mimicking how your eye sees them. However, at night, a 1/50 shutter lets in a LOT of light. This is why we will use ND filters later.

Step 3: Set ISO to Auto (with a Ceiling of 1600)

The DJI Osmo Pocket 4P has a dual native ISO. The second native ISO is around 1600. This means that at ISO 1600, the sensor is at its cleanest for low-light, with minimal noise penalty compared to ISO 400.

  1. Tap the ISO setting in the Manual menu.
  2. Select "Auto" but then tap the "Max ISO" option.
  3. Set the Maximum ISO to 1600. Do not go to 3200 unless you are desperate; the noise reduction algorithms will start to soften details, making the food look mushy.

Field Note: At a night market in San Francisco, I pushed the ISO to 3200 to capture a dark alley shot. The result was usable but lacked the crisp detail of the ISO 1600 footage. Stick to 1600.

Step 4: Apply the ND Filter (The Secret Weapon)

You might be thinking, "If I lock my shutter to 1/50 and my ISO to 1600, my image will be blown out." Correct. This is why you need a Neutral Density (ND) filter. The 4P's f/1.8 aperture is very fast, letting in too much light for night scenes with lots of artificial lighting.

  1. Turn off the camera.
  2. Attach the ND16 filter to the lens using the magnetic mount (or the twist-lock if you have the pro version).
  3. Turn the camera back on. Check your exposure meter (the little light meter at the bottom of the screen).
  4. If the meter is still overexposed (showing +1.0 or higher), swap to the ND32.

When to use which:

  • ND16: For areas with moderate lighting (street lamps, open plazas).
  • ND32: For directly under bright neon signs or food stalls with halogen lights.
  • No Filter: For dark alleys or far from light sources.

Your goal is to get the exposure meter to sit at 0.0. If it dips to -0.3, that is fine. It is better to underexpose slightly than to clip the highlights on the neon signs.

Step 5: White Balance – Stop Using Auto

Auto White Balance (AWB) at a night market is a disaster. The camera will shift from warm orange (under sodium lights) to cool blue (under LED signs) mid-shot. This makes color grading impossible later.

  1. Go to the White Balance (WB) menu.
  2. Select Custom (the icon with "WB" and a slider).
  3. Set the Kelvin value to 4000K to start.

The Logic: Night markets are usually lit by warm tungsten or amber lights. Setting a custom WB to 4000K will render those warm lights as a rich, golden orange rather than a sickly yellow. If you are near a lot of cool white LED lights (like the ones used in modern food trucks), you might want to bump it to 4800K to keep the whites looking white.

Pro Tip: Use the "Spot WB" function. Point the camera at a white paper plate or a white vendor apron, tap the screen to lock WB, and then switch to Manual. This gives you a perfect neutral base.

Step 6: Select the Right Video Resolution and Frame Rate

The 4P can shoot 4K at up to 120fps. But for night markets, you need to balance resolution with sensor readout speed.

  1. Swipe up on the screen to access Video Settings.
  2. Select 4K (3840x2160).
  3. Select 24fps (for a cinematic look) or 30fps (if you plan to post to social media without converting).

Important: Do NOT use the "High Frame Rate" (HFR) modes at night unless you are shooting a specific slow-motion shot of steam rising. HFR modes crop the sensor and reduce light gathering by about 30%.

Step 7: Set the Color Profile to D-Log M

If you want to color grade your footage later, you need to shoot in a flat profile. The 4P offers "Normal" and "D-Log M."

  1. Go to Color Profile.
  2. Select D-Log M.

D-Log M retains more highlight and shadow detail than Normal. At a night market, this is crucial for pulling details out of the shadows (the dark ground) and preventing the neon signs from clipping. If you don't plan to edit, use "Normal" with a slight contrast bump, but D-Log M is the professional choice.

Note: D-Log M will look flat and gray on your screen. Do not panic. This is normal. You are preserving data.

Step 8: Configure Autofocus (AF) Settings

Night markets have a lot of moving subjects. The 4P uses phase-detect autofocus, but it can struggle with low contrast.

  1. Tap on the AF/MF toggle to ensure it is set to AF (Auto Focus).
  2. Go to AF Assist. Enable it.
  3. Set the AF Speed to Slow. This prevents the focus from "wobbling" or hunting when a person walks between you and your subject.

If you are shooting a static shot (like a vendor flipping pancakes), tap on the screen to lock focus. The camera will hold that focus point even if people walk in front of the lens.

Step 9: Enable the "Low Light" Enhancement (Smart Mode)

The 4P has a specific feature for this. It is not a gimmick; it actually works.

  1. Swipe down to access the top menu.
  2. Look for the "Low Light" icon (a moon symbol).
  3. Enable it.

This mode merges multiple frames to reduce noise. However, it only works in specific resolutions (usually 1080p or 2.7K) and it reduces the field of view slightly. Use this only for static shots (like a wide shot of the market) and not for moving shots, as it can cause ghosting.

Step 10: Set Up ActiveTrack for Vlogging

If you are walking through the market and talking to the camera, you want the gimbal to follow you.

  1. Enable ActiveTrack by double-tapping your face on the screen.
  2. Set the Gimbal Speed to Medium. Too fast looks jarring; too slow loses you in the crowd.

Remember, ActiveTrack relies on contrast. If you are wearing a dark jacket and walking past a dark stall, the track might lose you. Keep your face visible and well-lit by a nearby stall if possible.

Step 11: Audio Settings for the Ambiance

Visuals are only half the story. The sizzle of the grill and the chatter of the crowd sell the experience.

  1. Go to Microphone Settings.
  2. Select Stereo for ambient sound.
  3. If using the DJI Mic 2, set the audio to "Mic" and turn the gain to -3dB to avoid clipping when a vendor shouts.

Step 12: The "Steam Shot" Technique

This is a personal favorite. To capture steam rising from a hot food stall, you need a fast shutter speed (1/100) to freeze the vapor, but that breaks the 180-degree rule. Here is the compromise:

  1. Switch to Pro Mode.
  2. Set Shutter to 1/100.
  3. Set ISO to Auto (Max 1600).
  4. Shoot for 3 seconds.

This will look slightly more "video-like" but it captures the texture of the steam beautifully. I use this for close-up B-roll of dumpling baskets and BBQ grills.

Common Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Even with the right settings, things go wrong. Here are the three most common issues I see shooters face with the Osmo Pocket 4P at night markets in the US, and how to solve them on the fly.

Mistake 1: The "Jelly Roll" Effect

Symptom: Vertical lines (like tent poles) lean to the side when you pan the camera.

Cause: This is rolling shutter distortion. It happens when you pan too fast with a slow shutter speed.

Fix: Slow down your panning. The gimbal has a smooth motion, but if you flick your wrist, you will get distortion. Use the joystick to pan at a slow, steady rate. If you must pan fast, increase your shutter to 1/100 (accepting the loss of motion blur) to reduce the readout delay.

Mistake 2: Flickering Lights

Symptom: The neon signs or LED screens flicker in your video, but not in real life.

Cause: The frequency of the electrical grid (60Hz in the US) conflicts with your shutter speed.

Fix: In the US, your shutter speed must be a multiple of 60. Use 1/60 (for 30fps) or 1/120 (for 60fps). Do not use 1/50 in the United States, as this is a European standard (50Hz) and will cause a rolling bar across the screen.

Mistake 3: The Screen is Too Bright, Footage is Too Dark

Symptom: The rear screen looks perfect, but when you review the footage on your computer, it's dark.

Cause: The screen brightness is set too high, making you think you have more exposure than you do.

Fix: Set your screen brightness to 50% while shooting. This is a neutral baseline. Also, enable "Zebra Stripes" (in the Exposure menu) to show you exactly where the highlights are clipping. Set the zebra level to 100 IRE for highlights.
