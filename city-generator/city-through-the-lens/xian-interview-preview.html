Xi'an is a city of layers — ancient hutongs tucked beneath futuristic skyscrapers, imperial palaces standing beside brutalist government buildings. It is one of the most politically and historically significant cities on Earth, and filming here requires navigating a complex web of permits, drone restrictions, and cultural sensitivities that catch most tourists completely off guard.

In the upcoming Xi'an episode of City Through the Lens, we are searching for a local videographer to answer 15 practical questions about filming in China's capital. While we finalize that interview, this guide gives you the essential field notes — from the three locations that define the city's visual identity, to the dust storms that can ruin unprotected gear, and the drone regulations that are stricter than anywhere else in the world.

⚡ Key Takeaways — Xi'an

  • Best filming seasons: Spring (April–May) for cherry blossoms; Autumn (September–October) for crisp, clear skies.
  • Top 3 locations: Forbidden City, Temple of Heaven, and the Summer Palace.
  • Critical permit rule: Drones are heavily restricted — you need advance permits for most areas. Tripods are banned inside the Forbidden City.
  • Airport tip: Xi'an Capital Airport (PEK) is 30 km from the city. The Airport Express train takes about 40 minutes.

3 Must-Film Locations in Xi'an

Local videographers often use gimbals here.

Xi'an's ancient and modern landmarks offer some of the most recognizable imagery in the world. But capturing them without falling into cliché requires local knowledge. Here are the three essential locations — and the insider tips that make the difference.

1. The Forbidden City

The Forbidden City is the world's largest imperial palace complex — a 72-hectare sea of golden roofs and red walls that served as the home of Chinese emperors for nearly 500 years. It is Xi'an's most filmed attraction, but getting a clean shot without crowds is a logistical challenge.

Filming tip: The Forbidden City opens at 8:30 AM. Be at the Meridian Gate (south entrance) at least 45 minutes early to be first in line. Head straight to the Palace of Heavenly Purity for the classic throne-room shot before the crowds flood in. Drones are strictly banned — you will be stopped by security immediately. Tripods are also prohibited inside the palace complex. A compact gimbal (like the DJI RS 3 Mini) or a fast prime lens for handheld shooting is your best bet.

2. The Temple of Heaven

The Temple of Heaven is a masterpiece of Ming Dynasty architecture — a circular temple complex where emperors once performed annual ceremonies of atonement. The Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests is the iconic shot, with its triple-tiered blue roof and marble base.

Filming tip: Visit the Temple of Heaven at sunrise or sunset when the golden light strikes the blue tiles. The park surrounding the temple is a gathering place for local retirees practicing tai chi — a wonderful B-roll opportunity. Ask permission before filming individuals — most locals are happy to be filmed but appreciate the courtesy of being asked.

3. The Summer Palace

The Summer Palace is a sprawling imperial garden on the shores of Kunming Lake — a retreat from the summer heat for Qing Dynasty emperors. The Long Corridor, the Marble Boat, and the Seventeen-Arch Bridge offer endless compositional opportunities.

Filming tip: The Summer Palace is vast — rent a boat on Kunming Lake for unique low-angle shots of the pavilions and bridges from the water. The best light is during the "golden hour" before sunset when the western hills are silhouetted behind the lake. A circular polarizer (CPL) helps cut through the glare on the water and deepen the blue of the sky.

Xi'an's Weather Reality for Videographers

Xi'an has a semi-humid continental climate, meaning hot summers, cold winters, and — critically — frequent dust storms in spring. The city's air quality is notoriously variable, and understanding the weather patterns is essential for protecting your gear and timing your shoots.

  • Spring (April–May): Mild temperatures (10–25°C), but dust storms and sandstorms are common, blowing in from the Gobi Desert. These can ruin lenses and clog camera bodies. A weather-sealed camera and a dust blower are absolutely essential. The cherry blossoms at the Summer Palace are spectacular but short-lived.
  • Summer (June–August): Hot, humid, and occasionally rainy. Temperatures regularly hit 35°C, and the smog can be thick. The upside: dramatic storm clouds and vibrant green foliage. Bring lens wipes for the humidity.
  • Autumn (September–October): Arguably the best time to film. Clear, blue skies, crisp temperatures (10–25°C), and the city's famous autumn foliage. The air is cleaner, and the light is beautiful for most of the day.
  • Winter (December–February): Cold and dry (temperatures often below freezing, occasionally dipping to -15°C). The skies are often brilliant blue due to the cold air, but battery life plummets. Keep spare batteries in your inner pocket to keep them warm.
Pro tip for dust and smog: If you're filming during a dust storm, do not change lenses outdoors. The fine particles will get on your sensor. Use a zoom lens with internal focus to minimize dust ingress. In post-production, use a Dehaze tool (available in Lightroom, DaVinci Resolve, and Premiere Pro) to cut through atmospheric haze and restore contrast.

Airport Transfer & Gear Logistics

Xi'an has two major airports, but for most international travelers, Xi'an Capital Airport is the primary gateway.

  • Xi'an Capital International Airport (PEK): Located about 30 km northeast of the city center. The Airport Express train connects to the subway system at Sanyuanqiao and Dongzhimen stations, taking about 40 minutes (cost: ¥25). It's efficient, clean, and you can easily navigate with camera bags. Taxis and ride-hailing apps (like Didi) are also available but can get stuck in traffic — especially during peak hours.
  • Xi'an Daxing International Airport (PKX): A newer, futuristic airport located 50 km south of the city. If you fly into Daxing, the Daxing Airport Express takes about 20 minutes to Caoqiao Station, where you can transfer to the subway. It's less crowded than PEK but further from the city center.

Pro tip for security: Chinese airport security is thorough. Keep your gimbal batteries, camera bodies, and lenses in your carry-on. Lithium batteries are strictly prohibited in checked luggage. You may be asked to power on your camera to prove it's functional — so keep your batteries charged.

Permits, Etiquette & The Reality of Filming in Xi'an

Xi'an has some of the strictest filming regulations in the world — but they are predictable if you do your homework. Here's what you need to know:

  • Drones: Xi'an is a drone-restricted zone. The entire city center is a no-fly zone. You must obtain permits from the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC) and the local Public Security Bureau — a process that can take weeks and requires you to have a registered drone and approved flight routes. Do not fly your drone in central Xi'an — you will likely have it confiscated and face a fine.
  • Tripods and gimbals: Tripods are prohibited inside the Forbidden City and the Temple of Heaven. Security guards will ask you to pack them up. Gimbals (like the DJI RS series) are generally tolerated if used discreetly. A monopod is a good compromise — it provides stability but is less conspicuous than a full tripod.
  • Filming people: China has strict privacy and national security laws. Avoid filming government buildings, military installations, and sensitive infrastructure. When filming street scenes, be mindful that the Great Firewall and local regulations mean your footage may be subject to review. Always ask permission before filming individuals.
  • Hutong etiquette: The traditional alleyways (hutongs) of Xi'an are residential neighborhoods. Be respectful — don't block doorways, keep your voice down, and ask before filming someone's home or courtyard.
