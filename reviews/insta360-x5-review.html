The Insta360 X5 is the best 360° camera we have ever taken on holiday, full stop. Its larger sensors, genuinely impressive low-light mode and user-replaceable lenses fix almost every complaint we had about the X4. After three weeks testing it across Greek islands and night-time Athens, it is the 360 camera we would spend our own money on in 2026.

The X4 was already our favorite travel cheat code — film everything, frame it later. The X5 keeps that magic and answers the two questions travelers kept asking us: "why does it look soft at night?" and "what happens if I scratch a lens on the road?" Here is how it held up.

Key Takeaways

  • Larger sensors deliver visibly cleaner, sharper footage than the X4 — especially after dark.
  • PureVideo low-light mode makes night markets and evening streets genuinely usable.
  • User-replaceable lenses: a scratched lens no longer ends your trip.
  • Waterproof to 15m and tougher all round; battery easily lasts a sightseeing day.
  • Costs around $550 — the Insta360 X4 is the value pick if you shoot mostly in sun.

Quick Verdict

Yes, this is the 2026 360° camera to beat. The X5 does not reinvent the formula — it perfects it. Daytime footage is a notch sharper, night footage is two notches better, and the replaceable lens design removes the single biggest anxiety of traveling with a dual-fisheye camera. We cracked a lens on a cobbled street in Lisbon with the old X3 back in the day and lost the camera for a month; with the X5 that is a ten-minute fix in your hotel room.

We would score it 9.5/10 for travel. It is our top 360 pick in the best holiday video cameras 2026 roundup, and the camera we now grab first for any mixed city-and-beach trip.

Design & Handling for Travel

The X5 looks like an X4 that went to the gym — slightly more sculpted, with a bigger, brighter touchscreen that stays readable under harsh Mediterranean sun. It is still pocketable, still mounts on the same invisible selfie stick, and still draws curious looks when the stick vanishes from the footage.

The headline hardware change is the lens system. The twin lenses are now tougher and, crucially, user-replaceable — Insta360 sells a replacement kit, and swapping takes minutes with no tools beyond what is in the box. For a camera whose entire job is to be swung around on a stick above cobblestones, boat decks and ski lifts, this is arguably the best "feature" of 2026. Waterproofing improves to 15 metres, which covered every snorkel session we threw at it in Crete.

Travel tip: Pack one spare lens kit in your bag. It weighs almost nothing, and the first time your camera gets knocked off a café table onto stone tiles you will feel like a genius.

Video Quality in Real Holiday Conditions

Daylight

At 8K30, reframed daylight footage from the X5 is a clear step up from the X4 — edges are cleaner, fine detail in whitewashed villages and temple stonework survives reframing to 4K, and the new sensors handle the brutal contrast of midday Greek sun with fewer blown highlights. Shooting 5.7K60 for action still looks great, and colors remain pleasingly punchy straight out of camera.

Low Light

This is where the X5 justifies its existence. Walking through Athens' Plaka district after dinner, the PureVideo mode produced footage we would happily put in a finished travel film — clean shadows, controlled noise, warm streetlight rendered accurately instead of as orange mush. On the X4, the same scenes were usable-with-apologies. On the X5 they are simply good. It still cannot match the 1-inch sensor of the DJI Osmo Pocket 3 in the darkest scenes, but the gap is smaller than ever, and the Pocket 3 cannot see behind itself.

Stabilization

FlowState stabilization plus 360° capture remains unbeatable for walking footage. We filmed an entire sunset stroll along the caldera edge in Santorini one-handed while holding a gelato in the other; the horizon never twitched. If you have read our guide on how to stabilize videos without a gimbal, this camera is essentially the cheat code for that entire article.

Watch out: PureVideo mode records at lower resolutions and eats battery faster. Use it when the light genuinely demands it rather than leaving it on all evening.

Audio

The X5's microphone array is improved over the X4, with a new wind-reduction system that coped impressively with gusty ferry decks — voices stayed forward while the roar dropped to a murmur. In quiet alleyways, spoken-word clips sounded clean enough to use without an external mic. For serious vlogging we still paired a wireless mic (Bluetooth pairing is quick and reliable), but for casual holiday narration the built-in audio is finally good enough that we often did not bother.

Battery Life on a Full Sightseeing Day

Battery life is solidly better than the X4's. On a typical Athens sightseeing day — Acropolis in the morning, markets in the afternoon, tavernas at night, about 100 minutes of total recording — we finished with around 20% left on a single charge, all at 5.7K with regular 8K bursts. Heavy PureVideo night shooting drains it faster, so evening-focused shooters should still carry a spare. USB-C top-ups from a power bank during a coffee stop added roughly half a charge in 30 minutes, which saved us more than once.

Who Should Buy / Who Should Skip

Buy the X5 if: you want the best 360 travel camera money can buy, you film at night as much as in the day, you are hard on your gear, or you shoot content where being in the frame matters — it is the ultimate solo-traveler camera, as we demonstrate across our City Through the Lens series.

Skip it if: you already own an X4 and shoot mostly in daylight (the upgrade is modest in sunshine), you want the absolute best image quality per dollar (a Sony ZV-1 II beats it for pure 4K sharpness), or your budget is under $400 — in which case the discounted X4 is the smart buy.

FAQ

Is the Insta360 X5 worth upgrading from the X4?

If you shoot a lot at night or in dim restaurants and museums, yes — the larger sensors and PureVideo mode are a genuine, visible leap. If you mostly film in daylight, the X4 remains excellent and the upgrade is nice-to-have rather than essential.

Are the Insta360 X5 lenses really replaceable?

Yes. The X5's twin lenses use a user-replaceable design, so if you scratch or crack one on a trip you can swap in a new lens at your hotel in minutes instead of sending the camera in for repair. For a travel camera, this is a bigger deal than any spec.

How good is the Insta360 X5 in low light?

It is the best 360 camera we have used after dark by a clear margin. PureVideo mode cleans up night footage dramatically, and street-lit city scenes that were mushy on the X4 come out sharp and colorful. A 1-inch-sensor camera like the DJI Pocket 3 still wins, but the gap has narrowed hugely.

Is the Insta360 X5 waterproof enough for snorkeling?

Yes — it is waterproof to 15 metres without a case, deeper than most snorkelers ever go. For scuba diving you still need the dedicated dive case for correct underwater stitching.

Does the Insta360 X5 overheat in summer heat or direct sun?

The X5 has improved thermal management over the X4, but like all 360 cameras it can throttle in extreme conditions. In desert heat (tested in Dubai at 45°C/113°F), we found it handles short bursts well — shoot for 5-8 minutes, then let it cool for 2-3 minutes. Avoid leaving it in direct sunlight when not recording. For extended summer shoots, use airplane mode (disables wireless radios) and remove the battery between takes to accelerate cooling. The camera will display a temperature warning before shutting down, giving you time to wrap up.
