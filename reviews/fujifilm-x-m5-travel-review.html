The Fujifilm X-M5 is the most enjoyable carry-everywhere travel camera we have used in years — a genuinely pocketable interchangeable-lens body whose film simulations make holiday footage look finished straight out of camera. After three European city breaks with it hanging from a wrist strap, it is the camera we now recommend to travelers who care about how their videos feel, not just how sharp they are.

The pitch is simple: take Fujifilm's proven roughly 26-megapixel APS-C sensor and beloved film-simulation color science, wrap it in one of the smallest and lightest X-mount bodies ever made, and price it around $800 body-only or roughly $900 with the kit zoom. We spent three weekends finding out whether small-and-pretty could survive real travel — dawn trams in Lisbon, Prague's Christmas-market crowds, and a very cold Krakow food tour.

Key Takeaways

  • One of the lightest APS-C interchangeable-lens cameras you can buy — it genuinely disappears in a jacket pocket with a pancake lens.
  • Film simulations (Classic Chrome, Reala Ace and friends) deliver gorgeous, editing-free color for travelers who hate grading.
  • Video specs punch above the price: detailed 4K and even 6.2K open-gate recording for cropping vertical and horizontal from one clip.
  • No in-body stabilization and no viewfinder — video-first travelers only.
  • More beautiful but less effortless than the DJI Osmo Pocket 3 — different tools, different travelers.

Quick Verdict

If you want a small camera that makes your holiday footage look like a film without opening an editor, buy the X-M5. It is the rare camera that made us film more, not less — because the results on the flip-out screen were so pretty we kept finding excuses to shoot. In Lisbon we clipped it to a wrist strap at 7am and were still happily filming the last pastel de nata at 10pm.

The trade-offs are clear: no viewfinder, no in-body stabilization, and a grip so small that big lenses feel silly on it. Treat it as a prime-lens street camera and it sings. Score: 8.5/10 — it has earned a place in our best compact cameras for city breaks guide.

The Carry-Everywhere Test

Every camera claims to be portable. The X-M5 actually is. Body and a compact prime together weigh roughly what a large phone plus a power bank does, and the whole rig slid into the inside pocket of a light jacket. In Prague this mattered constantly: castle stairs, crowded trams, a river cruise where bags went under the seat — the camera was simply never a burden, which meant it was never left at the hotel. That is the single most important spec in travel photography and nobody puts it on a chart.

The flip side of small is deliberate compromise. There is no viewfinder — you compose on the articulating touchscreen, which is bright and good, though harsh midday sun in Lisbon occasionally meant shading it with a hand. And the minimal grip rewards small lenses: pair it with a pancake or compact prime and it is a dream; bolt on a big zoom and the balance goes comical.

Film Simulations: Editing-Free Color

Why they matter on holiday

Here is the thing about travel video: most of us never color-grade it. Footage sits on a drive because "I'll edit it later" becomes never. Fujifilm's film simulations attack that problem head-on — they are sophisticated color profiles baked into the camera, tuned to mimic classic film stocks. Dial in Classic Chrome and a Prague side street instantly looks moody and cinematic; Reala Ace gives soft, natural color that flatters skin and stone alike; Velvia makes Lisbon's tiled facades and tram yellows explode.

Real-world results

We shot an entire Krakow food-tour video in-camera: Classic Chrome for the market hall, a warmer simulation for the evening milk-bar scenes, and cut it together on the train home with zero grading. Uploaded as-is, it looked better than footage we have spent hours grading from other cameras. For travelers, this is not a gimmick — it is hours of your holiday returned to you. Local shooters we interviewed for our City Through the Lens series say the same: Fuji color is why many of them never learned to grade at all.

City-break tip: Pick one simulation per day and stick to it — your day's clips will automatically match in the edit. Consistency, not variety, is what makes holiday videos look professional. More in our guide to filming better holiday videos.

Video Quality on a City Break

Video quality is seriously good for the size and price. Standard 4K is detailed and clean, and the party trick is 6.2K open-gate recording, which captures the full sensor height so you can crop vertical and horizontal versions from the same clip — film a Lisbon funicular once, post it to YouTube and Reels both. Digital stabilization smooths walking footage respectably, though with a crop, and it cannot work miracles on cobblestones at a jog.

Low light is solid APS-C territory: evening footage from Prague's lantern-lit lanes stayed clean and atmospheric, a clear step above any 1-inch compact. Autofocus with subject detection tracked faces through market crowds reliably — not quite Sony-sticky, but dependable enough that we stopped thinking about it, which is the point.

Watch out: The X-M5 has no in-body stabilization and no weather sealing. Cobbled old towns demand a steady hand or a stabilized lens, and rain means a pocket, not heroics. Pack a simple rain sleeve — our rain protection guide has the cheap solutions that actually work.

Design, Dials and Handling

Fujifilm gave the X-M5 a proper film-simulation dial on the top plate — a small joy in daily use. Swapping looks mid-street became reflexive, and it made experimenting feel physical rather than menu-diving. The vlog mode simplifies the interface for self-filming, the three-microphone array captured surprisingly respectable street ambience and café narration, and the touchscreen interface is the friendliest Fuji has made. The only handling grumble across three trips: the small buttons get fiddly with gloves, which Krakow in winter made very clear.

Battery and Day-Trip Logistics

Battery life is the honest weak point — the compact body takes a compact battery. A full sightseeing day of mixed clips and stills needed one spare battery or a USB-C top-up at lunch, and cold weather in Krakow shaved things further. Our routine: two batteries, charge both nightly, done. Storage is the other planning note — 6.2K clips eat cards fast, so size up following our memory card guide rather than grabbing whatever is in the drawer.

Who Should Buy / Who Should Skip

Buy the X-M5 if: you value beautiful straight-out-of-camera color, you want interchangeable lenses in a jacket-pocket body, you shoot both stills and video on city breaks, or you simply will never learn to color grade. It is the most charming travel camera of the year.

Skip it if: you want fully automatic grab-and-go filming (the DJI Osmo Pocket 3 wins), you need an all-in-one pocket zoom (look at the Sony ZV-1 II), or you demand in-body stabilization and weather sealing for rough trips. FAQ

Is the Fujifilm X-M5 good for travel video?

Yes — it is one of the best small travel video cameras we have tested. The APS-C sensor delivers detailed 4K and even 6.2K open-gate footage, and the film simulations make clips look graded straight out of camera. Just know there is no in-body stabilization, so walking shots rely on digital stabilization and steady technique.

What are Fujifilm film simulations and do they work for video?

Film simulations are Fujifilm's built-in color profiles modeled on classic film stocks — Classic Chrome, Velvia, Reala Ace and more. They absolutely work for video, and they are the X-M5's superpower: footage comes out of the camera with rich, filmic color that needs no editing, which is ideal for travelers who never open a grading app.

Fujifilm X-M5 vs Sony ZV-1 II for a city break?

The X-M5 has a much larger APS-C sensor, interchangeable lenses and superior color straight out of camera; the Sony ZV-1 II is a fixed-lens pocket zoom that is simpler and smaller with a lens attached. Choose the X-M5 for image quality and creative growth, the ZV-1 II for zero-decision convenience.

Is the Fujifilm X-M5 good for beginners?

Very much so. The dedicated film-simulation dial, friendly touchscreen interface and simplified vlog mode make it approachable immediately, while the X-mount lens system gives years of room to grow. The only beginner caveat is the small battery — carry a spare.
