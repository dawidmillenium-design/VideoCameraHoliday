👖 Ricoh GR IV Travel Review (2026)

Is this the ultimate pocketable camera for travel? We tested the GR IV on the streets of Tokyo, the night markets of Lyon, and coastal walks in Goa. Here's the verdict.

📅 Tested: Sep 2026 👖 Pocket Fit: Perfect ⭐ Rating: 9.3 / 10

Verdict: The Ricoh GR IV is the best truly pocketable APS-C camera on the market. It slips into a jeans pocket and delivers exceptional image quality. The 26MP sensor and IBIS make it a street photographer's dream. Just don't expect 4K video or a zoom lens. It's a specialist tool — and it's brilliant at what it does.

🔍 First Impressions: The Pocketable APS-C Dream

Ricoh has been refining the GR line for years, and the GR IV is the most complete version yet. With a 26MP APS-C sensor, 5-axis IBIS, and a 28mm f/2.8 lens, it packs professional image quality into a body that weighs just 262g. I carried it in my jeans pocket for three weeks across three countries — it's the camera I forgot I was carrying, until I needed it.

✈️ Travel Performance – Real-World Testing

🇯🇵 Tokyo – Street Photography & Tight Spaces

In the narrow alleys of Shinjuku and the quiet streets of Yanaka, the GR IV was invisible. The 28mm lens is wide enough for environmental portraits and narrow enough to avoid distortion. The snap focus mode — set to 2.5m — allowed me to shoot from the hip without looking through a viewfinder. The 26MP sensor captures incredible detail, even in the chaotic neon of Shibuya.

🇫🇷 Lyon – Night Markets & Low Light

At the Marché de la Croix-Rousse, the f/2.8 lens and IBIS worked together beautifully. I could shoot handheld at 1/15s with sharp results, capturing the warm glow of the market lights. The ISO performance is clean up to 3200, and usable at 6400. The lack of 4K video is a downside — but if you're buying this camera, video is not your priority.

🇮🇳 Goa – Beach & Daylight

In bright Goan sunshine, the GR IV's contrast-detect autofocus (yes, not phase-detect) was snappy enough for street and landscape. The dynamic range is excellent — highlights in the sky didn't blow out, and shadows retained detail. The small body made it easy to carry in a swim-short pocket.

⚔️ Ricoh GR IV vs Fujifilm X100VI – Which One to Buy?

This is the $1,500 vs $1,800 question that every travel photographer faces. Here's my take after using both extensively:

  • Pocketability: GR IV wins — 262g vs 521g. The X100VI needs a jacket pocket or a bag; the GR IV fits in a jeans pocket.
  • Image Quality: X100VI has higher resolution (40MP vs 26MP), but both are excellent. The X100VI has a more "premium" rendering; the GR IV is sharp and clinical.
  • Lens: X100VI is 35mm (classic all-rounder). GR IV is 28mm (wider, more environmental). Both are great — it's a personal preference.
  • Viewfinder: X100VI has a hybrid OVF/EVF. GR IV has none (you use the rear screen or an add-on). If you love viewfinders, the X100VI wins.
  • Video: X100VI shoots 6.2K. GR IV shoots 1080p. If video matters, don't buy the GR IV.

📌 My Take: If you want a compact camera that's a joy to use and can sit in a jacket pocket, get the X100VI. If you want the smallest possible APS-C camera that slips into a jeans pocket, get the GR IV. It's that simple.

📱 Ricoh GR IV vs iPhone 16 Pro – The "Always-There" Camera

The iPhone 16 Pro is always in your pocket, and it shoots 4K video. So why carry a GR IV? Here's when the GR IV wins:

  • Low light: The GR IV's sensor is much larger — you get natural noise, not computational smoothing.
  • Depth of field: Even at f/2.8, the APS-C sensor gives you real bokeh, not a software simulation.
  • Control: Physical dials for aperture and shutter speed — it's a real photographer's tool.
  • File quality: 26MP RAW files give you far more latitude in Lightroom than iPhone ProRAW.

The iPhone wins on video, zoom, and convenience. The GR IV wins on image quality and creative control. Choose accordingly.
