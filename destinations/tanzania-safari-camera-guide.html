🦁 Tanzania Safari Camera Guide 2026

The best cameras and lenses for filming the Serengeti, Ngorongoro Crater, and the Great Migration – tested by a local safari videographer.

✅ Updated: September 2026 • Tested on 2 safari trips

Filming Tips

Tanzania is the ultimate safari destination – home to the Serengeti, the Ngorongoro Crater, and the greatest wildlife show on Earth: the Great Migration. Filming here requires serious reach (you can't get close to lions), dust-proof gear, and the ability to shoot in harsh midday light. We sat down with Musa Juma, a Tanzanian safari videographer with 15+ years of experience, to get the real story on capturing East Africa's wildlife.

⚡ Quick Takeaways for Safari Filmmakers

  • Best camera for safari video: Panasonic Lumix ZS99 / TZ99 – 720mm reach in a pocket, or Sony RX10 IV for pro quality.
  • Top locations: Serengeti (Great Migration), Ngorongoro Crater (Big Five), Tarangire (elephants), Lake Manyara (flamingos).
  • Dust is your enemy: Bring a rain cover and a blower. Don't change lenses in the open.
  • Shoot in the golden hours: 6–9 AM and 4–7 PM. Midday light is harsh.

📍 4 Must-Film Safari Locations

1. Serengeti National Park – The Great Migration

Why it's great: The annual migration of 1.5 million wildebeest and zebras is one of the world's greatest wildlife spectacles. The river crossings are pure cinema.

Filming tip: July–October is the best time for river crossings. A telephoto lens (400mm+) is essential. The Panasonic ZS99's 720mm is a game-changer for safari.

2. Ngorongoro Crater – The Big Five

Why it's great: A UNESCO World Heritage site with the densest population of lions, elephants, rhinos, buffalo, and leopards in Africa.

Filming tip: The crater floor is dusty – protect your gear with a rain cover. A polarizing filter cuts through the haze.

3. Tarangire National Park – Elephants & Baobabs

Why it's great: Famous for massive elephant herds and ancient baobab trees. The landscape is incredibly photogenic.

Filming tip: Golden hour (sunset) is the best time – the baobabs silhouette against the orange sky. A wide-angle lens (16-35mm) captures the landscape.

4. Lake Manyara – Flamingos & Tree-Climbing Lions

Why it's great: A shallow soda lake with thousands of flamingos and the unique tree-climbing lions.

Filming tip: Morning (7-10 AM) is the best time for birdlife. A telephoto lens (200-600mm) is required for flamingos.

🌞 Safari Weather & Logistics

  • Dry Season (June–October): Best for wildlife – clear skies, dusty roads. Pack dust protection.
  • Wet Season (November–May): Lush landscapes, but rain can make roads impassable. Green season is great for birdlife.

"The biggest mistake tourists make is bringing a lens that's too short. You need at least 400mm for lions and 600mm for birds. The Panasonic ZS99 is a lifesaver for budget travellers — it gives you 720mm without changing lenses." — Musa Juma, Tanzania Safari Videographer

📜 Permits & Etiquette

  • Park fees: All national parks require entry fees (payable at the gate).
  • Drone ban: Drones are strictly prohibited in all Tanzanian national parks.
  • Respect animals: Stay in the vehicle. No flash photography.
