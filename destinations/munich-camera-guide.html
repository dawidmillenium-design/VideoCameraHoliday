📍 4 Must-Film Locations in Munich

1. Marienplatz – The Heart of Munich

Why it's great: Munich's central square is dominated by the neo‑Gothic New Town Hall with its famous Glockenspiel. The square is bustling with life and offers classic Bavarian architecture.

Filming tip: Sunrise (6:30 AM) is the best time – the square is empty and the light is soft. The Glockenspiel chimes at 11 AM, 12 PM, and 5 PM – a great filming opportunity. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) captures the full square, while a telephoto (70–200mm) compresses the tower and the surrounding buildings. A gimbal is essential for smooth walking shots through the crowds. Visit early to avoid the tourist crowds.

2. English Garden – Urban Nature

Why it's great: One of the world's largest urban parks – larger than Central Park. The lush greenery, the river surfers, and the Chinese Tower create a unique filming experience.

Filming tip: Golden hour (just before sunset) is the best time – the warm light over the meadows and the river is beautiful. The Eisbach wave (river surfing) is a must‑film – arrive in the afternoon when the surfers are out. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) compresses the surfers against the river, while a wide-angle captures the full park scene. A gimbal is essential for smooth walking shots along the paths.

3. Nymphenburg Palace – Baroque Grandeur

Why it's great: The magnificent Baroque palace with its sprawling gardens and lake offers a glimpse into Bavarian royal life. The sunset light on the yellow façade is stunning.

Filming tip: Sunset is the best time – the warm light on the palace façade is beautiful. The reflection pond offers a classic composition with the palace mirrored in the water. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) captures the full palace, while a telephoto compresses the façade. A tripod is essential for sunset timelapses. Visit in autumn for stunning foliage in the palace gardens.

4. Oktoberfest – The World's Largest Volksfest

Why it's great: The famous beer festival offers a unique cultural experience – colourful tents, traditional costumes, and a lively atmosphere.

Filming tip: Weekday mornings are quieter than weekends. The tents and fairground rides offer endless composition opportunities. A fast prime lens (35mm f/1.8) is perfect for low‑light tent interiors. Ask permission before filming people – most are happy to be featured but appreciate the courtesy. Respect the locals – don't disrupt the festivities.

🌦️ Munich Weather Reality for Filmmakers

Munich has four distinct seasons, each offering unique filming opportunities. Here's what to expect:

  • Summer (June–August): Warm and pleasant – temperatures hit 20–28°C (68–82°F). The city is bustling, and the beer gardens are full. Film early morning (6–9 AM) and golden hour (6–8 PM) to avoid the crowds and heat.
  • Autumn (September–November): One of the best times to film – the autumn colours are stunning, and the Oktoberfest (late September to early October) offers unique cultural footage. Temperatures are 10–20°C (50–68°F).
  • Winter (December–February): Cold and snowy – temperatures drop to -5 to 5°C (23–41°F). The Christmas markets (December) are magical. Battery life is reduced in the cold – carry spares. Protect your gear from snow and moisture.
  • Spring (March–May): Mild and pleasant – temperatures are 5–15°C (41–59°F). The spring blossoms and greenery are beautiful.

🌡️ Pro Tips for Munich's Seasons

  • Winter filming: Keep spare batteries in your inner pocket to keep them warm. The Christmas markets are best shot at dusk when the lights are on.
  • Summer filming: Keep cameras and batteries in the shade when not shooting – heat drains battery life.
  • Autumn colours: The best time is mid‑October – the colours in the English Garden are stunning.
  • Oktoberfest: Arrive early (9 AM) for quieter shots. Use a fast prime lens for low‑light tent interiors.
  • Shoot in flat profile: Gives you the most dynamic range for grading the beautiful light.

✈️ Airport & Gear Logistics

Munich Airport (MUC) is Germany's second‑busiest airport. Here's what you need to know:

  • Getting to the city: The S‑Bahn (S1 and S8) takes about 40 minutes to the city centre. It's comfortable and camera-bag friendly.
  • Public transport: Munich's U‑Bahn (subway) and trams are efficient. A day pass is a great option for getting around.
  • Security: German airport security is thorough. Keep your gimbal batteries, camera bodies, and lenses in your carry-on.
  • Gear insurance: Consider travel insurance that covers camera gear – Munich's cold winters are tough on equipment.

📜 Permits, Rules & Local Etiquette

  • Marienplatz: Filming is allowed for personal use. Commercial filming requires a permit from the city. No tripods during busy times.
  • Drones: Strictly regulated around the city centre and near the airport. Always check local regulations before flying.
  • Oktoberfest: Ask permission before filming inside the tents. Don't disrupt the festivities. Respect the locals – don't climb on tables or interfere with the beer servers.
  • Respect local customs: Germans appreciate punctuality and order. Avoid intrusive filming. Always ask before filming people.

  • Accommodation: $40–100 per night for a budget hotel or hostel. Mid-range hotels are $100–200.
