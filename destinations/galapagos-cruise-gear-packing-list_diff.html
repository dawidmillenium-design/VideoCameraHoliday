By Dawid Millennium | Published September 1, 2026 | 15 min read | 🏝️ Destination Guide

The Galapagos is unlike anywhere else on Earth. Blue-footed boobies nesting at your feet, marine iguanas swimming alongside you, giant tortoises older than civilization. But here's the problem: most travelers bring the wrong gear.

⚡ Key Takeaways

  • Zoom is king — 200mm+ essential for birds, 100mm for land animals
  • Waterproof housing mandatory — snorkeling with sea lions is non-negotiable
  • Dry bags save lives — daily wet landings mean constant water exposure
  • Battery management critical — no charging during day trips

Why Galapagos Gear Planning Matters

You're not just visiting a beach destination. The Galapagos demands:

  • Wildlife photography range — animals stay at safe distances (usually)
  • Water protection — Zodiac landings, snorkeling, sudden rain
  • Dust resistance — volcanic terrain kicks up fine particles
  • All-day battery life — 6-8 hours away from ship with no charging

Lens Strategy

This is where most people fail. You need THREE focal ranges:

  1. Wide-angle (16-35mm)
    • Landscape shots of volcanic terrain
    • Underwater wide scenes
    • Ship deck photography
  2. Standard zoom (24-70mm or 24-105mm)
    • Giant tortoises (they're close!)
    • Sea lion pups on beaches
    • General wildlife encounters
  3. Telephoto (70-200mm or 100-400mm)
    • Critical for birds — blue-footed boobies, frigatebirds
    • Marine iguanas on rocks (from safe distance)
    • Sea turtles swimming offshore
⚠️ Critical: Don't leave telephoto lens on ship. Birds won't come close. I saw travelers crying because they only brought 24-70mm lenses.

Underwater Camera

Snorkeling in the Galapagos is world-class. You'll swim with:

  • Sea lions (playful, curious)
  • Marine iguanas (unique to Galapagos)
  • Sea turtles (green and hawksbill)
  • Tropical fish in massive schools
  • Occasionally: hammerhead sharks, manta rays

Essential Accessories

1. Dry Bags (Non-Negotiable)

Zodiac landings mean getting wet. Even on "dry" landings, waves splash.

  • 20L dry bag — main camera bag protector
  • 5L dry bag — for phone, wallet, snacks during hikes
  • Dry bag rating: IPX8 or roll-top closure minimum

2. Waterproof Camera Housing

If using mirrorless/DSLR for underwater:

  • Olympus TG-6 housing (if using TG-6)
  • Ikelite or SeaLife housing for mirrorless
  • Test before trip! — submerge in bathtub first

3. Lens Cleaning Kit

Salt spray + volcanic dust = lens nightmare

  • Microfiber cloths (bring 10+, they get ruined)
  • Lens cleaning solution (travel size)
  • Rocket blower for dust
  • Lens pen for stubborn spots

4. Battery Strategy

You'll be away from ship 6-8 hours daily. Plan accordingly:

  • Minimum 4 batteries per camera
  • Dual charger — charge overnight in cabin
  • Power bank (20,000mAh+) — for phones, action cams
  • Battery case — protect from humidity

5. Memory Cards

  • Shoot more than you think — wildlife is unpredictable
  • Minimum 256GB per day — 4K video fills cards fast
  • Bring backup cards — no shops in Galapagos
  • V60 or V90 rating — for 4K video

6. Stabilization

  • Gimbal: DJI RS3 Mini (if using mirrorless for video)
  • Tripod: Leave it — not allowed on most islands, unstable on Zodiacs
  • Monopod: Good compromise for telephoto support

Packing by Activity

Wet Landings (Zodiac to Beach)

💡 What to Carry:
  • Camera in dry bag inside backpack
  • One lens attached (24-70mm or 70-200mm)
  • GoPro in pocket (ready for snorkeling)
  • Phone in waterproof case
  • Leave extras on boat when possible

Dry Landings (Zodiac to Dock/Stairs)

  • Full camera kit acceptable
  • Still use dry bag — waves happen
  • Telephoto lens for highland tortoises

Snorkeling Sessions

  • GoPro or action cam only (easiest)
  • Red filter for color correction below 3m
  • Floating hand strap (don't lose it!)
  • Anti-fog inserts (see our anti-fog guide)

Onboard Photography

  • Wide-angle for landscapes
  • Golden hour from upper deck
  • Wildlife spotting between islands

What NOT to Bring

  • Heavy tripods — impractical, often prohibited
  • Multiple camera bodies — one good body + lens swaps works
  • Expensive prime lenses — zoom versatility beats prime quality here
  • Drone — ILLEGAL in Galapagos National Park
  • Fisherman vest with 100 pockets — you'll overheat, look like tourist
⚠️ Drone Warning: Drones are strictly prohibited in Galapagos National Park. Fines up to $5,000 and equipment confiscation. Leave it home.

  • 1x Mirrorless camera (Sony a7 V or similar)
  • 2x Lenses: 24-105mm + 70-200mm

  • 3x Lenses: 16-35mm, 24-105mm, 100-400mm
  • 1x GoPro Hero 13 + housing

  • 1x 20L dry bag + 5L dry bag
  • 1x Full lens cleaning kit
  • 1x Dual battery charger
  • 1x 30,000mAh power bank
  • 1x Monopod

Galapagos-Specific Tips

Wildlife Etiquette

  • Stay 2 meters from animals — they don't fear humans, but you must respect them
  • No flash photography — disturbs wildlife
  • Don't touch — even if sea lion pup approaches you
  • Follow guide instructions — they know animal behavior

Best Times to Shoot

  • Early morning (6-8am): Active wildlife, soft light
  • Late afternoon (4-6pm): Golden hour, seabirds returning
  • Midday: Rest time — animals sleep, heat harsh

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I rent camera gear in Galapagos?

No. There are no camera rental shops in Galapagos. All gear must be brought from mainland Ecuador (Quito/Guayaquil) or from home. Pack everything you need before arrival.

Is a smartphone enough for Galapagos?

For casual photos, yes — iPhone 16 Pro or Samsung S25 Ultra have decent zoom. But for serious wildlife photography, you'll regret not having 200mm+ reach and faster autofocus. Bring at least an action cam for underwater.

How do I protect gear from salt corrosion?

Rinse all equipment with fresh water daily (ship has freshwater showers). Wipe down with microfiber. Store in dry bag with silica gel. Never leave gear exposed to sea spray overnight.

What about travel insurance for camera gear?

Galapagos trips are expensive — ensure your policy covers equipment loss/damage. Standard policies often exclude water damage. Get specialized adventure travel insurance that covers snorkeling/diving activities.

Should I bring a drone for aerial shots?

Absolutely not. Drones are illegal in Galapagos National Park. Rangers check luggage on arrival. Fines start at $2,000 and can reach $5,000 plus equipment confiscation. The risk isn't worth it.
