🇻🇳 Vietnam Travel Camera Guide 2026

The best cameras and gear for filming Vietnam's old quarters, Ha Long Bay, rice terraces, and more – tested in the field by local videographers.

✅ Updated: September 5, 2026 • Tested on 2 trips across Vietnam

Vietnam is a country of staggering diversity – from the chaotic streets of Hanoi's Old Quarter to the peaceful waters of Ha Long Bay, and from the golden rice terraces of Sapa to the bustling energy of Ho Chi Minh City. Filming here is a dream, but it's also a challenge. The heat and humidity are intense, the crowds are relentless, and the weather can change in an instant. After two trips across Vietnam and interviews with local videographers, I've put together this guide to help you choose the right camera and gear for this incredible country.

⚡ Quick Takeaways for Vietnam Filmmakers

  • Best cameras for Vietnam: Sony ZV-E10 II (versatility), DJI Osmo Pocket 4 Pro (portability), GoPro Hero 13 (water activities).
  • Top 3 filming locations: Hanoi's Old Quarter, Ha Long Bay, and Sapa's rice terraces.
  • Weather reality: North has four seasons; South is tropical year-round. Expect rain from May to October.
  • Battery life: Heat and humidity drain batteries fast – carry spares and keep them cool.
  • Drone rules: Drones are legal but require registration. Check local regulations before flying.

📍 3 Must-Film Locations in Vietnam

1. Hanoi's Old Quarter – at Dawn

Why it's great: The historic heart of Hanoi is a maze of narrow streets, each named after the goods once sold there. At dawn, the streets are quiet, the light is soft, and the city wakes up.

Filming tip: Arrive at 5:30 AM – the streets are empty and the morning light is perfect. Focus on Hoan Kiem Lake and the weekend night market (if you're there on a weekend). A fast prime lens (35mm f/1.8) is essential for low-light street scenes. Ask permission before filming people – most locals are happy to be featured but appreciate the courtesy.

2. Ha Long Bay – at Sunset

Why it's great: Thousands of limestone karsts rise from the emerald waters – one of Vietnam's most iconic landscapes. Sunset is magical.

Filming tip: Book a 2-day, 1-night cruise for the best light. The sunset from the deck and the sunrise over the karsts are unforgettable. A wide-angle lens (16–35mm) captures the full scale, while a telephoto isolates individual karsts. A polarizing filter helps cut through the glare on the water.

3. Sapa's Rice Terraces – at Golden Hour

Why it's great: The terraced rice paddies of Sapa are among the most photographed landscapes in Asia. Golden hour brings them to life.

Filming tip: The best time is September to October when the rice is golden and ready for harvest. The Muong Hoa Valley offers the most famous views. A drone (if you have a permit) captures the scale – but check local regulations before flying. A telephoto lens (70–200mm) compresses the landscape beautifully.

🌧️ Vietnam Weather Reality for Filmmakers

Vietnam's climate varies dramatically from north to south:

  • North (Hanoi, Ha Long Bay, Sapa): Four distinct seasons. Winter (December–February) is cool and dry (10–20°C). Spring (March–April) is mild and pleasant. Summer (May–August) is hot and rainy. Autumn (September–November) is the best time to film – clear skies, golden light, and comfortable temperatures.
  • Central (Da Nang, Hoi An, Hue): Hot and dry from January to August, rainy from September to December. Typhoons are possible from September to November – check the forecast before filming.
  • South (Ho Chi Minh City, Mekong Delta): Tropical – hot and humid year-round. Rainy season is May to October with short, intense downpours. Dry season is November to April.

🌡️ Pro Tips for Vietnam's Heat & Humidity

  • Keep batteries cool: Heat and humidity drain battery life – store spare batteries in a cool bag or your hotel room fridge.
  • Use a variable ND filter: The intense sunlight requires a ND filter to keep your shutter speed at 1/50 or 1/60 for cinematic motion blur.
  • Protect from dust and rain: A dust blower and lens wipes are essential. Carry a rain cover – downpours can appear suddenly.
  • Bring a lightweight tripod: The best shots are at sunrise and sunset – a small travel tripod is worth the extra weight.
  • Watch for condensation: Moving from an air‑conditioned hotel to the humid outdoors can cause lens fogging. Let your camera acclimatise before shooting.

✈️ Airport & Gear Logistics

Vietnam has three major international airports:

  • Noi Bai Airport (HAN) – Hanoi: The best airport for the north. Taxis to the city centre cost around 400,000 VND (~$16). The airport is about 30 km from the centre.
  • Da Nang Airport (DAD) – Central Vietnam: The best airport for central Vietnam. Taxis to the city centre cost around 150,000 VND (~$6).
  • Tan Son Nhat Airport (SGN) – Ho Chi Minh City: The busiest airport in Vietnam. Taxis to the city centre cost around 200,000 VND (~$8). Traffic can be brutal – allow plenty of time.
  • Security: Vietnamese airport security is thorough. Keep your gimbal batteries, camera bodies, and lenses in your carry-on. Lithium batteries are strictly prohibited in checked luggage.
  • Visa requirements: Most nationalities can get a 30‑day visa exemption. Check before you travel.

📜 Permits, Rules & Local Etiquette

  • Temples and pagodas: Always ask before filming inside – some require a small donation. Remove your shoes before entering, and dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees).
  • Street filming: Filming on public streets is generally allowed, but ask before filming people – especially in rural areas where locals may be less comfortable with cameras.
  • Drones: Drones are legal in Vietnam but must be registered with the Civil Aviation Authority of Vietnam (CAAV). Many tourist locations (including Ha Long Bay) have restricted zones. Always check local regulations before flying.
  • Respect local customs: Avoid pointing your camera at religious statues, don't photograph local people without permission, and be mindful of local cultural sensitivities.

💰 Budget Travel & Camera Gear

Vietnam is an incredibly affordable destination. Here's what you can expect to pay:

  • Accommodation: $10–30 per night for a hostel or budget hotel. Mid-range hotels are $30–60.
  • Food: Street food is $2–5 per meal. A local beer is under $1.
  • Transport: Motorbike rentals start at $5–10 per day. Grab (ride-hailing) is cheap and reliable.
  • Camera gear insurance: Consider travel insurance that covers camera gear – Vietnam's heat, humidity, and crowds are tough on equipment.

🎬 Final Advice

Vietnam is one of the most rewarding countries to film – the diversity of landscapes, the warmth of the people, and the incredible light make it a filmmaker's paradise. The key is to be prepared: bring the right gear, protect it from the heat and humidity, and be respectful of local customs.
