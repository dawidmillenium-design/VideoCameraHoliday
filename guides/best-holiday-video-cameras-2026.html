How We Test on Real Holidays

No lab charts here. Every camera here traveled in my actual carry-on: whipped out one-handed mid-walk, mounted on a ferry rail in sea spray, charged off power banks in airport lounges.

We score footage in mixed light, pocket-to-recording speed (missed moments kill holiday videos), battery over a full sightseeing day, durability, and carry comfort at hour six. A camera that stays in the hotel safe scores zero.

The 8 Best Holiday Video Cameras of 2026

1. DJI Osmo Pocket 3 — Best Overall

Verdict: The camera I recommend to almost everyone. A 1-inch sensor plus a three-axis gimbal means walking footage so smooth people assume I hired help. It starts instantly, face-tracking keeps you framed solo, and low light embarrasses every action camera here — a Chiang Mai night market shoot stayed colorful where my GoPro turned to mush. It's excellent in dim light. For a full deep dive on ISO performance and sensor size, see our guide to the best low light cameras for evening vacations. Full verdict in my DJI Osmo Pocket 3 review.

  • Pros: Superb stabilized 4K; great low light; instant to shoot; pocketable
  • Cons: Not waterproof; gimbal needs care around sand; not built for mounting to bikes or boards

2. GoPro Hero 13 Black — Best Action Camera

Verdict: Still the toughest way to film a holiday. Waterproof to 10 m bare, drop-proof on rocky trails, and lens mods (macro, ultra-wide, ND) genuinely expand what one small camera does. HyperSmooth remains class-leading on ski runs and handlebars; it went snorkeling with me in Lombok without a second thought. Built-in mics are okay, but for professional sound you need a jack. See our list of the best travel cameras with external mic inputs. Full notes in my GoPro Hero 13 review.

  • Pros: Bare waterproofing; brilliant stabilization; huge accessory ecosystem; lens mods
  • Cons: Weak low light; small-sensor look indoors; battery drains fast at max settings

3. Insta360 X5 — Best 360 Camera

Verdict: The solo traveler's secret weapon. Film everything in 8K 360, choose the angle later — never miss a moment because you pointed the wrong way. The invisible selfie stick delivers drone-like third-person shots, and replaceable lenses fixed my biggest X4 complaint after I scuffed one skiing. Sample footage notes in my Insta360 X5 review.

  • Pros: Never miss a shot; invisible selfie stick; replaceable lenses; great app editing
  • Cons: Reframing takes time; huge files; needs a selfie stick for the magic

4. Sony ZV-1 II — Best Compact Vlog Camera

Verdict: The best image quality per gram for city breaks. A 1-inch sensor and bright wide zoom deliver creamy background blur action cameras can't touch, with vlogger-friendly autofocus. It was my pick for a food-focused Lisbon weekend where dim restaurant footage mattered. Tiny, stylish, and fits in any pocket. Perfect for candid street shots where you don't want to draw attention.   • Pros: Cinematic look; great autofocus; mic input; compact
  • Cons: No weather sealing; digital stabilization crops; ~$700-$800

5. iPhone 16 Pro — Best Phone for Holiday Video

Verdict: The best camera you probably already own. Honest advice: with a recent flagship phone you may not need anything else. The iPhone 16 Pro shoots 4K 120 fps slow motion and Log video, with superb walk-and-talk stabilization. With my favorite editing apps for holiday footage, trip videos get cut on the flight home. 6. Canon PowerShot V10 — Best Budget Beginner Camera

Verdict: The friendliest first camera for vloggers. A 1-inch sensor, built-in stand, decent mics and flip screen for around $350-$450. Not waterproof, digital stabilization — but for beginners filming markets and viewpoints, it flatters. My Canon PowerShot V10 review has the full beginner-focused verdict.

  • Pros: Cheap for a 1-inch sensor; dead simple; built-in stand; good mics
  • Cons: Digital stabilization only; no zoom; not rugged

7. DJI Osmo Action 5 Pro — Best Battery Life

Verdict: The action camera that lasts all day. DJI's flagship recorded nearly four hours of 4K in my testing — roughly double a GoPro — and kept going in freezing ski weather that killed rivals. Its larger sensor gives the best action-cam low light I've used; it's waterproof to 20 m bare. Cold-weather data in the DJI Osmo Action 5 Pro review.

  • Cons: No lens mods; app less polished than GoPro's

8. Insta360 Go 3S — Wildcard: Best Wearable POV

Verdict: The tiny camera that films what your hands are doing. A thumb-sized 4K camera that clips to a cap or magnetizes to a shirt, capturing first-person moments — cooking classes, haggling, bike rides — impossible to get holding a camera. What to Avoid When Buying a Holiday Camera

Cheap no-name "4K action cameras" under $80. I've tested three: terrible stabilization, fantasy battery claims, housings fogged in minutes. Worse than a five-year-old phone.

Big DSLR or mirrorless kits for a first holiday. Unless video is your hobby, a 700 g body plus lenses will spend the trip in the hotel safe. Start small.

Buying on megapixel counts. For video, sensor size, stabilization and bitrate matter far more — a 12 MP action camera outshoots a 48 MP budget camcorder at dusk. Confused between form factors? We break down the pros and cons in our action camera vs camcorder comparison. Also, remember that traditional video cameras still have a place. See our updated picks for the best camcorders for family vacations.

Watch out: Marketplace "brand new, 70% off" GoPro and DJI listings are often refurbished, region-locked or fake. Buy from the manufacturer or an authorized retailer.

Buying Advice: When to Buy Last-Gen and Save

Generations move slowly; last year's model is often 90% as good for 25-35% less. The GoPro Hero 12 Black and DJI Osmo Action 4 remain excellent discounted buys, and the Insta360 X4 (see my Insta360 X4 review) is a steal now that the X5 exists — my budget camera guide tracks the best of these deals.

Buy current-gen when a feature is genuinely new (the X5's replaceable lenses, the Action 5 Pro's battery) or you keep cameras for 4-5 years. Best times: spring sales, Black Friday, and the weeks after a successor launches.

FAQ

What is the best holiday video camera for most people in 2026?

The DJI Osmo Pocket 3. Its 1-inch sensor and mechanical gimbal deliver smooth cinematic 4K, it starts in seconds, and it fits in a pocket. Choose otherwise only for waterproofing (GoPro Hero 13, DJI Action 5 Pro) or 360 (Insta360 X5).

Is my phone good enough for holiday videos?

Usually, yes. A recent flagship like the iPhone 16 Pro shoots stabilized 4K that's hard to fault on casual trips. Add a camera for waterproofing, all-day battery, or better low light.

GoPro or DJI action camera — which is better in 2026?

The GoPro Hero 13 Black wins on stabilization, lens mods and accessories; the DJI Osmo Action 5 Pro wins on battery, low light and value. For trips where charging is awkward I pick the DJI; for mounting flexibility, the GoPro.

How much should I spend on a holiday camera?

Around $300-$450 buys an excellent action camera (Hero 13, Action 5 Pro) or beginner vlog camera (Canon V10). Around $500-$700 gets the DJI Pocket 3 or Insta360 X5. More only makes sense for compacts like the Sony ZV-1 II.
