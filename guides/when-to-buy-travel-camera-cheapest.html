The New Model Release Strategy

When manufacturers announce new cameras, previous-generation prices plummet—often within hours. Here's the playbook:

Timing Your Purchase Around Releases

  1. Watch for rumors: Follow SonyAlphaRumors.com, CanonNews.com, NikonRumors.com
  2. Wait for official announcement: Prices don't drop until confirmation
  3. Buy within 48 hours: Retailers race to clear old stock first
  4. Consider refurbished: Manufacturer refurbished units get additional 15% off

Real Example: Sony ZV-1 Price History

  • Launch (May 2020): $748
  • ZV-1 II announced (May 2023): dropped to $598 same day
  • Black Friday 2023: $548 (lowest ever)
  • Current (2026): $498 refurbished, $548 new

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Black Friday actually good for cameras?

Yes, but selectively. Current-gen models see 20-30% discounts. Avoid "doorbuster" specials with inferior specs. Best deals are on Sony, Canon, and Fujifilm mirrorless bodies.

Should I wait for the next model or buy now?

If your current camera works, wait 2-3 months after new announcements for price drops. If you need it for an upcoming trip, buy now—missing shots costs more than saving $100.

Are holiday bundle deals worth it?

Sometimes. Calculate individual accessory prices. Many bundles include cheap SD cards and bags you'd replace anyway. Only buy if total value exceeds bundle price by 20%+.

What's the safest way to buy used cameras online?

Use KEH or MPB for graded warranties. For private sales, insist on shutter count photos, sensor test images, and PayPal Goods & Services (never Friends & Family).

Do camera prices drop faster for Sony or Canon?

Sony prices drop faster (15-20% in first year). Canon holds value better (10% drop). Fujifilm is middle ground. Panasonic Lumix sees steepest depreciation (25%+ first year).
