Why Cruising Requires Specialized Gear

Cruising presents unique challenges: salt spray that corrodes electronics, limited cabin storage, and diverse shooting scenarios from poolside relaxation to rugged shore excursions. A camera that works great in Paris might fail miserably on a Caribbean catamaran.

Regional Recommendations

🌴 Caribbean & Bahamas

Prioritize underwater performance. The GoPro Hero 13 with HyperSmooth 6.0 is ideal for snorkeling stops in Grand Cayman or St. John.

🏛️ Mediterranean (Europe)

You need zoom for architecture and low-light for evening dinners. The Sony ZV-E10 II with a 16-50mm lens offers the best balance of portability and quality for Rome and Barcelona.

🧊 Alaska

Cold weather drains batteries. Bring the DJI Pocket 3 for its internal heating efficiency, and carry 3x spare batteries kept warm in your pocket.

Essential Accessories for Cruisers

  • Silica Gel Packs: Throw 5 packs in your camera bag to combat humidity.
  • Floating Strap: Mandatory for any deck shooting.
  • Microfiber Cloths: Salt spray leaves residue; clean lenses daily.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bring my drone on a cruise?

Generally no. Most lines (Royal Caribbean, Carnival) ban drones on board. Some allow them stored away but forbid flying from the ship. Check your specific line's policy.

How do I protect my camera from salt spray?

Use a rugged action camera when possible. If using a mirrorless, wipe it down with a fresh water cloth immediately after sea exposure and store in a dry bag.

What about storage for a 7-day cruise?

Bring at least two 256GB SD cards. 4K video eats space quickly; offload to a portable SSD like the SanDisk Extreme every 2 days.

Is a GoPro better than an iPhone for pools?

Yes. iPhones are water-resistant but not designed for high-pressure water or submersion. GoPros are built for it.

Best camera for formal nights?

Leave the action cam in the cabin. Bring a compact like the Sony ZV-1 II for low-light dining room shots without flash.
