Canon EOS R50 vs Canon G7X Mark III – 2026 Camera Comparison

Choosing between the Canon EOS R50 and the Canon G7X Mark III comes down to one question: do you want a travel camera that grows with you, or one that disappears into your pocket? Both are Canon bodies, both shoot video, and both appear on our Best Cameras 2026 Under $500 shortlist. But they solve very different problems for very different travellers.

This comparison is based on real travel camera testing — no lab charts. We took both bodies on city breaks, beach holidays, and ski trips to see which one actually earns its place in a carry-on.

Canon EOS R50 – Pros & Cons

Pros

  • Best Color Science — Canon's colour pipeline is the reason so many travel filmmakers stay in the system.
  • Easy Menu System — you can hand this camera to a partner or a friend and they will find their way around it.
  • APS-C Sensor — a larger sensor than a typical compact, which matters for low light and background separation.

Cons

  • Limited RF Lenses — the RF ecosystem is thinner than older mounts, especially at the budget end.
  • Plastic Body — light, but it does not feel like a camera you would abuse on a safari.
  • No IBIS — in-body stabilisation is absent, so handheld footage depends on lens stabilisation and technique.

Real-World Use

On a city break, the R50 rewards you for slowing down. The APS-C sensor and Canon colour science mean less time in post, and the easy menu system means you spend your first afternoon shooting rather than reading a manual. The trade-off is bulk: with a lens attached, it is a bag camera, not a pocket camera.

Because there is no IBIS, the R50 pushes you toward good habits. Our guide to stabilizing without a gimbal is essentially written for bodies like this one — braced elbows, deliberate pans, and short clips rather than long wandering takes.

The G7X Mark III sits at the opposite end. It is the camera you carry when you would otherwise carry nothing. For beach holidays and evenings out, that difference in willingness to bring the camera often matters more than any specification.

Which Should You Buy?

Buy the Canon EOS R50 if: you want to build a system, you care about Canon colour science, and you are willing to carry a small bag. It is the stronger long-term travel video camera, and the APS-C sensor gives you more room to grow. Just budget for the limited RF lens range and plan around the lack of IBIS.

Buy the Canon G7X Mark III if: portability is the deciding factor. If the camera is not in your pocket, it is not filming. A compact that travels everywhere beats an interchangeable-lens body that stays in the hotel room.

FAQ

Is the Canon EOS R50 better than the Canon G7X Mark III for travel video?

For image quality and flexibility, the R50 has the advantage thanks to its APS-C sensor and interchangeable RF mount. For pure portability, the G7X Mark III wins because it fits in a pocket. The right answer depends on whether you will actually carry it.

Does the Canon EOS R50 have image stabilisation?

The R50 does not have IBIS (in-body image stabilisation). Handheld footage relies on lens stabilisation and shooting technique. See our guide to stabilizing without a gimbal.

How much does the Canon EOS R50 cost?

Our reference price for the Canon EOS R50 is 3,299 PLN. Prices vary by region and kit configuration, so check current listings before buying.
