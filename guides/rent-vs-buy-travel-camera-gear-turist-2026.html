⚡ Quick Answer

Rent if: You need high-end gear for a single trip (under 10 days), want to test before buying, or need specialized lenses (e.g., 600mm wildlife).
Buy if: You travel more than 3 times a year, shoot weekly, or need gear ready for spontaneous trips. The Break-Even Point: Typically 14-21 days of rental fees equals the cost of buying used.

Key Takeaways

  • The 14-Day Rule: If your trip is longer than 2 weeks, buying used often becomes cheaper than renting.
  • Insurance Matters: Rental insurance adds 10-15% to the cost but is essential for peace of mind abroad.
  • Tax Write-offs: Buying gear can be depreciated if you earn income from it; rentals are immediate expenses.
  • Try Before You Buy: Renting is the cheapest way to test a $3,000 camera before committing.

The Real Cost Breakdown

Many travelers underestimate the hidden costs of both options. Let's look at a real-world example: Renting a DJI Ronin RS3 Pro + Camera Kit vs. Buying It.

Rental Costs (Example: 7 Days)

  • Base Rate: $85/day × 7 = $595
  • Insurance: 10% = $60
  • Shipping (if not local): $40
  • Total: ~$695

Buying Costs (New vs. Used)

  • New Price: $2,800
  • Used Price (MPB/KEH): $2,100
  • Resale Value (after 1 year): ~$1,600
  • Net Cost of Ownership: $500 (depreciation)

In this scenario, if you only need it for one week, renting ($695) is slightly more expensive than the depreciation cost of buying and selling ($500), BUT buying requires $2,100 upfront capital. If you need it for two weeks, renting jumps to ~$1,250, making buying the clear financial winner.

🧮 The "Break-Even" Calculator

Use this simple formula to decide:

(Daily Rental Rate × Days) + Insurance = Total Rental Cost

If Total Rental Cost > 20% of Purchase Price, consider buying used.

Rule of Thumb: If rental costs exceed 20% of the item's value, start looking at buying options.

When Buying Makes Sense

Buying isn't just about cost; it's about availability and familiarity. You should buy if:

  • You shoot spontaneously: Gear is ready on your shelf when inspiration strikes.
  • You travel frequently: More than 3 trips a year usually justifies owning a core kit.
  • You need muscle memory: Knowing your camera blindfolded is crucial for capturing fleeting moments.
  • You can resell easily: Popular gear (Sony, DJI, Canon) holds value well on MPB or eBay.
Pro Tip: Buy your "core" kit (camera body, main lens, mic) and rent the "specialty" gear (telephoto lenses, heavy gimbals, drones) as needed.

FAQ

Does rental insurance cover international travel?

Most standard policies cover domestic use only. For international trips, you must purchase specific "travel coverage" or use a credit card that offers rental protection (like some premium Amex cards). Always read the fine print.

What happens if my gear gets stolen abroad?

You will be liable for the deductible (usually $500-$1,000) unless you have full replacement insurance. File a police report immediately within 24 hours to validate the claim.

Can I extend a rental if my trip runs long?

Yes, most platforms allow extensions via their app/website. However, if someone else has booked it next, you may be forced to return it on time or pay a premium rush fee.

Is it better to buy used or new?

For travel, buying used (from reputable sources like MPB, KEH, or B&H Used) is often smarter. You save 20-30% upfront, and since travel gear takes a beating, the resale hit is already absorbed by the first owner.
