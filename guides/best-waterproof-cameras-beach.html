Quick Answer: The Best Waterproof Beach Camera

Buy the GoPro Hero 13 Black. It's waterproof to 10 m without any housing, its HyperSmooth stabilization turns choppy swim footage into watchable clips, and the accessory ecosystem (floaty grips, dome ports, bite mounts) is unmatched. If you snorkel more than you sunbathe, step up to the DJI Osmo Action 5 Pro — its 20 m rating means no case anxiety, and the battery survives a full boat day.

How We Test on Real Holidays

Every camera here has been properly soaked on assignment: snorkeling over reefs in Bali, body-surfing in Portugal, and long beach days where fine sand gets into everything. We test the things spec sheets skip — whether the touchscreen works with wet fingers, how fast fogging appears inside housings, and whether the mic recovers after a dunking.

We also test the unglamorous part: what happens after the swim. Salt crystallizes in buttons and ports within hours, so a camera's rinse-and-dry tolerance matters as much as its depth rating. Anything that corroded, fogged or glitched is excluded from this list.

Pro tip: Before every swim, check the port doors and battery latch for sand grains — one grain breaks the seal. Then film a 10-second test clip in the shallows and review it before trusting the camera in deeper water. Our guide to protecting gear in wet conditions covers seals and anti-fog in more detail.

Verdict: The one that never needs a housing.   • Cons: Smaller accessory ecosystem; no lens mods; app less polished than GoPro's

3. Insta360 X5 (with Dive Case) — Best 360 for the Water

Verdict: Reef footage like nothing else — with one caveat. Bare, the X5 is waterproof to 15 m, but for underwater 360 you want the dedicated dive case: it corrects the stitching lines where the two lenses meet the water, producing seamless spherical footage of reefs and dive buddies. Above water it's brilliant for beach days — film everything, frame later. My Insta360 X5 review covers the dive case results, and the older Insta360 X4 is a cheaper route to the same trick.

  • Pros: Unique 360 underwater footage; invisible selfie stick; 15 m bare rating; replaceable lenses
  • Cons: Needs the dive case for good underwater stitching; big files; editing takes effort

4. Insta360 Go 3S — Best Tiny Beach Camera

Verdict: The camera you forget you're wearing — in the shallows. The thumb-sized camera unit is itself waterproof to 10 m, so it clips to a hat or rash guard for hands-free POV clips in the surf. The charging pod isn't waterproof though — treat the pod like a phone. Image quality trails the big action cams, but for casual beach clips the perspectives win.

  • Pros: Tiny and wearable; camera unit waterproof; effortless hands-free clips
  • Cons: Pod is not waterproof; short battery off the pod; modest image quality

5. Waterproof Phone Pouches — The Budget Option, With a Reality Check

Verdict: Acceptable for poolside splashes; a gamble in the sea. A quality IPX8 pouch from a known brand protects a phone for snorkeling at the surface, and I've used them without disaster. But I've also watched a traveler's pouch fog, leak, and kill a $900 phone in Thailand. If you go this route: test the pouch with a tissue inside first, keep the phone in shallow water only, and accept that touchscreens barely work through plastic underwater. What to Avoid: Phone Pouches & False Promises

"Water resistant" phones in the sea. IP68 ratings cover fresh water in lab conditions. Saltwater voids most warranties, and corrosion can kill a phone days later. Treat any phone near the ocean as unprotected.

Generous-looking depth ratings on cheap cameras. A no-name camera "rated 30 m" (with its included case) usually leaks at 3 m. Stick to GoPro, DJI or Insta360 for anything deeper than a paddling pool.

Underwater use without testing the seal first. Every flooded camera story starts with "it was fine last time." Seals age, sand hides, latches loosen. Test in the shallows every single day.

Watch out: Condensation inside a lens or housing means moisture is already in. Stop using it, power off, remove the battery if possible, and dry it in a sealed bag with silica gel before it becomes corrosion.

Buying Advice & Saltwater Care Rules

On timing: last-gen waterproof cameras are excellent buys — the DJI Osmo Action 4 (18 m) and GoPro Hero 12 Black (10 m) both drop well under $300 in sales and remain superb beach cameras. Our budget cameras under $500 guide tracks those deals.

Saltwater care rules I follow religiously:

  • Rinse in fresh water within an hour of any saltwater contact — a bottled-water rinse on the beach beats nothing.
  • Soak, don't blast: 10 minutes in a bowl of fresh water dissolves salt around buttons better than a tap jet.
  • Open doors only when fully dry, and dry the seals with a lint-free cloth before closing again.
  • Lubricate o-rings on dive housings with silicone grease every few trips; replace scratched or flattened o-rings immediately.
  • Anti-fog inserts inside housings on humid tropical days — fog ruins more underwater footage than leaks do.

FAQ

What is the best waterproof camera for a beach holiday in 2026?

The GoPro Hero 13 Black for most travelers (10 m waterproof, best stabilization, huge accessory range) or the DJI Osmo Action 5 Pro if you snorkel — it's rated to 20 m without a case and has far better battery life.

Can I take my iPhone swimming in the sea?

It's a gamble. IP68 ratings cover fresh water in lab conditions, saltwater voids most warranties, and corrosion can kill the phone days later. Use a quality IPX8 pouch for surface snorkeling at most — a real action camera is far safer.

Do I need a dive case for the Insta360 X5?

The X5 is waterproof to 15 m bare, which is fine for swimming and surface snorkeling. For underwater 360 footage you want the dive case — it corrects the lens stitching so the two halves of the sphere join seamlessly underwater.

How do I care for a camera after saltwater use?

Rinse it in fresh water within an hour, soak it for 10 minutes to dissolve salt around buttons, open the doors only when fully dry, and grease housing o-rings regularly. Salt crystallizes fast — the rinse is not optional.
