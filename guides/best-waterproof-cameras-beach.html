Best Waterproof Cameras for Beach 2026

I learned the hard way that salt water and cameras don't mix. On my first beach trip with a brand-new camera, a rogue wave splashed my gear, and the camera never worked the same again. That mistake cost me hundreds of dollars — and a lot of frustration.

Since then, I've made it my mission to find cameras that can actually survive the beach. I've tested rugged cameras on two beach trips — Goa (India) and the Maldives — shooting in salt spray, direct sunlight, and underwater. I've dropped them in sand, submerged them in the ocean, and filmed in the most challenging conditions a traveller could face.

After all that testing, I've learned one thing: there is no single "best" waterproof camera. It depends on what you want to shoot. If you want the best stills and macro shots, choose the OM System TG-7. If you want smooth, stabilised video, choose the GoPro Hero 13. And if you're on a tight budget, the Fujifilm XP130 or Panasonic TS30 are excellent value under $200.

In this guide, I'll break down everything you need to know — from the best cameras to cleaning tips, underwater techniques, and how to choose the right camera for your beach holiday.

🧪 How We Tested These Cameras

Locations: Palolem Beach (Goa) and Maafushi Island (Maldives) — two very different beach environments with saltwater, sand, and strong sunlight.

Conditions: 35°C heat, high humidity, salt spray, and sudden rain. We tested each camera in the worst possible conditions to see what would survive.

Duration: Each camera was used as the primary beach camera for at least 5 days of intensive shooting — from sunrise to sunset.

What we looked for: Waterproofing reliability (no leaks), image quality (stills and video), battery life in heat, ease of cleaning after beach use, and overall portability. We also tested how well each camera handled sand, salt, and splashes.

🧼 How to Clean Your Waterproof Camera After the Beach

Salt water and sand are the enemies of any camera. Here's my step-by-step routine after every beach shoot:

  1. Rinse with fresh water: Immediately after leaving the beach, rinse your camera with fresh water (not salt water). Use a gentle stream — never high pressure.
  2. Dry with a soft cloth: Use a microfibre cloth to dry the body and lens. Pay special attention to the lens and any seals.
  3. Open all doors: Open the battery door and port covers to let moisture escape. Leave them open for at least an hour in a dry place.
  4. Remove the battery: If you're storing the camera, remove the battery to avoid corrosion.
  5. Store in a dry place: Keep the camera in a cool, dry place with silica gel packets to absorb residual moisture.

🤿 Underwater vs Beach: Choosing the Right Camera

Not all waterproof cameras are created equal. Here's how to choose based on your activity:

  • Snorkeling & Shallow Diving (up to 15m): TG-7 is perfect — it's rated to 15m and has excellent macro capabilities. Hero 13 is also good but needs a dive housing for deeper dives.
  • Deep Diving (15m+): Hero 13 with a dive housing — rated to 60m with third-party housings. TG-7 is not suitable beyond 15m.
  • Beach & Surface: Hero 13 (10m waterproof) or XP130 (15m). Both handle splashes and short dips easily.
  • Casual Pool/Waterpark: XP130 or TS30 — cheap, cheerful, and you won't cry if you lose them.

📱 iPhone with a Waterproof Case – A Viable Option?

Yes, for casual use. A waterproof case for your iPhone 16 Pro can be a cost-effective solution for beach days. The downsides: touchscreens don't work well underwater, cases add bulk, and the image quality is still limited by the phone's small sensor. A dedicated camera is still better for serious underwater photography.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Which is better for snorkeling – TG-7 or Hero 13?
A: The TG-7 is better for stills and macro; the Hero 13 is better for video. If you want both, consider taking both cameras — they serve different purposes.
Q: Can I use these cameras for deep diving?
A: The TG-7 is rated to 15m – great for snorkeling and shallow diving. The Hero 13 needs a dive housing for deeper dives. With a housing, the Hero 13 can go to 60m.
Q: How do I clean a waterproof camera after beach use?
A: Rinse with fresh water immediately after use. Never press buttons underwater. Dry with a soft cloth and open the battery door to let it air out. Store with silica gel.
Q: Are there cheaper options under $100?
A: The Panasonic TS30 is often under $100, but quality drops significantly. For a reliable beach camera, the XP130 is the better choice at around $150.
Q: Can I use a waterproof phone case instead of a camera?
A: Yes, for casual use. A waterproof case for your iPhone 16 Pro is a cost-effective option for beach days. But touchscreens don't work well underwater, and image quality is limited. A dedicated camera is better for serious underwater photography.
