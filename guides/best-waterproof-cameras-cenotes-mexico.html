Best Waterproof Cameras for Exploring Cenotes in Mexico

Field Tested in the Yucatán Peninsula | Updated for 2026

Swimming in a cenote feels like stepping into another world. These natural limestone sinkholes, scattered across Mexico’s Yucatán Peninsula, offer crystal-clear freshwater, ancient cave systems, and some of the most unique underwater videography opportunities on the planet. But filming them requires the right gear. In this guide, we break down the best waterproof camera for cenotes Mexico travelers can rely on, along with essential tips for capturing the magic.

🌊 What are Cenotes? (And Where to Film Them)

Cenotes (pronounced seh-NOH-tehs) are natural pits formed by the collapse of porous limestone bedrock, revealing the groundwater underneath. To the ancient Maya, they were sacred gateways to the underworld (Xibalba). Today, they are a filmmaker's paradise.

For the best variety, base yourself in Tulum or Valladolid. You must visit Cenote Suytun for its iconic circular opening and stunning sunbeams, Cenote Dos Ojos for expansive underwater cave systems, and Cenote Ik Kil for its dramatic hanging vines and deep, dark blue water.

Top Waterproof Cameras for Cenotes

When you are dangling your camera over a 30-foot drop into emerald water, or swimming through tight cave passages, you need gear that is rugged, compact, and delivers excellent color underwater.

Best Overall

GoPro Hero 13 Black

The undisputed king of action videography. Its HyperSmooth stabilization is a lifesaver when swimming against gentle currents, and the new wide-angle lens captures the massive scale of open cenotes perfectly. Waterproof to 10m without a housing.

🎬 Tips for Underwater Filming in Cenotes

  • Chase the "God Rays": The most magical shots happen when sunbeams pierce the canopy. To get this, you must visit open cenotes between 11:00 AM and 1:00 PM when the sun is directly overhead.
  • Lock Your White Balance: Water absorbs red light, making everything look blue or green. If your camera allows it, manually set your white balance to "Underwater" or "Cloudy" to bring back the natural skin tones and rock colors.
  • Get Low and Look Up: The most dramatic perspective in a cenote is looking up toward the surface. Position yourself near the bottom (safely) and point your camera upward to capture the contrast between the dark water and the bright opening above.

Travel Logistics: Getting There & Weather

Transportation: While you can take colectivos (shared vans) from Tulum or Playa del Carmen to major cenotes, having a rental car gives you the freedom to arrive right at opening time to beat the crowds. Roads in the Yucatán are generally flat and easy to drive.

Weather & Timing: The best time to film is during the dry season (November to April). The water is at its clearest. During the rainy season (June to October), heavy rains can wash sediment into the cenotes, reducing visibility and making the water murky.

Post-Swim Food Recommendation: After a morning of filming, drive into Valladolid and head to La Paloma de Valladolid. Order the Cochinita Pibil (slow-roasted pork marinated in achiote and citrus). It’s the ultimate Yucatán comfort food and will refuel you for your afternoon shoot!

🛂 A Quick Note on Visas & Local Policies

Please note that visa policies and entry requirements can change over time. Citizens of the US, Canada, UK, EU, and many other countries do not need a visa for tourism stays up to 180 days in Mexico, but you must fill out the FMM form upon arrival. Always check the official Mexican consulate website for the most current regulations.

⚠️ Crucial Environmental Policy: Mexican law strictly prohibits the use of standard chemical sunscreens and insect repellents in cenotes to protect the fragile aquatic ecosystem. You must use biodegradable, reef-safe sunscreen, or better yet, wear a UV-protective rash guard. Fines for violating this are heavy, and it protects the beautiful water you are there to film!
