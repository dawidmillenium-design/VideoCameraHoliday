Imagine driving 500 miles, capturing stunning sunsets and incredible landscapes, and then dropping your SD card into a puddle. All your memories are gone.

If you are traveling on a road trip, you need a solid backup strategy. The “one copy” rule is a recipe for disaster. Here is how to protect your data on the road.

The “3-2-1” Backup Rule

  1. 3 copies of your data.
  2. 2 different types of media (SD card + external hard drive).
  3. 1 copy stored off-site (Cloud storage).

How to Execute the 3-2-1 on a Road Trip

1. The “Nightly” External Drive Backup

Every evening when you get back to your hotel or campsite, plug your SD card reader into a rugged, portable SSD (like a Samsung T7) and copy the day’s folder over. Label the folder with the date and location. Keep this drive in a separate pocket from your camera.

2. The “Cloud Bridge” (Best for Slow Wi-Fi)

If you have decent Wi-Fi, upload the most important shots to Google Drive or iCloud immediately. It may take a while, but starting the upload while you go out for dinner ensures they are saved by the time you get back.

3. The “Second Card” Rule

If you are shooting high-volume (like a timelapse), carry a second memory card and swap them out halfway through the day.
