How to Store Camera Gear When Traveling (Airport Security & Humidity)

📅 2026-10-31 📖 2 min read ✍️ Dawid
⚠️ Disclosure: Some links are affiliate links. I earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

You’ve spent thousands on your camera gear. Now, you have to navigate airport security, humid environments, and bumpy bus rides. How do you store your gear to ensure it survives?

Here is the ultimate travel storage guide.

Airport Security: The “Hand Luggage” Rule

The Golden Rule: Your camera gear ALWAYS goes in your carry-on bag. Checked luggage gets thrown, crushed, and stolen. Camera gear is delicate. Never check your camera, batteries, or SD cards.

Batteries and TSA Rules

You can bring lithium-ion batteries on the plane, but they must be in your carry-on. * Pro Tip: Use electrical tape over the battery terminals. This prevents accidental shorts if they touch metal. TSA might ask, but it’s the safest method.

Fighting Humidity (The Silica Gel Trick)

If you are traveling to a tropical destination, humidity is your enemy. Condensation can damage the internal electronics of your camera and cause fungus to grow on your lenses. * Pack Silica Gel: Put a few silica gel packets (the little “Do Not Eat” packets) inside your camera bag to absorb moisture. * The Ziplock Bag: If your bag gets wet, take the silica packets and your camera, put them in a large ziplock bag, and freeze the moisture out.

Packing for Bumpy Rides (The Cube System)

Don’t throw your gear into a soft backpack. Use a “Camera Cube” (a padded insert) inside a regular backpack. This keeps your camera safe from impacts without looking like an expensive camera bag to thieves.

  • Protect your lens: Always put the lens cap on and face the lens upward when sitting in a bag. Never let a lens dig into the bottom.
