Color Grading Travel Videos: From Flat to Cinematic

📅 2026-10-15 📖 3 min read ✍️ Dawid
⚠️ Disclosure: Some links are affiliate links. I earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Have you ever watched a travel video and wondered why the colors look so vibrant and moody? That is not magic—that is Color Grading.

Travel footage captures real-world lighting, which is often flat, grey, or unpredictable. Color grading is the process of adjusting the colors, contrast, and tone of your video to transform it into a cinematic masterpiece.

Here is how to color grade your travel videos like a pro.

Step 1: Shoot for the Grade (Before You Edit)

To color grade, you must first “Shoot Flat.” If your camera has a “Log” or “Flat” picture profile, turn it on. This makes the image look washed out and low-contrast, but it captures much more data in the shadows and highlights. Think of it as a RAW ingredient; you need the flat file to cook a beautiful final dish.

Step 2: The Basics of Color Correction

Before you get creative, you must do Correction (fixing the issues). 1. White Balance: Ensure that whites are actually white. 2. Exposure: Bring the brightness up or down so the image is not too dark or blown out. 3. Contrast: Add contrast to get rid of the “flat” look.

Step 3: The “Teal & Orange” Look

This is the most famous cinematic look in Hollywood and travel videos. Why? Because skin tones are orange, and shadows are teal. The contrast between these two colors naturally pops. * Shadows: Push slightly toward cyan or blue. * Midtones: Keep skin tones nice and warm (orange).

Step 4: The “Golden Hour” Boost

Travel is all about sunsets and sunrises. If your video lacks that golden, warm glow, use the “Temperature” slider and push it slightly warm (toward yellow). Lower the contrast slightly, and add a subtle vignette (darkening the edges of the frame) to focus the eye on the center.

Step 5: Adding Atmosphere

A cinematic travel video often has a specific “mood” based on the location: * Tropical/Beach: Vibrant blues, boosted greens, and high saturation. * Moody City: Cool tones, crushed blacks, and desaturated colors. * Snowy Mountains: Slightly blue, crisp, and bright.

Color Grading on the Go

You don’t need a desktop PC to color grade. Most modern apps on your phone or tablet have color wheels and curves.

Check out our guide on Editing Travel Videos on Phones & Tablets to learn the exact app settings for pushing colors on a mobile device.

The Importance of Light

Color grading can save an ugly image, but it can’t create light where there is none. To avoid heavy grading, make sure you capture beautiful footage in the first place. Read our Travel Video Lighting Guide to master natural light before you start editing.

Final Thoughts

Color grading is a journey, not a destination. Don’t be afraid to experiment. Start by copying looks you like, then branch out to create your own unique travel style.
