How to Film in the Rain: Protecting Your Travel Camera

📅 2026-10-21 📖 2 min read ✍️ Dawid
⚠️ Disclosure: Some links are affiliate links. I earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Rain is not the enemy of travel videography; shooting in the rain is how you capture moody, cinematic, and unique footage that nobody else has. The problem is protecting your expensive gear from water damage.

Here is the ultimate survival guide for shooting in wet weather.

Step 1: Protect the Camera Itself

Unless you are using a dedicated waterproof action camera, your camera body is not waterproof.

  • Rain Covers: Look for cheap, clear plastic rain covers that fit over your lens and camera body. They are cheap, pack flat, and provide instant protection.
  • The “Umbrella Rule”: If you have an assistant or a tripod, have them hold a small umbrella over the camera. This protects the lens and allows you to shoot even in a downpour.

Step 2: Protect the Lens (The Biggest Threat)

Raindrops hitting your front lens element will ruin your shots. * Invest in a Lens Hood: Even a small hood keeps 90% of rain off the glass. * The “Rain Wipe”: Carry a microfibre cloth in a zip-lock bag. When a drop hits the lens, wipe it immediately.

Step 3: Condensation (The Silent Killer)

Moving from the cold, wet street into a warm, humid cafe is a nightmare for lenses. Your lens will instantly fog up. * The Fix: Keep your camera in your bag when you enter a building. Let the temperature equalize slowly before opening the bag.

Step 4: The Gear that Survives

If you want to shoot heavy rain without worry, you need gear that is built for it. * Action Cameras: The best choice for rain and splashing water. * Weather-Sealed Cameras: Look for mirrorless cameras with weather sealing.
