What is the Sony ZV-1 II?

Released in 2023, the ZV-1 II is Sony's second-generation vlogging camera. It is essentially a compact point-and-shoot optimized for video creators.

Sensor 1" stacked CMOS (20.1MP)
Lens 18-50mm f/1.8-4 (fixed)
Video 4K30, 1080p120
Screen 3" flip touchscreen
Weight 292g
Battery NP-BX1 (~45min 4K)
Price $898

🎬 Video Quality: The Real Test

Daylight Performance

The ZV-1 II produces stunning 4K video in good light. Sony's color science delivers natural skin tones without the yellow cast of Canon or the oversaturation of GoPro.

Tested locations:

  • ☀️ Bangkok midday sun — Excellent, ND filter essential
  • 🌅 Bali sunset — Beautiful warm tones, minimal noise
  • 🏛️ Chiang Mai temples — Good in mixed light, some shadow noise

Low Light Performance

At ISO 1600-3200, noise becomes noticeable but acceptable for YouTube. Above ISO 6400, footage gets grainy.

Comparison:

  • ZV-1 II at ISO 3200: Usable with light noise reduction
  • iPhone 16 Pro at ISO 3200: Similar noise, less detail
  • Canon R50 at ISO 3200: Cleaner, but costs $300 more

For a complete breakdown of how the ZV-1 II compares to using just your phone, see our iPhone 16 Pro vs dedicated camera shootout — the results might surprise you.

Autofocus: The Star Feature

Sony's Real-time Eye AF is genuinely incredible. It locks onto faces instantly and tracks them across the frame. For solo vlogging, this is a game-changer.

Test: Walking through Chatuchak Market (crowded, chaotic) — focus never lost my face.

✈️ Travel-Friendly Features

1. Compact Size

At 292g, it fits in a jacket pocket. I carried it every day for 90 days without fatigue.

2. Flip Screen

The 3" flip screen is bright enough for outdoor use. Touch controls are responsive. Perfect for framing selfies and vlogs.

3. Product Showcase Mode

Tap a button and the camera instantly focuses on objects you hold up. Great for:

  • Showing travel gear
  • Food reviews
  • Souvenir unboxings

4. Built-in ND Filter

The 3-stop ND filter lets you shoot at f/1.8 in bright sun. Essential for:

  • Beach videos
  • Snow scenes
  • Desert landscapes

5. Directional 3-Capsule Mic

The built-in mic is surprisingly good. For vlogging in quiet environments, you do not need an external mic.

⚠️ Wind Noise: Noticeable above 20mph. Use the included wind muff for outdoor shoots.

❌ What is Not Great

1. No 4K60

This is the biggest limitation. For slow-motion or fast action, you are stuck with 1080p120. The DJI Osmo Pocket 3 offers 4K120 for $380 less.

2. Battery Life

~45 minutes of 4K recording per battery. For a full day of shooting, you need:

  • 3-4 spare batteries ($25 each)
  • USB-C power bank for charging between locations

Battery anxiety is real with the ZV-1 II. Learn essential camera accessories every holiday videographer needs — including power banks, spare batteries, and charging solutions that keep you shooting all day.

3. No Headphone Jack

You cannot monitor audio while recording. This is frustrating for interviews or scripted content.

4. Fixed Lens

The 18-50mm lens is versatile but not replaceable. No zoom beyond 2.7x digital. For wildlife or distant landmarks, you need a different camera.

5. Price

At $898, it is expensive for a compact camera. The Canon PowerShot V10 at $429 offers 80% of the performance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Sony ZV-1 II worth it in 2026?

The Sony ZV-1 II is worth $898 in 2026 if you prioritize compact 4K vlogging with industry-leading autofocus. After 90 days of testing across Thailand, Bali, and Vietnam, the Real-time Eye AF proved genuinely incredible for solo vlogging, and the built-in ND filter and directional microphone eliminate the need for extra accessories. However, the lack of 4K60 recording and 45-minute battery life per charge are significant limitations. Consider waiting for the rumored ZV-1 III (late 2026) if 4K60 is essential, or buy the Canon PowerShot V10 at $429 if budget is tight.

Does the Sony ZV-1 II have 4K60 video?

No, the Sony ZV-1 II does not support 4K60 recording. It is limited to 4K30 and 1080p120 for slow-motion. This is the camera's biggest limitation for action and travel content creators who need higher frame rates. For 4K120 slow-motion in a compact body, the DJI Osmo Pocket 3 at $519 offers significantly better value. The rumored Sony ZV-1 III (expected late 2026) is anticipated to add 4K60, making it worth waiting if this feature is essential to your workflow.

How long does the Sony ZV-1 II battery last?

The Sony ZV-1 II battery lasts approximately 45 minutes of continuous 4K30 recording per NP-BX1 battery. For a full day of travel vlogging, you need 3-4 spare batteries ($25 each) or a USB-C power bank for charging between locations. The battery drains faster in hot climates (Thailand, Bali) and when using the flip screen at maximum brightness. Unlike the Canon PowerShot V10 (50 minutes) or DJI Osmo Pocket 3 (100 minutes), the ZV-1 II's battery life is below average for its class.

Is the Sony ZV-1 II good for low light and night vlogging?

The Sony ZV-1 II performs adequately in low light up to ISO 3200, producing usable footage with light noise reduction for YouTube. At ISO 1600, noise is minimal and colors remain natural. However, above ISO 6400, footage becomes noticeably grainy. Compared to the iPhone 16 Pro at ISO 3200, the ZV-1 II retains more detail but shows similar noise levels. The Canon R50 performs cleaner at ISO 3200 but costs $300 more. For night markets and sunset dinners, the ZV-1 II is sufficient, but dedicated low-light cameras like the Sony A7C II significantly outperform it.

Can you use external microphones with the Sony ZV-1 II?

Yes, the Sony ZV-1 II supports external microphones through its multi-interface shoe, which accepts Sony's proprietary microphones like the ECM-G1 shotgun mic without cables. However, the camera lacks a 3.5mm headphone jack, meaning you cannot monitor audio while recording. This is frustrating for interviews, scripted content, or situations where audio quality is critical. The built-in directional 3-capsule microphone is surprisingly good for vlogging in quiet environments, but wind noise becomes noticeable above 20mph without the included wind muff.
