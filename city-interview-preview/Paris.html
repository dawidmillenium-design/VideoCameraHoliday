Paris Filmmaker's Guide: Best Spots, Settings & Laws

Master the City of Light: How to film the Eiffel Tower legally, avoid Louvre bans, and capture perfect golden hour footage on the Seine.

📅 September 4, 2026⏱️ 8 min read

⚡ Quick Answer

Copyright Warning: You cannot commercially use footage of the Eiffel Tower at night (lighting is copyrighted). Best spots: Trocadéro (sunrise), Seine Banks (blue hour), Montmartre (street life). Drone: Strictly no-fly zone in central Paris. Gear: Fast prime lens (f/1.8) for dim museums and evening cafes.

Key Takeaways

  • Eiffel Tower Copyright: Daytime shots are free. Nighttime lighting design is copyrighted by SETE. Commercial use requires permission/fees.
  • Museum Bans: Louvre and Orsay strictly prohibit video recording in most galleries. Check signs at entry.
  • Drone No-Fly: Central Paris is a strict red zone. Flying near landmarks can lead to arrest and equipment confiscation.
  • Best Light: Golden hour on Haussmann buildings is magical. Blue hour on the Seine is essential.

Top 5 Filming Locations in Paris

1. Trocadéro Gardens

The classic wide shot of the Eiffel Tower. Arrive at sunrise (06:30 in summer) to have the plaza to yourself. The fountains add dynamic foreground interest.

Camera Settings: Wide angle (16-24mm). Aperture f/8 for sharpness across the scene. Use ND filter if shooting video at 24fps in bright morning sun.

2. Seine Riverbanks (UNESCO Site)

Perfect for walking shots, picnics, and blue hour timelapses. The bridges (Pont Alexandre III, Pont Neuf) offer incredible architectural details.

Tip: Film from the lower banks (Les Berges) for unique low angles. Watch out for high tide flooding in winter.

3. Montmartre & Sacré-Cœur

Cobblestone streets, artists' square (Place du Tertre), and the basilica. Great for handheld street videography and capturing bohemian atmosphere.

Warning: Scammers with "free" bracelets operate heavily here. Keep gear secured and don't engage.

4. Le Marais District

Narrow medieval streets, trendy shops, and Place des Vosges. Excellent for food vlogging (falafel, crepes) and fashion B-roll.

5. Sainte-Chapelle

Stained glass windows that create a kaleidoscope of light inside. A visual masterpiece.

Settings: High ISO (1600-3200) needed indoors. Fast aperture (f/1.8 or f/2.8). No tripods allowed.

Permits & Legal Warnings

⚠️ Critical Copyright Issue: The Eiffel Tower's nighttime lighting display is considered an artistic installation protected by copyright. Publishing night shots of the lit tower for commercial purposes (ads, paid client work, stock footage) without permission from SETE is illegal and can result in fines. Personal social media sharing is generally tolerated but technically restricted.

Commercial Filming: Requires permit from Paris Film Office. Difficult to obtain for small crews in busy areas. Louvre and Versailles strictly ban commercial filming without high-level authorization.

Drones: Paris is a "No Fly Zone" (PRA Paris). Drones are banned within the city limits. Exceptions require military and civil aviation approval (rarely granted for tourists).

Safety & Etiquette

Pickpockets: Extreme risk in Metro, at Eiffel Tower, and Sacré-Cœur. Use anti-theft bags. Never set camera down on cafe tables.

Police Checks: Police may ask to delete photos/videos if you film sensitive government buildings (Ministries, Embassies). Comply politely.

Metro Rules: Filming for personal use is allowed without flash/tripod. Commercial shoots require RATP permit. Be mindful of rush hour crowds (08:00-09:30, 17:30-19:30).

FAQ

Can I film the Eiffel Tower at night for YouTube?

For personal, non-monetized travel vlogs, it is usually tolerated. However, strictly speaking, the lighting is copyrighted. If you monetize the video or use it commercially, you risk a copyright claim or legal action. Best practice: Use daytime footage or blur the lights.

Are tripods allowed in public squares?

Small tripods are generally okay in parks (Trocadéro, Champ de Mars) if not obstructing traffic. Large tripods or setting up a "shoot" will attract police attention. Tripods are banned inside most museums and monuments.

Can I film inside the Louvre?

Photography is allowed in permanent collections (no flash), but video recording is strictly prohibited in most areas. Temporary exhibitions often ban all cameras. Always check the specific gallery rules.

Is it safe to film in the Metro?

Yes, for personal use. Keep gear compact. Avoid filming faces of strangers without permission (French privacy laws are strict). Rush hour is too crowded for good audio/video.

What about filming cafes and restaurants?

Filming the exterior is fine. Inside, always ask the manager first. French patrons value privacy; avoid filming other customers. Tipping the staff often helps secure permission.
