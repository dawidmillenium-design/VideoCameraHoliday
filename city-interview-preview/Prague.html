⚡ Quick Answer

Best spots: Charles Bridge (sunrise), Prague Castle, Old Town Square, Petřín Hill. Drone law: Strictly banned in the entire city center (UNESCO zone). Gear: Wide-angle for architecture, fast prime for low-light interiors.

Key Takeaways

  • Crowd Control: Charles Bridge is impassable by 10 AM. Shoot at 05:30–06:30 for empty shots.
  • Drone Ban: No flying within the UNESCO heritage zone (basically everywhere tourists go). Fines are heavy.
  • Castle Rules: Tripods require a special permit; handheld vlogging is usually tolerated in courtyards but not inside chapels.
  • Cobblestones: Use a gimbal or IBIS. The uneven streets make handheld shaky footage inevitable otherwise.

Top 5 Filming Locations in Prague

1. Charles Bridge (Karlův most)

The iconic symbol of Prague. Lined with baroque statues, it offers stunning views of the Vltava River and castle. It gets extremely crowded.

Camera Settings: Aperture f/8 for sharpness across the bridge. Use a polarizer to cut glare off the river. Shoot at sunrise (05:30 in summer) to avoid tour groups.

Pro Tip: Instead of filming ON the bridge, film FROM the bridge towards the Lesser Town (Malá Strana) for a unique perspective with fewer people in the frame.

2. Prague Castle & St. Vitus Cathedral

Largest ancient castle complex in the world. The Gothic spires of St. Vitus are breathtaking. The Golden Lane offers colorful, small-house aesthetics.

Warning: Security is strict. No large bags. Tripods often confiscated at entry without a permit.

3. Old Town Square & Astronomical Clock

Heart of the city. The clock's hourly show draws huge crowds. The square is surrounded by baroque and gothic buildings.

Strategy: Film the clock mechanism from the side alley to avoid the main crowd crush. Capture the square from the tower steps if accessible.

4. Petřín Hill & Lookout Tower

Green oasis overlooking the city. The lookout tower mimics the Eiffel Tower. Perfect for panoramic time-lapses of the cityscape.

Access: Funicular railway or a steep hike. Great for sunset shots looking back at the castle.

5. Lennon Wall & Kampa Island

Colorful graffiti wall dedicated to John Lennon. Kampa Island offers quiet riverside paths and modern art sculptures (giant crawling babies).

Permits & Filming Laws

⚠️ Critical: Drone Ban: Prague city center is a strict no-fly zone due to UNESCO status and proximity to government buildings. Flying here can lead to confiscation and fines up to €1,000+.

Commercial Permits: Required for any commercial shoot (ads, music videos, paid client work) in public spaces, especially the Castle and Old Town. Apply through the Prague Film Office weeks in advance. Personal travel vlogs (handheld, no lighting rigs) are generally ignored.

Tripod Rules: Tripods are often considered "commercial equipment" by guards at major sites (Castle, Charles Bridge towers). Be prepared to fold it up if asked.

Safety & Etiquette

Pickpockets: High risk on Tram 22, at the Astronomical Clock, and on Charles Bridge. Keep camera straps tight and bags zipped.

Scams: Beware of "free" bracelet scams near the bridge and aggressive money changers. Stick to ATMs inside banks.

Cobblestones: The streets are uneven and slippery when wet. Watch your step while framing shots. Protect gear from drops.

FAQ

Can I fly my drone in Prague?

No. The entire city center is a no-fly zone. Even outside the center, rules are strict. Do not risk it.

When is the best time to film Charles Bridge?

Sunrise (05:30–06:30 in summer). By 09:00 it is packed. Sunset is also crowded but offers nice backlight.

Are tripods allowed at Prague Castle?

Generally no, unless you have a specific commercial permit. Handheld gimbals are usually fine.

Can I film inside the Astronomical Clock?

You can film the exterior freely. Inside the Old Town Hall tower requires a ticket; video is usually allowed but no flash.

Is Prague safe for solo filmmakers?

Yes, very safe. Just watch out for pickpockets in tourist hotspots. Night filming in the center is safe and well-lit.
