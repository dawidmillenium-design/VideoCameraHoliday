New York City Filmmaker's Guide: Best Spots & Permit Laws

From Brooklyn Bridge sunrises to Times Square chaos—how to film NYC legally, safely, and cinematically without getting shut down.

📅 September 4, 2026⏱️ 8 min read

⚡ Quick Answer

Permits: Required for commercial shoots in parks and on bridges (NYC Mayor's Office). Subway: Personal filming OK with small gear; commercial requires MTA permit. Drone: Completely banned in all 5 boroughs without FAA/City waiver (nearly impossible for tourists). Gear: DJI Pocket 3 for crowds, Sony A7S III for low light.

Key Takeaways

  • Drone Ban: NYC has some of the strictest drone laws in the US. Do not fly in the city unless you have specific FAA waivers and NYPD approval.
  • Permit-Free Zones: Sidewalks (if not blocking traffic) and most indoor locations (with owner permission) don't need permits for small crews.
  • Subway Rules: No tripods, no lights, no extra cables. Handheld only for personal use. Commercial shoots require MTA permit ($2,000+).
  • Safety: Gear theft is real. Never leave bags unattended. Use discreet camera bags.

Top 5 Filming Locations in NYC

🎒 Gear Used in This City

Filming in New York requires lightweight, secure gear. Here is what I carried for this guide:

  • Tripod: A compact carbon fiber model (under 500g) to fit in my bag while navigating the Subway. See our Top 5 Lightweight Tripods →
  • Bag: An anti-theft carry-on backpack to protect gear in crowded areas like Times Square. See our Best Camera Bags Guide →

1. Brooklyn Bridge (Sunrise)

The iconic shot. Start on the Brooklyn side (DUMBO) and walk towards Manhattan at sunrise (06:00) to avoid crowds. The pedestrian path gets packed by 09:00.

Permit Note: Tripods require a permit from the NYC Mayor's Office. Handheld vlogging is generally tolerated if you keep moving.

Settings: f/8 for sharpness, ND filter for motion blur on the walk.

2. Central Park (Bethesda Terrace)

Classic movie location. Bethesda Fountain and the arcade offer great acoustics and light. Great for interviews or B-roll.

Permit Note: Commercial filming requires a permit from NYC Parks. Personal handheld use is fine.

3. Times Square (Night)

Neon overload. Best shot after midnight when crowds thin slightly. The sheer brightness of the billboards means you can shoot at low ISO even at night.

Safety: Watch out for costumed characters demanding tips. Don't engage if you don't want to pay.

4. DUMBO (Washington Street)

The famous "Empire State Building framed by brick buildings" shot. Very popular, so expect other photographers. Early morning is key.

5. The High Line

Elevated park with unique perspectives of the city and Hudson Yards. Good for walking shots. No tripods allowed.

Permits & Filming Laws

⚠️ Critical: Commercial Permits: Any filming for profit (including monetized YouTube channels, though enforcement varies) technically requires a permit from the NYC Mayor's Office of Media and Entertainment. Fines can be up to $1,000. Small handheld crews often fly under the radar, but tripods/lighting will get you stopped.

Subway (MTA) Rules: Personal photography/videography is allowed without a permit as long as you don't block traffic, use tripods, or use lights. Commercial shoots require an MTA permit which costs $2,000+ for a half day.

Drone Law: NYC Administrative Code §10-126 prohibits launching or landing drones in NYC without permission from the NYPD. Combined with FAA restrictions over populated areas, effectively a total ban for travelers.

Safety & Etiquette

Gear Security: NYC is generally safe, but opportunistic theft happens. Never set your bag down to frame a shot. Use a wrist strap. In crowded subways, wear your backpack on your front.

Privacy: New Yorkers are used to cameras, but don't film people aggressively close-up without permission. Street photography is legal in public spaces, but be respectful.

Tripod Aggression: If you set up a tripod without a permit in a busy spot, police or security will ask you to move. Don't argue; just pack up and move to a sidewalk spot.

FAQ

Can I fly my drone in Central Park?

No. Drones are banned in all NYC parks and effectively banned city-wide due to NYPD regulations and FAA airspace restrictions. Confiscation is a real risk.

Do I need a permit for a travel vlog?

Technically, if your channel is monetized, it's commercial. However, the city primarily targets large productions with tripods and lights. Small handheld vloggers are rarely stopped, but proceed at your own risk.

Is filming in the Subway allowed?

Yes, for personal use. You cannot use tripods, lights, or reflectors. You cannot block doors or aisles. Be prepared to move if an MTA worker asks.

What about filming inside shops?

Always ask permission. Private property owners can ban filming. Most small bodegas don't care if you're quick, but larger stores may say no.

Best time to film to avoid crowds?

05:30–07:30 AM is the golden window. Times Square and Brooklyn Bridge are surprisingly empty then. Midday (12:00–14:00) is the worst for crowds.
