⚡ Quick Answer

Best spots: Brooklyn Bridge (sunrise), Central Park (Bethesda Terrace), DUMBO (Washington St), The High Line (sunset). Drone law: Completely banned in all 5 boroughs without FAA/City waiver (nearly impossible). Gear: Compact mirrorless or DJI Pocket 3. Avoid large tripods on sidewalks.

Key Takeaways

  • Permits: Required for commercial shoots in parks (Central Park) and on bridges (Brooklyn Bridge). Personal handheld vlogging is usually tolerated.
  • Subway Rules: No tripods, no lights, no blocking doors. Personal filming OK with small gear. Commercial requires MTA permit.
  • Drone Ban: NYC has the strictest drone laws in the US. Do not fly unless you have specific written authorization.
  • Safety: Keep gear secured. Pickpockets target tourists with expensive cameras in crowded areas like Times Square.

Top 5 Filming Locations in NYC

1. Brooklyn Bridge

Iconic skyline views. Film from the pedestrian walkway. Sunrise (6 AM) is the only time you'll avoid crowds. Manhattan Bridge view from Washington Street in DUMBO is also classic.

Permit: Required for commercial shoots with tripods. Handheld personal use is generally fine.

2. Central Park (Bethesda Terrace)

The heart of the park. Great for timelapses and walking shots. The Bow Bridge and Great Lawn are also iconic.

Rule: Commercial filming requires a permit from NYC Parks. No drones allowed.

3. The High Line

Elevated park with unique city perspectives. Best filmed early morning or late evening. Sunset over the Hudson River is stunning.

4. Times Square

Neon chaos. Best at night for the full effect. Use a fast lens (f/1.8) and high ISO. Be prepared for aggressive street performers.

5. DUMBO (Brooklyn)

Cobblestone streets with Manhattan Bridge framing. Washington Street is the famous shot. Very crowded on weekends; go weekday mornings.

🎒 Gear Used in This City

Filming in New York requires lightweight, secure gear. You'll be walking 15,000+ steps a day and navigating crowded subways. Here is exactly what I carried for this guide:

  • Tripod: A compact carbon fiber model (under 500g) to fit in my bag while navigating the Subway. Large tripods attract unwanted attention and are banned in many spots.

Permits & Drone Laws

⚠️ Critical: Drone flight is banned in all 5 boroughs without explicit FAA and City permission. Fines up to $2,000. Commercial filming in Parks (Central, Bryant, etc.) requires a permit from NYC Parks Dept.

MTA Subway Rules: Personal filming with handheld cameras is allowed. No tripods, no lights, no blocking doors or platforms. Commercial shoots require an MTA permit ($150+).

Safety & Etiquette

Pickpockets: High risk in Times Square, on the 4/5/6 trains, and at major tourist attractions. Use a bag with lockable zippers. Never leave gear unattended.

Street Performers: In Times Square and Union Square, many costumed characters expect payment ($5-10) if you film them. Some are aggressive; just keep walking.

Privacy: New Yorkers are used to cameras, but don't film people in compromising situations. Public space filming is legal, but use common sense.

FAQ

Can I fly my drone in Central Park?

No. Drones are banned in all NYC parks and most of the city airspace. The only legal way is with specific FAA waivers and NYPD approval, which are rarely granted to tourists.

Do I need a permit to vlog in Times Square?

For handheld, personal vlogging, no permit is needed. If you set up a tripod, lights, or reflectors, you will be shut down by police without a commercial permit.

Is it safe to film at night?

Most tourist areas (Midtown, Brooklyn, DUMBO) are safe and well-lit. Avoid isolated parts of parks after dark. Stay aware of your surroundings.

Can I film inside the Subway?

Yes, for personal use. Keep the camera small, don't block doors, and no tripods. Flash photography is discouraged. Commercial shoots need an MTA permit.
