⚡ Quick Answer

Best spots: Piazzale Michelangelo (sunset), Duomo exterior (early morning), Ponte Vecchio (blue hour). Rules: No video inside Duomo climb or Uffizi Gallery. Tripods banned in crowded squares without permit. Gear: Wide-angle for architecture, fast prime for leather market low-light.

Key Takeaways

  • Museum Bans: Video recording strictly prohibited inside Uffizi Gallery and Accademia (David).
  • Duomo Access: Handheld video allowed in cathedral nave (no flash), but banned on the dome climb.
  • Cobblestone Challenge: Streets are uneven; use gimbal or IBIS for smooth walking shots.
  • Best Time: Sunrise (07:00) for empty piazzas; sunset from Piazzale Michelangelo for iconic city views.

Top 5 Filming Locations in Florence

1. Piazzale Michelangelo

The definitive panoramic view of Florence. Best for sunset time-lapses of the Duomo and Arno River. Crowded at golden hour, so arrive early to secure a tripod spot.

Camera Settings: ISO 100, f/8, 1/125 for daylight. For sunset time-lapse: Interval 2s, exposure 1/50.

2. The Duomo (Santa Maria del Fiore)

Film the intricate marble facade from Piazza del Duomo. The dome climb offers interior views but no video allowed on the stairs or inside the dome.

Tip: Film the exterior at sunrise when light hits the pink/green marble softly and crowds are minimal.

3. Ponte Vecchio

Medieval bridge lined with jewelry shops. Extremely crowded during day. Best filmed at blue hour when shop lights reflect on the Arno.

Warning: Selfie sticks and tripods are banned on the bridge itself due to congestion.

4. San Lorenzo Leather Market

Outdoor market near the basilica. Great for street life and texture shots. Low light under awnings requires fast lenses.

Ethics: Ask vendors before filming close-ups of their work. Some refuse cameras.

5. Boboli Gardens

Renaissance gardens behind Pitti Palace. Perfect for slow-motion walking shots among statues and hedges. Video allowed in gardens, not inside palace.

Permits & Filming Laws

⚠️ Critical: No video inside major museums (Uffizi, Accademia). Guards will enforce this strictly. Commercial filming in Piazza del Duomo requires a permit from the Opera del Duomo. Tripods generally tolerated if small and not obstructing traffic, but police can ask you to fold them.

Drone Law: Strictly no-fly zone over historic center (ZFU). Nearest legal takeoff is far outside city walls (requires ENAC permit).

Safety & Etiquette

Pickpockets: High risk around Duomo and Santa Maria Novella station. Keep camera strap around neck, not shoulder.

ZTL Zones: Historic center is a Limited Traffic Zone. Don't drive filming gear into it without permit; fines are automatic via camera.

Church Dress Code: Shoulders and knees must be covered to enter churches with cameras. Guards turn away tourists in shorts/sleeveless tops.

FAQ

Can I film inside the Duomo?

Handheld video without flash is allowed in the main nave. However, video is strictly banned on the dome climb, in the crypt, and inside the museum.

Are tripods allowed in piazzas?

Small tripods are usually tolerated if not obstructing foot traffic. Large rigs or commercial setups require a permit from the city council.

Can I fly a drone in Florence?

No. The entire historic center is a no-fly zone. Flying near the Duomo is illegal and dangerous. Permits are rarely granted for tourists.

Is the Leather Market safe for filming?

Yes, but always ask vendors before filming close-ups. Some may ask for payment or refuse. Keep gear secure against pickpockets in crowds.

Best time to avoid crowds?

Sunrise (07:00-08:00) is magical and empty. Midday (12:00-15:00) is packed with tour groups. August sees many locals away, but tourists remain high.
