Tokyo Filmmaker's Guide: Best Spots, Settings & Laws

From the neon chaos of Shibuya to the serene temples of Asakusa—how to film Japan's capital without breaking rules or offending locals.

⚡ Quick Answer

Best spots: Shibuya Crossing (early morning), Senso-ji Temple (blue hour), Shinjuku Gyoen (daytime). Drone law: Strictly banned in most of central Tokyo without special permission. Gear: DJI Pocket 3 for crowds, Sony ZV-E10 for low light.

Key Takeaways

  • Drone Bans: Almost all of central Tokyo is a no-fly zone. Permits are extremely difficult for tourists to obtain.
  • Temple Etiquette: Many temples ban photography inside main halls. Always check signs. Never use flash.
  • Crowd Management: Shibuya and Shinjuku are incredibly dense. Use compact gear and keep straps secure.
  • Best Time: Early morning (05:00-07:00) for empty streets. Blue hour (18:30-19:30) for neon lights.

Top 5 Filming Locations in Tokyo

1. Shibuya Crossing

The world's busiest pedestrian crossing. Best filmed from the Starbucks overlooking the intersection or from the Mag's Park rooftop.

Camera Settings: Shutter speed 1/100 to freeze motion while keeping some blur. Aperture f/5.6 for depth. ISO 800-1600 at night.

2. Senso-ji Temple (Asakusa)

Tokyo's oldest temple. The Kaminarimon Gate and Nakamise shopping street are iconic. Visit early morning or late evening to avoid crowds.

Tip: The main hall prohibits photography inside. Film the exterior and the five-story pagoda nearby.

3. Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden

Vast park blending Japanese, English, and French garden styles. Perfect for cherry blossoms (spring) or autumn colors. Tripods allowed in open areas.

4. Akihabara Electric Town

Neon-lit streets filled with anime shops, arcades, and electronics. Great for cyberpunk-style night shoots. Beware of no-photo zones in some stores.

5. teamLab Planets / Borderless

Immersive digital art museums. Photography allowed but no tripods or video lights. Best shot with high ISO capability cameras.

🎒 Gear Used in This City

Tokyo streets are narrow and crowded. Minimalist gear is essential to avoid blocking paths and offending locals.

  • Tripod: A tabletop or ultra-compact carbon fiber tripod. Full-size tripods are often prohibited in busy districts like Shinjuku without permits.

Permits & Drone Laws

⚠️ Critical: Drone flights are strictly banned in most of central Tokyo (including Shibuya, Shinjuku, Chiyoda) under Japanese aviation law. Fines up to ¥1,000,000. Special permits required from Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) and local police—rarely granted to tourists.

Commercial Filming: Requires permits for most public locations (parks, temples). Personal vlogging is generally tolerated if unobtrusive.

Safety & Etiquette

Respect Privacy: Japanese culture values privacy. Do not film locals close-up without permission. Blurring faces in post is recommended.

Temple Manners: Speak quietly. Remove hats. Follow "No Photography" signs strictly. Never point camera at people praying.

Crowd Safety: Rush hour trains are packed. Keep gear close. Pickpocketing is rare but possible in crowded tourist spots.

FAQ

Can I fly drones in Tokyo?

Almost entirely banned in central Tokyo. No-fly zones cover most wards. Permits are complex and rarely issued to tourists. Risk of heavy fines.

Is filming allowed in temples?

Exterior filming is usually fine. Interior photography often prohibited (check signs). Never use flash. Be respectful of worshippers.

How do I handle crowds at Shibuya?

Arrive before 07:00 for empty shots. Use compact gear. Shoot from elevated positions (Starbucks, rooftops) for safe overhead angles.

What about cherry blossom season?

Late March to early April. Parks are extremely crowded. Arrive at sunrise. Tripods may be restricted in peak bloom areas.

Is Tokyo safe for solo filmmakers?

Extremely safe. Low crime rate. Standard precautions apply. Locals are helpful if lost. Carry pocket Wi-Fi for navigation.
