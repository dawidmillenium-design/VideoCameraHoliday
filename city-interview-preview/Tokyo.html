Tokyo Filmmaker's Guide: Best Spots, Settings & Laws

From the neon chaos of Shibuya to the serene temples of Asakusa—how to film Japan's capital without breaking rules or offending locals.

📅 September 4, 2026⏱️ 7 min read

⚡ Quick Answer

Best spots: Shibuya Crossing (early morning), Senso-ji Temple (blue hour), Shinjuku Gyoen (daytime). Drone law: Strictly banned in most of central Tokyo without special permission. Gear: DJI Pocket 3 for crowds, Sony ZV-E10 for low light.

Key Takeaways

  • Drone Ban: Almost all of central Tokyo is a no-fly zone. Permits are extremely difficult for tourists to obtain.
  • Temple Etiquette: No tripods inside active temples. Ask permission before filming worshippers.
  • Crowd Management: Shibuya Crossing is impassable during peak hours. Film from elevated viewpoints (Starbucks Tsutaya).
  • Battery Life: Cold winters drain batteries fast. Keep spares warm in inner pockets.

Top 5 Filming Locations in Tokyo

1. Shibuya Crossing

The world's busiest pedestrian crossing. Best filmed from the Starbucks overlooking the intersection or from the Shibuya Sky observation deck.

Camera Settings: High shutter speed (1/100+) to freeze motion. Aperture f/5.6 for depth. ISO 400-800 for night neon.

2. Senso-ji Temple (Asakusa)

Tokyo's oldest temple. Vibrant red architecture and incense smoke create atmospheric shots. Nakamise shopping street leads up to it.

Tip: Arrive at 06:00 for empty grounds. Avoid weekends. No tripods inside main hall.

3. Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden

Massive park blending Japanese, English, and French garden styles. Cherry blossoms in spring, colorful foliage in autumn.

Rules: Tripods allowed in open areas, but not in greenhouses. No commercial shoots without permit.

4. Akihabara Electric Town

Neon-lit streets filled with anime shops, arcades, and maid cafes. Cyberpunk aesthetic perfect for night shoots.

Warning: Many shops prohibit filming inside. Ask before pointing cameras at maids or cosplayers.

5. teamLab Planets (Toyosu)

Immersive digital art museum. Barefoot walking through water and light installations. Visually stunning but crowded.

Settings: High ISO (3200+) for dark rooms. Fast lens (f/1.8) essential. No flash allowed.

Permits & Filming Laws

⚠️ Critical: Drones are banned in most of Tokyo (Category 2 & 3 airspace). Flying without permission can lead to arrest under Japanese aviation law. Fines up to ¥500,000.

Commercial Filming: Requires permit from local ward office. Personal vlogging is generally tolerated if unobtrusive. Some parks (Shinjuku Gyoen) require permits for tripods.

Privacy Laws: Strict privacy culture. Do not film individuals without consent, especially in residential areas or on trains.

Safety & Etiquette

Train Etiquette: No filming on trains during rush hour. Keep gear close. Speak quietly. Flash photography is forbidden.

Temple Respect: Remove hats. Bow slightly before entering. Never turn your back on the main shrine for a selfie. No smoking.

Crowds: Tokyo is safe, but pickpockets exist in crowded areas like Shibuya and Shinjuku. Use wrist straps for cameras.

FAQ

Can I fly a drone in Tokyo?

Almost certainly no. Central Tokyo is a no-fly zone. Permits are rarely granted to tourists. Violations carry heavy fines and potential jail time.

Is filming allowed inside temples?

Handheld video is usually OK in outer courtyards. Inner halls often ban video or require a fee (~¥500). Always check signs. No tripods.

Can I film on the subway?

Personal handheld filming is tolerated if you don't block doors or disturb passengers. No tripods, lights, or commercial setups without MTA permit.

What about filming in Akihabara?

Street filming is fine. Inside shops? Ask first. Many anime goods stores ban photography. Never film maids on the street without permission.

Best time to visit for filming?

Spring (March-April) for cherry blossoms. Autumn (November) for foliage. Winter (January-February) for clear skies but cold temps. Avoid Golden Week (late April/early May).
