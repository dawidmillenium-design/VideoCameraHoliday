Timelapse & Hyperlapse for Travel — Complete Guide (2026)

Timelapses and hyperlapses add cinematic value to travel videos. Here's how to do them right — from sunset clouds to starry nights.

⚡ Quick Verdict: The DJI Osmo Pocket 4 is the easiest camera for travel timelapses. For night skies, the Sony a7 V is unbeatable.

Timelapse vs. Hyperlapse — What's the Difference?

  • Timelapse: Camera stays still, time moves fast (sunset, clouds, stars)
  • Hyperlapse: Camera moves, time moves fast (walking through a city, drive through a landscape)

What You Need

  • Timelapse: Camera, sturdy tripod, intervalometer (or built-in feature)
  • Hyperlapse: Camera, gimbal, and patience (you move while shooting)

Timelapse Settings — Quick Reference

Clouds / Daytime

Interval: 2-5 seconds

Duration: 15-30 minutes

Aperture: f/8-f/11

ISO: 100-400

Sunset / Golden Hour

Interval: 5-10 seconds

Duration: 20-45 minutes

Aperture: f/5.6-f/8

ISO: 200-800

Night / Stars

Interval: 20-30 seconds

Duration: 1-3 hours

Aperture: f/1.8-f/2.8

ISO: 800-3200

Hyperlapse Technique

  1. Use a gimbal: Stabilization is essential
  2. Move in a straight line: Choose a direction and walk steadily
  3. Take a photo every 2-3 seconds: Use an intervalometer
  4. Shoot 5-10 minutes of walking: = 10-20 seconds of final footage
  5. Stabilize in post: Use After Effects (Warp Stabilizer) or Premiere Pro

Pro Tips for Timelapses

  • Battery life: Bring spare batteries or a power bank — timelapses drain batteries
  • Memory cards: Use large cards — each timelapse can be 100-500 photos
  • Start early: For sunsets, start 30 minutes before golden hour
  • Use exposure bracketing: For changing light (sunset), use AEB to avoid overexposure
Pro Tip: "For timelapses, always shoot in manual mode with fixed ISO, aperture, and shutter speed. If you're shooting sunset, use a slower shutter speed as light fades."
