Filming in Rain & Bad Weather — How to Protect Your Gear and Get Great Shots

Rain doesn't have to ruin your shoot. Here's how to film in bad weather — from protecting your gear to capturing beautiful, atmospheric footage.

⚡ Quick Verdict: Embrace the rain — it adds atmosphere and drama. Just protect your gear with a cover, and you're set.

Why Bad Weather Footage Is Often the Best

  • Atmosphere: Rain, mist, and storms add mood and drama
  • Lighting: Overcast skies create soft, even light — no harsh shadows
  • Uniqueness: Most tourists don't shoot in bad weather — your footage stands out

Protecting Your Gear in Rain

Rain Cover

Silicone or plastic cover designed for your camera model. DJI Osmo Pocket rain cover is excellent. For mirrorless, consider Op/Tech USA covers.

Umbrella

A clear umbrella is both protection AND a prop. It creates beautiful rain patterns and protects your gear.

Plastic Bag

Cheap but effective. Cut a hole for the lens, secure with rubber bands. Emergency solution when nothing else is available.

Waterproof Case

Essential for action cameras. GoPro and DJI Action cameras are waterproof without cases — but check depth ratings.

Filming Techniques in Rain

  • Embrace the rain: It adds atmosphere — don't fight it
  • Slow shutter speed: Captures raindrops as streaks — adds cinematic feel
  • Rain on umbrellas: Good audio — capture the pitter-patter
  • Shoot from cover: Doorways, awnings, car windows — protect your gear
  • Use reflections: Puddles create beautiful mirror reflections

Audio Tips for Rainy Filming

  • Use a deadcat: Essential for reducing wind noise in rain
  • Protect your mic: Use a waterproof cover or keep it under an umbrella
  • Embrace rain sounds: Rain audio can be beautiful — don't over-process it
