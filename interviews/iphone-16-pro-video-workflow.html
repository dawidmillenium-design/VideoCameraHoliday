iPhone 16 Pro Video Workflow: Shooting ProRes & Log for Travel

Unlock the cinematic potential of your iPhone 16 Pro. Learn how to shoot Apple Log, use ProRes, and apply LUTs for professional travel videos.

In Short

  • The iPhone 16 Pro can shoot cinema-grade footage with Apple Log and ProRes — rivaling dedicated cameras.
  • Apple Log preserves maximum dynamic range for color grading in post-production.
  • ProRes files are massive (up to 6GB/minute) — you need an external SSD for extended shoots.
  • The Blackmagic Camera app unlocks manual controls that the native app hides.
  • Apply LUTs in CapCut, Premiere Pro, or DaVinci Resolve to transform flat Log footage into cinematic video.

Most travelers don't realize that the iPhone 16 Pro in their pocket is capable of shooting professional-grade video that rivals dedicated cameras costing thousands of dollars. With Apple Log, ProRes recording, and advanced computational photography, the iPhone 16 Pro is a legitimate tool for travel creators.

But to unlock its full potential, you need to go beyond the default settings. This guide walks you through the exact workflow professional travel vloggers use to shoot cinematic footage on iPhone.

Essential Camera Settings

Before diving into Log and ProRes, optimize your basic settings:

  • Resolution: 4K at 24fps (cinematic) or 30fps (standard). Avoid 60fps unless you need slow motion.
  • HDR Video: Turn OFF. HDR footage looks blown out on most platforms and is difficult to grade.
  • Grid: Turn ON for better composition.
  • Mirror Front Camera: Turn ON for natural-looking selfie videos.

Shooting Apple Log

Apple Log is a flat color profile that preserves maximum dynamic range. It looks gray and washed out straight out of the camera, but it gives you maximum flexibility in post-production.

Step 1: Enable Apple Log

Go to Settings > Camera > Formats and enable ProRes and Apple Log. Note: You need an external SSD connected via USB-C to shoot ProRes Log at 4K/60fps or higher.

Step 2: Expose Correctly

Log footage is unforgiving of bad exposure. Use the exposure slider to ensure your highlights are not clipped (no pure white areas). Slightly overexpose (by +0.3 to +0.7 stops) for cleaner shadows.

Step 3: Shoot a Reference

Before you start filming, shoot a 5-second clip of a color checker chart or a neutral gray card. This helps you match colors accurately in post.

ProRes: When to Use It

ProRes is Apple's professional video codec. It preserves maximum quality but creates massive files. Here's when to use it:

  • Use ProRes for: Important travel moments, cinematic B-roll, footage you plan to color grade heavily, or client work.
  • Skip ProRes for: Casual social media posts, quick clips, or when storage is limited.

File Sizes: ProRes 422 HQ at 4K/30fps generates about 6GB per minute. A 1-hour shoot = 360GB. You absolutely need an external SSD (like the Samsung T7 Shield) for extended ProRes shoots.

Applying LUTs in Post

Log footage needs color grading to look cinematic. LUTs (Look-Up Tables) apply pre-made color grades in one click.

Step 1: Import to Editing App

Transfer your ProRes Log files to CapCut, Premiere Pro, or DaVinci Resolve. If you shot to an external SSD, you can edit directly from it.

Step 2: Apply a Conversion LUT

First, apply Apple's official "Apple Log to Rec.709" conversion LUT. This transforms the flat Log footage into standard color space. You can download this LUT for free from Apple's website.

Step 3: Apply a Creative LUT

Now apply a creative LUT to give your footage a specific look (cinematic, vintage, warm, cool, etc.). Popular LUT packs for travel include FilmConvert, MotionVFX, and Fresh LUTs.

Step 4: Fine-Tune

Adjust exposure, contrast, and white balance to match your vision. LUTs are starting points — tweak them to fit your specific footage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an external SSD to shoot ProRes?

For ProRes at 4K/30fps or lower, you can record to the iPhone's internal storage (if you have a 256GB+ model). For 4K/60fps or higher, you must use an external SSD connected via USB-C. The Samsung T7 Shield (1TB or 2TB) is the most popular choice among creators.

Is Apple Log worth the extra effort?

If you're posting casual social media clips, no — the default HDR mode looks great. But if you want cinematic, professional-grade footage that you can color grade to match your brand, Apple Log is absolutely worth it. It gives you the same flexibility as shooting Log on a $5,000 cinema camera.

What's the best editing app for iPhone Log footage?

For mobile editing, CapCut and LumaFusion are the best options. For desktop editing, DaVinci Resolve (free version) is the industry standard for color grading. Premiere Pro and Final Cut Pro also work excellently.
