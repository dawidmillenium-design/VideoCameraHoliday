The Insta360 X5 World Tour: 20 Cities, 5 Continents, 1 Camera

From a Bangkok earthquake to the police at the Macao border — Dawid Millennium tells us everything that happened when one 360 camera met 20 cities across the globe.

Editor's Note

When Dawid Millennium returned from his round-the-world filming trip, his memory cards held giraffes in Nairobi, cherry blossoms in Tokyo, thermal warnings from Rio and Dubai, and a cloud upload estimate of 999 hours. His companion for all 20 cities: an Insta360 X5 bought in Bangkok two days after an earthquake. We sat down with him for the complete, unfiltered story.

🎬 Real field footage: Insta360 X5 in Huacachina, Peru — shot by Dawid Millennium

Part 1: The Earthquake & The Decision

Q

Let's start at the beginning. You bought the Insta360 X5 in Bangkok — right after an earthquake. What happened?

DMDawid Millennium

🇹🇭 Bangkok

It sounds like the opening of a movie, but it's true. The earthquake shook Bangkok, and two days later I walked into a camera shop and bought the X5. Feeling the ground move makes you realize how fast everything can change. I thought: I want to record everything. Not just one trip — the whole world, every angle, no misses. That's the beauty of a 360 camera. So I made the decision right there: this isn't a holiday, it's a 20-city global video project.

Q

You shot the entire trip in PURE VIDEO mode. What exactly is that?

DMDawid Millennium

PURE VIDEO is the X5's super high-quality lighting and shooting option — its cinema mode. Maximum dynamic range, minimum noise, the cleanest possible 360 footage. But here's the trade-off, and it became the theme of the whole trip: bigger files, faster battery drain, and a lot more heat. Almost every problem I had traces back to that.

Part 2: Africa & The Internet Problem

Q

First stop: Nairobi, Kenya. You filmed at the Giraffe Center. How did the camera perform?

DMDawid Millennium

🇰🇪 Nairobi

Camera-wise? Perfect. Around 24°C. The Giraffe Center sits next to Nairobi National Park — a UNESCO-recognized wildlife area on the edge of the capital — and the 360 format was born for that place. Giraffes approaching from every direction, and I never had to turn the camera. But the internet was impossible. I tried uploading to Insta360 Cloud and the progress bar barely moved. I waited 45 minutes and gave up. In East Africa, the cloud doesn't exist for 360 files. You need a portable SSD, full stop.

Part 3: Latin America, Internet & The 999-Hour Upload

Q

Then Latin America. We've heard rumors of a "999 hours" upload. Please tell us this is not real.

DMDawid Millennium

🇧🇷 Rio de Janeiro🇵🇪 Lima🇨🇴 Bogotá🇲🇽 Mexico City

(laughs) It's real. Rio's internet was better than Nairobi's, but the heat was brutal — 38 degrees. About 30 minutes into a session near Copacabana, the camera flashed a temperature warning and switched itself off. Then, uploading the remaining files, the app showed 999 hours. It's not a bug — it's the app saying "this file is too big for this connection."

Things improved fast after that. Lima and Bogotá had solid internet — 20 to 40 minutes per file on hotel WiFi. And Mexico City was a pleasant surprise: the high altitude keeps it mild around 22°C, so the camera never overheated and I could record for ages. Carlos from my City Through the Lens series there once told me "your first 100 videos will be bad" — standing in the Zócalo with the X5, I finally felt like they weren't.

Q

Was there a standout city for upload speeds in that region?

DMDawid Millennium

🇨🇷 San José

San José, Costa Rica — the surprise winner of that leg. I pushed a full 360 video to the cloud in under 10 minutes. The capital's fiber infrastructure is seriously impressive. If you're a traveling creator who depends on cloud backup, San José is your best friend.

Part 4: The Heat Kills Cameras

Q

Dubai. 40 degrees. And the Maldives. What happened?

DMDawid Millennium

🇦🇪 Dubai🇲🇻 Maldives

Forget using the X5 outdoors in Dubai during the day. The desert hit 40 Celsius and the camera switched off within 15 minutes. Here's the irony: Dubai had some of the fastest internet of the entire trip — uploads that took 40 minutes elsewhere finished in under 5. But what's the point of the world's fastest upload when the camera can't survive long enough to record?

The Maldives was paradise for your eyes, hell for your camera. Equatorial sun, high humidity, white reflective sand — it overheated in about 10 minutes. I filmed in 8-minute bursts, cooled it in the shade, then went again.

Q

Did the heat follow you to Marrakech and Singapore?

DMDawid Millennium

🇲🇦 Marrakech🇸🇬 Singapore

Marrakech was hot but survivable — around 35°C, so I could manage short bursts in the medina, though the dust got into every crevice. Amara from my City Through the Lens episode there, a food-tourism specialist, warned me: "Film at dawn, or don't film."

Singapore is the strangest case of the whole trip. It had the fastest internet I measured — even faster than Dubai. I uploaded everything in minutes. But it's hot AND humid, so the X5 still overheated. Best upload city on Earth, and I could barely record. The universe has a sense of humor.

Singapore had the fastest internet of the entire trip — and I could barely record. The universe has a sense of humor.

Part 5: The Good Zones — Record For Hours

Q

Was there anywhere you could simply record for hours without the camera dying?

DMDawid Millennium

🇮🇩 Bali🇻🇳 Hanoi🇯🇵 Tokyo🇵🇹 Lisbon

Four cities earned the title. Bali and Hanoi: cloud cover, shade, moderate temperatures — I recorded 30 to 45 minute sessions without a single shutdown.

Tokyo was excellent too. Cherry blossom season, mild spring air, and Yuki — from my City Through the Lens episode there — showed me the quiet streets where I could set up a long 360 capture without crowds. The camera loved it, and so did I.

And Lisbon was the sleeper hit. The Atlantic keeps it mild, the light is gorgeous, and Marco — who shot his first paid gig on a phone there — let me film the miradouros at golden hour. Forty-minute sessions, no shutdowns, perfect footage. If your project needs long-form 360 recording, these four cities are the answer.

Part 6: Cold, Rain & The Cameras That Loved It

Q

After all that heat, how did the X5 handle cold and rain in London and Reykjavik?

DMDawid Millennium

🇬🇧 London🇮🇸 Reykjavik

Like a dream, honestly. Low temperatures are far easier on this camera than heat. In London the drizzle never caused an overheating shutdown — the enemy there is the rain itself, so I kept a rain cover on and wiped the lens between takes. The X5 never once got hot.

Reykjavik was the coldest stop of the whole tour, and the camera never overheated — obviously. The only issue is that cold drains batteries about 30% faster, so I carried spares in my jacket, close to my body. It's the same lesson from my Iceland guide: cold is manageable, heat is the killer. If you're heading somewhere chilly, the X5 is a genuinely reliable companion.

Part 7: The Forbidden Zones & Legal Grey Areas

Q

Now the serious part. You mentioned legal trouble at the Macao–Zhuhai border and in Da Nang.

DMDawid Millennium

🇨🇳 Macao–Zhuhai🇻🇳 Da Nang

This is the part people need to hear. At the Chinese border crossing between Macao and Zhuhai, 360 cameras are effectively forbidden. The police will not let you use one — even for a moment. The penalty is a heavy fine taken straight from your bank account, or confiscation of the camera. Treated like a drone.

And in Da Nang, Vietnam, drones are banned outright — I watched them get confiscated on the beach. A 360 camera on a selfie stick attracts the same suspicion. My rule now: research local filming laws before you fly, not after you land.

Q

And Istanbul — was that a problem too?

DMDawid Millennium

🇹🇷 Istanbul

Istanbul is a grey area rather than a hard ban. The climate was fine and the camera behaved perfectly. But at the major historic sites — Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, inside the cisterns — tripod and 360 filming requires a permit or is restricted. Security will stop you if you set up without permission. I filmed handheld in the open squares and used a local guide for interior shots. The lesson: it's not illegal, but you need permission in the right places. Do your homework.

Part 8: The Verdict

Q

Final question. After 20 cities and 5 continents — what's your score for the Insta360 X5?

DMDawid Millennium

7.2 out of 10. Image quality in PURE VIDEO: 9 out of 10 when conditions are right. Heat management: 5. Cloud ecosystem in slow-internet regions: 4. It's a brilliant camera when the world cooperates — and the world often doesn't cooperate.

My advice after 20 cities: know your destination before you pack it. Film in the right conditions and it's magic. Push it into 40-degree deserts, past border police who treat it like a drone, or into humid megacities, and it becomes an expensive paperweight. The camera never lied to me. The spec sheet just left out the parts about heat, internet, and laws.

⚡ Quick Fire Round

Best location for the X5?
Bali. Tokyo a very close second.
Worst location?
Dubai at noon. The camera lasted 15 minutes. I lasted longer.
Fastest internet?
Singapore. And it was almost useless because of the heat.
Coldest stop?
Reykjavik. The camera never overheated once.
One accessory you'd add?
A portable SSD. The cloud is a myth in half the world.
Would you do the tour again?
Tomorrow. But I'd film at dawn, upload at night, and check every embassy website first.
