DJI Osmo Pocket 4P Deep Dive: Is the Dual-Camera System Worth It?

DJI released the Pocket 4P with a dual-camera system. We tested it on holiday to see if the extra lens is worth the upgrade over the standard Pocket 4.

In Short

  • The dual-camera system (wide + ultrawide) eliminates the need to carry separate lenses or adapters.
  • Image quality matches the standard Pocket 4, but the framing flexibility is a game-changer for solo vloggers.
  • Battery life remains excellent at ~110 minutes, though the dual-sensor array generates slightly more heat.
  • If you primarily shoot vertical content for social media, the standard Pocket 4 is still the better value.

DJI’s Pocket line has dominated the travel vlogging space for years, but the introduction of the Osmo Pocket 4P marks a significant shift. By integrating a dual-camera system into the same pocketable form factor, DJI is directly addressing the biggest limitation of the original Pocket design: fixed focal length. We took the 4P on a two-week European city break to see if the extra lens justifies the price premium.

What's New in the 4P?

The Pocket 4P retains the beloved 1-inch sensor and 3-axis mechanical gimbal of its predecessor but adds a secondary 120° ultrawide lens. This allows creators to switch between a natural 24mm equivalent for standard vlogging and an immersive 14mm equivalent for tight spaces, landscapes, or dynamic "follow-me" shots without changing hardware. For more context on older generations, check our DJI Osmo Pocket 3 Review.

Real Test: The Second Lens — When Do You Actually Use It?

During our trip through Prague and Vienna, the ultrawide lens proved invaluable in two specific scenarios: cramped cafe interiors and sweeping architectural shots. When filming a "walk and talk" through narrow cobblestone streets, the 14mm equivalent allowed us to capture both the subject and the surrounding environment without having to hold the camera at an awkward arm's length. However, for 80% of standard talking-head vlogs, the primary 24mm lens remains the go-to choice due to its more natural perspective and slightly better low-light performance.

Image Quality Comparison

In good lighting, the image quality between the standard Pocket 4 and the 4P is virtually indistinguishable. Both utilize the same 1-inch sensor technology, delivering excellent dynamic range and sharp 4K/60fps footage. However, in low-light scenarios (like evening street markets), the primary lens on the 4P maintains a slight edge over the ultrawide, which introduces minor noise at ISO 3200 and above. For most travel creators, this trade-off is entirely acceptable.

Stabilisation & Gimbal Performance

The 3-axis gimbal on the 4P is a masterpiece of engineering. Despite the addition of a second lens module, the stabilization remains buttery smooth. We tested it while walking briskly, riding bicycles, and even on a bumpy tram ride. The gimbal effortlessly corrected for pitch, roll, and yaw, producing footage that looked like it was shot on a professional Steadicam. If you are looking for tips on achieving similar movement without hardware, read our guide on how to Stabilize Videos Without a Gimbal.

Audio & Vlogging Features

Audio quality is solid, with improved wind noise reduction compared to previous generations. However, for serious vloggers, pairing the 4P with the DJI Mic 2 is still highly recommended. The 4P also features a fully articulating touchscreen, making it easy to frame shots from any angle, and supports direct vertical video recording, which is a massive time-saver for TikTok and Reels creators.

Battery Life & Charging

Battery life is impressive, clocking in at around 110 minutes of continuous recording. This is slightly less than the standard Pocket 4 due to the power draw of the dual-sensor array, but the difference is negligible in real-world use. The camera supports fast charging via USB-C, reaching 80% in just 30 minutes, which is perfect for quick top-ups between locations.

Who Should Buy the 4P?

The DJI Osmo Pocket 4P is the ideal choice for solo travel vloggers who frequently shoot in tight spaces or want the flexibility of an ultrawide lens without carrying extra gear. If you primarily shoot standard talking-head videos or vertical content for social media, the standard Pocket 4 offers better value. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the ultrawide lens for vertical video?

Yes, both lenses support native vertical video recording. The ultrawide is particularly effective for vertical content as it captures more of the background, making it ideal for "day in the life" style Reels and TikToks.

Does the dual-camera system affect the size of the camera?

Not significantly. The Pocket 4P is only marginally larger and heavier than the standard Pocket 4. It still fits comfortably in a jacket pocket or small bag, maintaining the core appeal of the Pocket line.

Is the Pocket 4P waterproof?

No, the Pocket 4P is not waterproof. While it can handle light rain, it should not be submerged. For underwater filming, you will need to purchase a dedicated waterproof case, which is sold separately.
