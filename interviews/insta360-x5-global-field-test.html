Insta360 X5 Global Field Test: 12 Countries, 3 Continents, 1 Camera

I bought it in Bangkok after an earthquake. Then I filmed across Africa, South America, Asia, and the Middle East. Here's what actually happened — the heat shutdowns, the 999-hour uploads, and the police bans.

By Dawid Millennium | Published August 29, 2026 | 14 min read
Camera Reviews

I bought my Insta360 X5 in Bangkok, Thailand, two days after an earthquake shook the city. Something about that moment — the ground moving, the uncertainty, the feeling that anything could change in an instant — made me decide: I'm going to record everything. Not just one trip. The whole world. 360 degrees. No angles missed.

So I packed the X5, enabled the PURE VIDEO lighting mode for maximum quality, and set off on a global filming journey across 12 countries and 3 continents. Africa. South America. Asia. The Middle East.

This is not a spec-sheet review. This is what actually happened when one 360 camera met the real world: slow internet, 40°C desert heat, police bans, and a cloud upload that told me to wait 999 hours.

What Is PURE VIDEO Mode?

💡 PURE VIDEO: The Lighting Option That Changes Everything

The Insta360 X5 features a shooting mode called PURE VIDEO — a super high-quality lighting option designed to maximize dynamic range, reduce noise, and produce the cleanest possible 360 footage in challenging conditions.

Think of it as the camera's "cinema mode." It prioritizes image quality over battery life and file size. In bright conditions, it produces stunning color accuracy and detail. In low light, it pushes the sensor to its limits.

The trade-off: PURE VIDEO generates larger files, drains battery faster, and produces more heat. This became very relevant later in this trip.

I shot the entire global journey in PURE VIDEO mode. Every country. Every location. Maximum quality. This is what happened.

🇰🇪 Nairobi, Kenya — Giraffe Center

📍 Nairobi, Kenya

📷 Camera: Excellent 🌐 Upload: Impossible ⚖️ Legal: No issues

Location: Giraffe Center, next to Nairobi National Park — a UNESCO-recognized wildlife area on the edge of the capital.

Camera performance: The Insta360 X5 worked perfectly here. Temperature was around 24°C, mild and comfortable. PURE VIDEO mode captured the giraffes in stunning detail — you could see every spot, every eyelash. The 360 perspective meant I captured the animals approaching from all directions without ever turning the camera.

The problem: Internet. I tried to upload footage to Insta360 Cloud after the session. The connection was so slow that the upload progress bar barely moved. After 45 minutes of waiting, I gave up and kept everything on the microSD card. No cloud backup in Kenya.

💡 Kenya Verdict

Camera: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (perfect performance)
Upload: ⭐ (impossible — bring local storage)
Tip: Carry a portable SSD for backup. Don't rely on cloud in East Africa.

🇧🇷 Rio de Janeiro, Brazil — Heat Shutdown

📍 Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

📷 Camera: Overheated 🌐 Upload: Better ⚖️ Legal: No issues

Camera performance: Better internet than Kenya — upload speeds were noticeably faster and I could actually push footage to the cloud. BUT: the heat was brutal. Rio in peak sun hit 38°C+, and the Insta360 X5 in PURE VIDEO mode simply switched itself off automatically.

The shutdown: I was filming near Copacabana, maybe 30 minutes into a session. The camera got hot to the touch, displayed a temperature warning, and powered off. No saving, no warning beyond a 3-second message. Just... off.

The cloud upload issue: When I tried uploading to Insta360 Cloud later, the estimated time showed 999 hours. Not a typo. Nine hundred and ninety-nine hours. The file was too large for the connection speed, and the app's algorithm just gave up calculating a realistic time.

⚠️ The 999-Hour Upload

If you see "999 hours" on Insta360 Cloud, it means: the file is too large for your current connection. Solutions: (1) Use WiFi, not mobile data. (2) Upload at night when networks are less congested. (3) Split files into smaller clips before uploading. (4) Use a local NAS or external drive instead.

🇵🇪 Peru & 🇨🇴 Colombia — Internet Improves

📍 Peru & Colombia

📷 Camera: Good 🌐 Upload: Fast ⚖️ Legal: No issues

Camera performance: Temperatures were manageable (20-28°C in the highlands, warmer on the coast). The X5 handled both countries without overheating. PURE VIDEO mode worked as intended.

Internet: Significantly better than Kenya or Brazil. In Lima, Medellín, and Bogotá, I could upload 360 files to Insta360 Cloud within 20-40 minutes on hotel WiFi. Not instant, but workable. Mobile data was also surprisingly fast in urban areas.

🇨🇷 San José, Costa Rica — The Upload Paradise

📍 San José, Costa Rica

📷 Camera: Good 🌐 Upload: Very Fast ⚖️ Legal: No issues

Internet: San José was the surprise winner of this entire trip. Upload speeds were genuinely fast — I pushed a full 360 video file to Insta360 Cloud in under 10 minutes. The capital's fiber infrastructure is impressive.

Camera: Worked well. Humidity was high but temperature stayed around 26°C. No overheating issues. PURE VIDEO performed cleanly.

✅ Costa Rica Verdict

Best upload speeds of the entire trip. If you need to back up 360 footage to cloud while traveling, San José is your friend. Fast WiFi in most hotels and cafes.

🇦🇪 Dubai, UAE — Desert Heat Kills Cameras

📍 Dubai, UAE

📷 Camera: Shut down (40°C) 🌐 Upload: Record fast ⚖️ Legal: Be careful

Camera performance: Forget using the Insta360 X5 outdoors in Dubai during the day. The desert heat hit 40°C+ and the camera switched itself off within 15 minutes. PURE VIDEO mode generates extra heat, which made it worse. The camera's thermal protection kicked in and it simply died mid-recording.

Upload speed: Ironically, Dubai had the fastest internet of the entire trip. Upload was record-fast — files that took 40 minutes in Colombia uploaded in under 5 minutes. The infrastructure is world-class. But what's the point of fast upload if the camera can't record?

Legal note: Be mindful of filming locations in Dubai. Some areas (government buildings, military sites, certain residential areas) restrict or ban filming. Check before you shoot.

⚠️ Dubai Survival Tips

Only film early morning (5:30-8:00 AM) or after sunset. Midday is camera death. Keep the camera in shade between takes. Bring a small portable fan. If the camera feels hot to the touch, stop immediately — don't wait for the auto-shutdown.

🇲🇻 Maldives — 10-Minute Limit

📍 Maldives

📷 Camera: 10-min limit 🌐 Upload: Resort WiFi ⚖️ Legal: No issues

Camera performance: The Maldives is paradise for your eyes but hell for your camera. The combination of direct equatorial sun, high humidity, and reflective white sand meant the Insta360 X5 overheated within 10 minutes of recording in PURE VIDEO mode.

The workaround: I had to film in 8-minute bursts, then let the camera cool down in shade for 15 minutes, then film again. It's doable, but you can't just hit record and walk around for an hour. Plan your shots, film short takes, cool down, repeat.

🇮🇩 Bali, Indonesia & 🇻🇳 Hanoi, Vietnam — The Good Zones

📍 Bali, Indonesia & Hanoi, Vietnam

📷 Camera: Excellent for long recording 🌐 Upload: Decent ⚖️ Legal: Mostly fine

Camera performance: Finally, places where I could just hit record and film for extended periods. Bali's cloud cover and lower temperatures (compared to direct equatorial sun) meant the X5 could record 30-45 minute sessions without overheating. Hanoi was similar — the urban environment provided shade, and temperatures were manageable.

These are the best locations for long-form 360 recording. If you're planning a project that requires extended continuous recording, these cities are ideal.

✅ Bali & Hanoi Verdict

Best locations for extended Insta360 X5 recording. Moderate temperatures, cloud cover, and shade mean you can film for 30+ minutes without thermal shutdown. Ideal for timelapses, long walkthroughs, and ambient 360 captures.

🇨🇳 Macao/Zhuhai Border & 🇻🇳 Da Nang — FORBIDDEN ZONES

📍 Macao-Zhuhai Border, China & Da Nang, Vietnam

📷 Camera: FORBIDDEN ⚖️ Legal: Police enforcement

Macao-Zhuhai border: This is critical. At the Chinese border crossing between Macao and Zhuhai, 360 cameras are effectively forbidden. The police and border authorities do not allow you to use them — even briefly. The penalty is either a heavy fine directly from your bank account or confiscation of the camera.

Da Nang, Vietnam: Similarly, drone usage is forbidden in Da Nang. While the Insta360 X5 isn't technically a drone, the 360 camera on a selfie stick can attract similar suspicion from authorities. I saw drones confiscated at the beach. Be extremely careful.

🚨 CRITICAL LEGAL WARNING

Macao-Zhuhai border: DO NOT use 360 cameras. Police enforce strictly. Fine or confiscation.
Da Nang, Vietnam: Drones are banned. 360 cameras on sticks may attract attention.
General rule: Research local filming laws BEFORE arriving. In some countries, 360 cameras are treated similarly to drones and require permits or are banned entirely. Check embassy websites and local forums before you travel.

Final Verdict: The Insta360 X5 Around the World

Action Camera Overheat Fix: Summer Thermal Management

After the X5 shut down repeatedly in Rio, Dubai, and the Maldives, I developed a thermal management protocol that extended recording time by 2-3x in extreme heat. These techniques work for any action camera (GoPro Hero 13, DJI Action 5 Pro, Insta360 Ace Pro 2):

Thermal Management Box: Stop Your Action Camera from Overheating
  1. Shade is your friend: Never leave the camera in direct sun between takes. Even 2 minutes on hot sand or a car dashboard can push internal temps past the shutdown threshold. Store in a shaded pocket or wrapped in a light-colored cloth.
  2. Reduce resolution/frame rate: 8K PURE VIDEO generates maximum heat. Switch to 5.7K or 4K for longer sessions. Lower frame rates (30fps vs 60fps) also reduce processor load and heat generation.
  3. Remove the battery between takes: A warm battery inside a sealed camera body creates a heat trap. Pop it out during breaks to let both the battery and camera internals cool.
  4. Use external cooling: In extreme conditions (35°C+), wrap the camera body in a damp microfiber cloth. Evaporative cooling can drop surface temperature by 5-8°C. For GoPros, small clip-on heatsinks are available online.
  5. Avoid charging while recording: This compounds heat from both the processor and battery charging circuit. Charge during cool-down periods, not during active use.
  6. Record in bursts: Instead of one 30-minute continuous take, do three 8-minute takes with 2-minute cool-down breaks. Total footage is similar, but the camera never reaches critical temperature.
  7. Turn off unnecessary features: GPS, WiFi streaming to phone, and image stabilization all generate extra heat. Disable what you don't need for the shot.
Warning: Never put a hot camera directly into a refrigerator or freezer. Rapid temperature change causes condensation inside the lens and electronics, which can cause permanent damage. Let it cool gradually in shade first.

Who Should Buy the Insta360 X5?

  • ✅ Travelers filming in moderate climates (20-30°C)
  • ✅ Creators who want 360 perspectives for immersive content
  • ✅ Filmmakers with access to reliable WiFi for cloud backup
  • ✅ Those who can plan around 10-30 minute recording sessions

Who Should NOT Buy It?

  • ❌ Anyone filming in desert/extreme heat (Dubai, Sahara, Australian Outback)
  • ❌ Travelers relying solely on cloud backup in developing regions
  • ❌ Anyone visiting countries with strict filming/drone laws
  • ❌ Those needing 60+ minute continuous recording sessions

💡 The Bottom Line

The Insta360 X5 is a brilliant camera when conditions are right. PURE VIDEO mode produces genuinely stunning 360 footage. But it's not a "take anywhere, do anything" camera. Heat is its enemy. Slow internet makes cloud backup useless. And in some countries, it's simply illegal.

Know your destination before you pack it. Film in the right conditions, and it's magic. Push it too hard, and it shuts off — or gets confiscated.
