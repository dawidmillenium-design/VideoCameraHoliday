How to Film Sunsets & Golden Hour: Travel Video Tips

The most beautiful light of the day — here's how to capture it without blowing out the sky or losing the details.

Dawid Millennium — Travel Videographer

Travel vlogger since 2019. Tested 50+ cameras on real trips across Southeast Asia, Europe, and South America.

In Short

  • Always expose for highlights (the sky), never shadows — you can lift blacks in post but cannot recover clipped whites.
  • Set manual white balance to 5600K–6500K to preserve warm tones; Auto WB will neutralize the golden glow.
  • Use ND filters to maintain cinematic shutter speed when ambient light is still bright during early golden hour.
  • Shoot in log profile if possible to maximize dynamic range for extreme contrast scenes.
  • The best sunset footage happens 15 minutes AFTER the sun dips below horizon — don't pack up too early.

Golden hour and sunset are the holy grail of travel videography. The warm, directional light transforms ordinary landscapes into cinematic masterpieces. But these moments are also technically treacherous — extreme dynamic range, rapidly changing exposure, and deceptive color temperatures ruin more footage than any other scenario. This guide provides battle-tested techniques to consistently capture stunning sunset sequences regardless of your camera tier.

The Golden Rule: Expose for the Sky

Your camera's meter will try to average the scene, resulting in blown-out skies and muddy foregrounds. Override this instinct:

Solution: Use spot metering or manual exposure. Point at the brightest part of the sky (not directly at sun) and set exposure so highlights sit at -1.0 to -1.5 EV on your waveform/histogram. Foreground subjects will appear dark — this is correct. Lift shadows in post-production where recovery is possible.

Problem 1: Sky Blown Out

Clipped highlights lose all color gradient and texture, creating flat white patches that destroy mood. Prevention strategies:

  • Graduated ND Filters: Darken only the upper portion of frame while keeping foreground properly exposed. Essential for beach/ocean horizons.
  • Bracketing: Record multiple exposures and blend in post. Some cameras offer HDR video modes that automate this process.
  • Silhouette Technique: Embrace underexposure intentionally. Position subjects against bright sky as dark shapes — eliminates exposure conflict entirely.

Problem 2: People Too Dark

When exposing for sky, human subjects become unrecognizable shadows. Solutions without sacrificing highlight detail:

  • Reflectors: Bounce remaining sunlight onto faces using collapsible reflectors or even white clothing/towels.
  • LED Panels: Small battery-powered lights set to 3200K provide subtle fill without competing with natural warmth.
  • Positioning: Place subjects at angles where rim light outlines their form rather than requiring frontal illumination.
  • Log Profile Recovery: Shoot in flat profiles preserving 12+ stops dynamic range, then apply LUTs with shadow lifting in post.

Problem 3: White Balance Confusion

Auto White Balance fights the very warmth you're trying to capture, shifting colors toward neutral as light changes. Manual control is mandatory:

Light Condition Recommended Kelvin Effect
Early Golden Hour 5600K Natural warmth, slight enhancement
Late Golden Hour 6000K–6500K Intense orange/red tones preserved
Blue Hour (post-sunset) 4000K–4500K Maintains cool twilight atmosphere
Mixed Artificial/Natural Custom WB via grey card Balances streetlights with residual daylight

Camera Settings for Sunset

  • Shutter Speed: Maintain 180° rule (1/50 for 24fps, 1/60 for 30fps). Use ND filters as light fades to avoid raising ISO prematurely.
  • Aperture: Stop down to f/5.6–f/8 for sharper edges and increased depth of field. Wide apertures create focus challenges in low contrast.
  • ISO: Keep at base ISO as long as possible. Increase only when shutter/aperture limits reached. Modern sensors handle ISO 1600–3200 cleanly during blue hour.
  • Profile: Log/CineLike profiles preserve maximum dynamic range. Standard profiles clip highlights faster in high-contrast sunset scenes.
  • Focus: Switch to manual focus once locked. Autofocus hunts unpredictably in low-contrast twilight conditions.

Best Cameras for Golden Hour

Dynamic range determines sunset success more than resolution:

  • Sony a7CR: 15+ stops DR handles extreme contrast effortlessly. Full-frame sensor excels in blue hour transitions.
  • DJI Osmo Pocket 3: 1-inch sensor delivers surprising latitude for its size. Perfect for spontaneous sunset captures without setup time.
  • Fujifilm X-T50: Film simulations like "Classic Chrome" render sunset tones beautifully straight from camera, reducing grading workload.
  • iPhone 16 Pro: Computational HDR merges multiple exposures automatically. Best backup option when dedicated cameras aren't accessible.

Lens Choices

Lens characteristics dramatically affect sunset aesthetics:

  • Wide Angle (14–24mm): Captures expansive skies and environmental context. Ideal for establishing shots showing scale.
  • Standard Zoom (24–70mm): Versatile framing for both landscapes and portraits within same sequence.
  • Telephoto (70–200mm+): Compresses perspective making sun appear larger relative to foreground elements. Creates dramatic silhouettes.
  • Coating Quality: Multi-coated lenses resist flare when shooting toward light source. Vintage/uncoated glass produces artistic flares but reduces contrast.

Real-World Example: Filming a Sunset Beach Scene

  1. Arrive 45 minutes before actual sunset to scout composition and set up tripod.
  2. Attach graduated ND filter if horizon is visible; otherwise use solid ND for exposure control.
  3. Set manual WB to 5600K; lock focus on mid-distance subject.
  4. Expose for sky highlights (-1.5 EV); verify histogram shows no clipping on right side.
  5. Capture wide establishing shot as sun approaches horizon.
  6. Switch to telephoto for compressed sun-disc shots with foreground interest (rocks, palm trees).
  7. Continue filming 20 minutes post-sunset for blue hour ambient glow.
  8. Record 30 seconds of static frame for noise reduction reference in post.

Post-Production Grade

Enhance captured footage while maintaining naturalism:

  • Highlight Recovery: Pull back clipped areas using dedicated tools; avoid global exposure reduction which crushes shadows.
  • Warmth Adjustment: Add subtle orange/teal split toning — warm highlights, cool shadows — mimicking human perception of sunset.
  • Contrast Curve: Gentle S-curve adds punch without destroying recovered highlight/shadow detail.
  • Noise Reduction: Apply selectively to shadow areas lifted in post; preserve grain structure in midtones for organic feel.
  • Vignette: Subtle darkening of edges draws eye toward illuminated center subjects.

Checklist: Sunset Filming Kit

  • [ ] Tripod (essential for low-light stability)
  • [ ] Graduated ND filter set
  • [ ] Solid ND filters (variable or fixed)
  • [ ] Collapsible reflector (white/silver/gold)
  • [ ] Small LED panel with 3200K gel
  • [ ] Grey card for custom white balance
  • [ ] Lens cleaning cloth (salt spray + sunset = smudges)
  • [ ] Headlamp (red light mode preserves night vision)
  • [ ] Extra batteries (cold evening temps drain faster)
  • [ ] Warm layers for yourself (comfort affects creativity)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I shoot sunset handheld successfully?

Possible but challenging. Use cameras with excellent IBIS like Sony a7CR or DJI Pocket 3. Increase shutter speed to 1/100s minimum to compensate for micro-movements. Accept slightly noisier footage over blurry results. Tripod remains strongly recommended.

Why does my sunset look orange in person but yellow on camera?

Auto White Balance neutralizes warm tones. Always use manual Kelvin settings between 5600K–6500K during golden hour. If shooting log profile, apply appropriate LUT in post that preserves warmth rather than correcting it.

Is blue hour worth filming after sunset?

Absolutely. The 20–30 minutes post-sunset often produce more usable footage than actual sunset. Ambient skylight provides even illumination perfect for cityscapes and silhouettes. Set WB to 4000K–4500K to maintain cool atmospheric tone.

Should I use polarizing filters during sunset?

Generally no. CPLs reduce overall light transmission by 1–2 stops and can create uneven sky darkening when shooting wide angles toward light source. Reserve CPLs for daytime landscape work; remove before golden hour begins.
