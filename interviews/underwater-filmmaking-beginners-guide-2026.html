Underwater Filmmaking for Beginners — Best Cameras & Tips (2026)

Filming underwater is different from filming on land. Here's how to capture stunning underwater travel footage — from snorkeling to scuba diving.

⚡ Quick Verdict: The GoPro Hero 13 is the best all-round underwater camera for most travelers. For serious diving, the Olympus TG-7 is unbeatable.

Why Underwater Filming Is Different

  • Color Loss: Red light disappears underwater — everything turns blue/green
  • Magnification: Water magnifies distance — get closer than you think
  • Stabilization: Your hands shake underwater — use trays or housings
  • Lighting: Colors fade at depth — bring underwater lights

Underwater Filming Tips

White Balance

Red light disappears underwater — use manual white balance or red filters. Many cameras have an "underwater" mode.

Stabilization

Use a tray/arm system — your hands shake underwater. Action cameras have built-in stabilization.

Lighting

Bring underwater lights — colors fade at depth. Red filters help restore color without lights.

Get Close

Water magnifies distance — get closer than you think. For macro shots, be within 10-30cm.

What to Pack for Underwater Filming

  • Camera: Waterproof action camera or compact + housing
  • Housing: For non-waterproof cameras (always test before you go)
  • Filters: Red or magenta filters for color correction
  • Lights: Underwater LED lights for deeper dives
  • Tray/Handle: For stability and control
  • Float Strap: So you don't lose your camera

Filming Underwater — Step by Step

  1. Test your housing: Always test before your trip — a leak ruins your camera
  2. Set white balance: Use "underwater" mode or manual
  3. Get close: Water magnifies distance — get within 1-2 meters
  4. Stay still: Move slowly — fast movements create shaky footage
  5. Shoot from below: Looking up creates dramatic shots
  6. Record long clips: Underwater, you can't reshoot easily — record 10-20 seconds
Pro Tip: "For the best underwater footage, get as close as possible and shoot with the sun behind you. Colors are most vibrant in the first 5 meters of depth."
