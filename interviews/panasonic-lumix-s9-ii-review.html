Panasonic Lumix S9 II — The Compact Full-Frame Camera Gets Better

The original S9 brought full-frame to the compact space. The S9 II promises to fix its biggest flaws. Here is what travel creators need to know about the 2026 update.

In Short

  • The Panasonic Lumix S9 II is expected to launch in late 2026, celebrating Lumix's 25th anniversary.
  • The biggest expected upgrade is the addition of Phase-Detect Autofocus (PDAF), fixing the original S9's main weakness.
  • A built-in Electronic Viewfinder (EVF) is highly anticipated, making it much more usable in bright sunlight.
  • It remains one of the smallest, lightest full-frame cameras on the market, perfect for long travel days.

When Panasonic released the original Lumix S9, it was a breath of fresh air in the mirrorless market. It proved that full-frame quality didn't have to come in a massive, heavy brick of a camera. With its vibrant color options, intuitive LUT support, and pocketable form factor, it quickly became a favorite among hybrid travel creators.

However, it wasn't perfect. The contrast-detect autofocus struggled with fast-moving subjects, and the lack of a built-in viewfinder was a dealbreaker for some traditional photographers. Enter the Panasonic Lumix S9 II. Expected to arrive this September, the S9 II is designed to address these exact pain points while keeping the compact magic alive.

S9 II Expected Upgrades

Based on industry leaks and Panasonic's recent trajectory with the S5 II and S1H II, here is what we expect from the S9 II:

  • Phase-Detect Autofocus (PDAF): This is the holy grail for S9 users. The S9 II is expected to finally adopt Panasonic's excellent PDAF system, bringing subject tracking and eye-AF that actually works for video and fast-paced street photography.
  • Built-in Electronic Viewfinder (EVF): The original S9 relied entirely on the rear LCD. The S9 II is rumored to feature a compact, high-resolution EVF, making it infinitely more usable for shooting in bright daylight or for photographers who prefer the traditional shooting experience.
  • Improved Ergonomics: Expect a slightly deeper grip and better button placement to make handling with larger L-mount lenses much more comfortable.
  • Enhanced Video Features: While the S9 was already a video powerhouse, the S9 II will likely include open-gate recording, better heat dissipation for longer 4K/60p shoots, and updated LUT workflows.

S9 II vs. Sony a7CR: The Compact Full-Frame Battle

The Verdict: The Sony a7CR is still the better choice for pure landscape photographers who need 61 megapixels for massive prints. However, for hybrid creators who prioritize video features, color science, and a lower price point, the S9 II is shaping up to be the ultimate travel workhorse.

The L-Mount Travel Lens Ecosystem

One of the biggest advantages of the Lumix S series is the L-Mount Alliance, which includes Panasonic, Sigma, and Leica. This gives you access to some of the best compact travel lenses on the market:

  • The Ultimate Travel Zoom: Sigma 28-70mm f/2.8 DG DN Contemporary. At just 470g, it is incredibly light for a full-frame f/2.8 zoom, making it the perfect pairing for the S9 II.
  • The Compact Prime: Panasonic Lumix S 50mm f/1.8. Tiny, sharp, and affordable. Perfect for low-light street photography and achieving beautiful background blur.

Should You Wait or Buy the Original S9?

If you are a video-first creator who loves applying custom LUTs and doesn't mind using the rear screen, the original Lumix S9 is still an incredible camera and can often be found at a significant discount now that the S9 II is on the horizon.

However, if you need reliable autofocus for tracking moving subjects (like kids or pets on vacation) or you absolutely require a viewfinder for shooting in bright sunlight, it is highly recommended to wait for the S9 II.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the S9 II overheat during long video shoots?

Panasonic has significantly improved heat dissipation in their recent full-frame bodies. While the original S9 could get warm, the S9 II is expected to feature a larger chassis and improved internal cooling to handle 4K/60p recording for extended periods without shutting down.

Is the S9 II weather-sealed?

Yes. Like the original S9 and the higher-end S5 II, the S9 II is expected to feature robust dust and splash resistance, making it perfectly safe for rainy travel days and humid environments.

Can I use my existing L-mount lenses on the S9 II?

Absolutely. The S9 II uses the standard L-mount. All your existing Panasonic, Sigma, and Leica L-mount lenses will work perfectly, and many will benefit from the improved autofocus system.
