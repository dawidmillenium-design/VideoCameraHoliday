Cruise travel is booming, and more creators want to document their voyages. But cruise ships present a unique set of challenges that most camera guides don't address: blinding ocean glare, cramped cabin spaces, strict indoor filming rules, and the constant motion of the ship itself.

After filming on Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, and Carnival cruises across the Caribbean and Mediterranean, here is our definitive guide to the best cameras and techniques for cruise ship videography.

Cruise Ship Filming Challenges

  • Ocean Glare: The sun reflecting off the water creates extreme contrast. Without a polarizing filter, your footage will be washed out and overexposed.
  • Cramped Spaces: Cabin balconies are tiny. You need compact gear that doesn't require a full tripod setup.
  • Indoor Lighting: Dining rooms and theaters use warm, dim lighting that challenges most cameras' low-light performance.
  • Ship Motion: Even on calm seas, there's constant subtle movement. You need excellent stabilization.

Balcony Time-Lapses & Sunsets

Balcony time-lapses are the crown jewel of cruise footage. Here's how to nail them:

  • Compact Tripod: Bring a small tabletop tripod that can clamp to the balcony railing. Full-size tripods are not allowed on most cruise lines.
  • Interval Settings: Set your camera to shoot one frame every 5-10 seconds for 30-60 minutes during sunset.
  • Lock Exposure: Use manual mode or AE lock to prevent the camera from adjusting exposure as the light changes.
  • Polarizing Filter: Essential for cutting glare and making the ocean look deep blue instead of washed-out white.
Port Day Pro Tip: Cruise ship excursions often involve water activities (snorkeling, jet skis, beach days). Bring a waterproof action camera with a floaty grip. The GoPro Hero 13 or DJI Action 5 Pro are perfect for this. Don't risk your expensive mirrorless camera in salt water.

Low-Light Dining & Shows

Cruise ship dining rooms and theaters use warm, dim lighting that's challenging for most cameras. Here's how to handle it:

  • Fast Lenses: Use lenses with f/1.8-f/2.8 apertures to gather more light.
  • High ISO: Don't be afraid to push ISO to 1600-3200. Modern cameras handle this well.
  • Stabilization: Enable image stabilization or use a gimbal to compensate for slower shutter speeds.
  • Respect the Rules: Many cruise lines prohibit flash photography in dining rooms and theaters. Check the policy before filming.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bring a drone on a cruise ship?

Most major cruise lines (Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, Carnival) prohibit drones on board. Some allow them in checked luggage but not in carry-ons, and you cannot fly them on the ship. Always check your cruise line's specific policy before packing.

Are tripods allowed on cruise ships?

Small tabletop tripods (under 12" when extended) are generally allowed. Full-size tripods are usually prohibited for safety reasons. For balcony time-lapses, bring a compact travel tripod or a flexible tripod like the Joby GorillaPod.

What's the best lens for cruise ship filming?

A versatile 24-70mm f/2.8 zoom covers 90% of situations. For balcony sunsets, add a polarizing filter. For shows and dining, the fast f/2.8 aperture handles low light beautifully. Avoid bringing multiple heavy lenses — cabin storage is limited.
