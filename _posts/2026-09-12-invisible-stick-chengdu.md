## Shooting 360 Photos in Chengdu

### Why 360 Cameras Are Perfect for Chengdu

Chengdu's streets are dense, layered, and chaotic in the best way. A traditional mirrorless camera forces you to choose a single frame — you miss the mahjong players on your left, the steaming dan dan noodle cart on your right, and the temple roofline above your head. A 360 camera captures all of it in a single exposure. When you pair it with an invisible stick, you get the best of both worlds: a complete spherical record of the scene *and* the ability to reframe it later into a dozen different "normal" shots, all without the stick appearing in frame.

The invisible stick works on a simple principle. Because a 360 camera shoots in every direction simultaneously, the stick sits directly below the lens in the camera's blind spot — the narrow vertical band between the two opposing lenses. The camera's stitching software then removes the stick (and often your hand) automatically, producing footage where the camera appears to hover in mid-air. In a city like Chengdu, where verticality matters — pagodas, skyscrapers, bamboo groves — this floating perspective is transformative.

### The Core Technique

Here's how to nail the invisible stick effect, whether you're shooting stills or 4K video:

- **Hold the stick perfectly vertical.** Any tilt shifts the stick out of the blind spot and into a visible lens.
- **Extend the stick as far as you comfortably can.** Longer extension = more dramatic floating effect, and it moves your hand further from the lens seam.
- **Keep your hand below the camera body.** Grip the base, not the midpoint.
- **Match the stick's width to the camera's blind spot.** Too thick and it bleeds into frame; too thin and it wobbles in wind.
- **Shoot in good light.** Image stabilization and stitching both perform better when the camera isn't fighting noise.

For video, the game-changer is **image stabilization**. Modern 360 cameras like the Insta360 X4 and X5 use gyroscopic data plus horizon lock to keep your floating shot buttery smooth even while you walk. In Chengdu's crowded pedestrian zones, this is essential — you'll be moving through crowds, and you want the result to feel like a drone shot, not a bumpy handheld.

### Still Photos vs. Video

**360 photos** are ideal for Chengdu's architectural landmarks: the sweeping courtyards of Wuhou Shrine, the layered eaves of temples, the geometric chaos of Chunxi Road at night. You shoot once and reframe endlessly.

**360 video** shines for movement: walking tours, market strolls, and the classic "camera floats ahead of me" shot. Just remember that 4K video eats storage fast — budget for high-capacity microSD cards (V30 or faster) and consider a compact power bank for top-ups between locations.

## Best Locations for Invisible Stick in Chengdu

Chengdu rewards planning. Here are the spots where an invisible stick shot will genuinely elevate your footage, plus practical notes for each.

### Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding

This is the marquee location, and for good reason. The bamboo-lined walkways and open enclosures give you clean sightlines and natural framing. A floating 360 shot following a path toward an enclosure — with pandas visible in the reframed view — is the kind of clip that performs everywhere.

- **Best time:** Arrive at opening (7:30–8:00 AM). Pandas are active, crowds are thinner, and the light is soft.
- **Technique:** Use a longer stick extension and walk slowly. Keep the camera at chest height for a natural "floating eye" perspective.
- **Watch out:** Some indoor viewing areas restrict tripods and large rigs. An invisible stick reads as a handheld monopod, which is usually fine, but be ready to collapse it if asked.

### Jinli Street

Jinli Street is a restored Qing-dynasty-style commercial street packed with lanterns, snack stalls, and red-painted timber. It's a photographer's playground and a nightmare for clean compositions — which is exactly why 360 shines here. You capture the whole sensory overload, then reframe the shot you actually want.

- **Best time:** Blue hour into early evening, when the lanterns glow and the sky still holds color.
- **Technique:** A slow walking shot with horizon lock creates a mesmerizing floating glide through the crowd. For stills, shoot from the center of the lane and reframe toward the ornate gateways.
- **Watch out:** It gets extremely crowded. Extend your stick high to clear heads, and keep the camera above eye level.

### Wuhou Shrine

Wuhou Shrine (Wuhou Ci) is a serene complex of gardens, halls, and ancient cypress trees dedicated to Zhuge Liang and Liu Bei. The symmetry and greenery make it ideal for 360 compositions where you want a calm, contemplative mood.

- **Best time:** Mid-morning on a weekday.
- **Technique:** Use the invisible stick to create a "floating through the garden gate" shot. The red walls and green foliage give beautiful color contrast in-camera.
- **Watch out:** This is an active cultural site. Be respectful, avoid blocking pathways, and never extend your stick into restricted areas or over barriers.

### Bonus Spots Worth the Trip

- **Taikoo Li & Chunxi Road:** For neon, glass, and modern Chengdu. Great for night 360 video.
- **People's Park:** Tea houses, bamboo, and the famous blind-date corner. Excellent for candid spherical stills.
- **Anshun Bridge:** Riverside night shots with reflections — a floating shot over the water's edge is stunning.

## Tips for China

Traveling and shooting in China comes with its own set of realities. Here's what every 360 shooter should know before landing in Chengdu.

### Connectivity and Apps

Many Western apps and services are restricted in mainland China. This affects your workflow in two big ways:

- **Download everything before you fly.** Install your camera's companion app (Insta360, GoPro, etc.), offline maps, and a VPN if you rely on Google services. App stores may not update reliably once you're in-country.
- **Test your camera's Wi-Fi transfer.** Some camera apps need to phone home for activation. Activate and update firmware at home.

### Power and Plugs

China uses **220V** and Type A/C/I plugs. Bring a universal adapter and a compact power strip — hotel outlets are often scarce, and you'll be charging a camera, phone, power bank, and possibly a laptop.

### Payments

Mobile payment (WeChat Pay, Alipay) dominates. Link an international card before you travel; cash is accepted but increasingly inconvenient. A small amount of RMB is still useful for small vendors.

### Etiquette and Rules

- **Drones are heavily restricted** in Chengdu, especially near the panda base and central districts. Your invisible stick is a huge advantage here — it gives you aerial-style shots *without* a drone and without the regulatory headaches.
- **Respect no-photo signs**, especially in temple interiors and museum exhibits.
- **Ask before filming people up close.** A 360 camera captures everyone in the sphere; be mindful and kind.

### Weather

Chengdu is famously overcast and humid — the "fog city" reputation is real. This is actually good news for 360 shooters: soft, diffused light is flattering and reduces harsh shadows. But it also means you should prioritize **weather-sealed** gear or at least carry a rain cover, and keep silica gel packs in your bag to fight humidity.
