## Shooting 360 Photos in Dhaka

Dhaka is, frankly, a 360 photographer's dream and nightmare rolled into one. The dream: sensory overload in every direction — hawkers, honking CNGs, colonial architecture, and riverscapes. The nightmare: that same chaos makes it brutally hard to get a clean shot. This is exactly where an invisible stick earns its keep.

### Why the Invisible Stick Matters Here

A 360 camera captures everything around it, including the pole you're holding. The invisible stick works by keeping the camera directly above the monopod's vertical axis. Because the camera's two lenses stitch a full sphere, the thin stick sits in the "blind zone" between the lenses and gets automatically removed by the stitching software. The result: your camera appears to hover in space, and you — the photographer — are nowhere to be seen.

In Dhaka, that invisibility is a superpower. You can stand in the middle of a crowded Baitul Mukarram plaza, extend the stick three meters above the crowd, and capture a clean, elevated bubble that no one else can replicate. No drone permits, no ladder, no confrontation.

### The Core Technique

Here's the workflow that works in Dhaka's tight, unpredictable spaces:

1. **Mount the camera dead-center** on the stick's 1/4"-20 thread. Off-center mounting creates parallax that ruins the stitch.
2. **Extend the stick vertically**, not at an angle. Angled sticks create a visible diagonal artifact.
3. **Hold still for 2–3 seconds** while the camera fires. Dhaka's crowds move fast; motion blur on people is fine, but motion blur on *you* is not.
4. **Shoot in HDR or bracketed mode** when the sun is brutal. Dhaka's midday light is unforgiving — you'll want the extra dynamic range.
5. **Stitch in-camera or in Insta360 Studio / GoPro Player**, then reframe in post.

### The Dhaka Light Problem

Between November and February, Dhaka enjoys a brief, gorgeous "winter" with soft light and clear skies. From March onward, the heat haze and dust roll in, and by monsoon (June–September), you're dealing with flat gray skies and sudden downpours. Plan your 360 shoots around:

- **Golden hour (6:00–7:30 AM and 5:00–6:30 PM)** for warm, directional light.
- **Blue hour** for the National Parliament House and Ahsan Manzil, which light up beautifully.
- **Overcast days** for even, shadowless 360s that stitch cleanly.

## Best Locations for Invisible Stick in Dhaka

Not every spot rewards a floating camera. Some are too tight, too dark, or too sensitive to photography. Here's a curated list of the **best invisible stick for Dhaka photography** locations, ranked by how well they play with the technique.

### 1. Lalbagh Fort

The 17th-century Mughal complex is a 360 goldmine. The central tomb of Pari Bibi, the Diwan-i-Aam, and the surrounding gardens give you layered depth in every direction. Extend your stick above the central pathway at sunrise and you'll capture the fort's symmetry with the camera hovering like a ghost.

- **Best time:** 7:00–9:00 AM (before tour buses arrive)
- **Watch for:** Guards who may ask you to lower the stick. Keep it under 2 meters here.
- **Tip:** Use the stick to shoot *down* into the gardens from above the parapet for a unique top-down bubble.

### 2. Ahsan Manzil

The Pink Palace on the Buriganga riverfront is arguably Dhaka's most photogenic building. The problem: it's often packed. The solution: a tall invisible stick. Raise your camera 3 meters above the crowd in the front lawn and you get a clean, elevated 360 of the dome and facade with the river glinting behind.

- **Best time:** Late afternoon, when the pink stone glows
- **Watch for:** Riverfront humidity — bring a microfiber cloth for lens fog
- **Tip:** Combine with a walk to the nearby Buckland Bund for a riverside 360.

### 3. National Parliament House (Jatiya Sangsad Bhaban)

Louis Kahn's modernist masterpiece is a geometric feast. The surrounding lake and manicured grounds make for stunning 360 compositions. Note that the immediate perimeter is a high-security zone — you can shoot from the public road and the lake's edge, but not from inside the compound.

- **Best time:** Blue hour, when the building is floodlit
- **Watch for:** Security personnel. Be respectful, keep your stick low, and don't linger too long.
- **Tip:** The reflections in the lake are perfect for a low-angle 360 with the stick extended horizontally over the water.

### 4. Old Dhaka Streets (Shakhari Bazar, Sadarghat)

This is where the invisible stick truly shines. The narrow lanes of Shakhari Bazar are impossibly dense — a normal camera gives you a wall of backs. A 360 camera on a 2-meter stick rises above the crowd and captures the entire street's texture: the Hindu temples, the brass shops, the rickshaws.

- **Best time:** Early morning (6:00–8:00 AM) before the crush
- **Watch for:** Overhead wires — Dhaka's streets are strung with them, and your stick will snag
- **Tip:** Use a shorter stick (1–1.5m) in the narrowest lanes to avoid tangling.

### 5. Hatirjheel

The revitalized lake and expressway is Dhaka's favorite evening hangout. The curved walkways, bridges, and water reflections give you endless 360 compositions. The invisible stick lets you shoot from above the railings for a clean, unobstructed panorama.

- **Best time:** Sunset and after, when the lights come on
- **Watch for:** Crowds on weekends — go on a weekday
- **Tip:** The pedestrian bridge near Rampura is a perfect elevated platform.

### 6. Curzon Hall & Dhaka University Campus

The red-brick colonial architecture and leafy avenues are a quieter alternative to the tourist hotspots. Great for architectural 360s with clean sightlines.

## Tips for Bangladesh

Shooting 360 in Dhaka isn't just about gear — it's about navigating a city that operates on its own rhythm. Here's how to do it without losing your camera, your cool, or your shots.

### Safety and Etiquette

- **Crowds are curious.** The moment you extend a stick, you become a spectacle. Expect questions, requests for photos, and kids wanting to see the screen. Embrace it — it's part of the experience.
- **Avoid sensitive areas.** Don't shoot near military installations, the airport perimeter, or government buildings with visible security. When in doubt, ask.
- **Keep your gear close.** Dhaka is generally safe for tourists, but petty theft happens in crowded markets. Use a wrist strap and never set your camera down.
- **Dress modestly** when shooting in Old Dhaka, near mosques, and in religious areas.

### Weather and Environment

- **Dust is your enemy.** Dhaka's air quality is notoriously poor, especially in winter. Dust settles on 360 lenses and creates smudges that ruin stitches. Carry a lens pen and microfiber cloth at all times.
- **Humidity fogs lenses.** Going from an air-conditioned car into Dhaka's humid streets will fog your lenses instantly. Let your camera acclimatize for 10 minutes before shooting.
- **Monsoon = drama.** Rainy season gives you moody skies and reflective streets — but also risks water damage. A **weather-sealed** camera or a rain cover is essential if you shoot between June and September.
- **Heat drains batteries.** Dhaka's summer heat (35°C+) accelerates battery drain. Carry spares and keep them out of direct sun.

### Power and Connectivity

- **Battery life** on 360 cameras is typically 60–90 minutes of continuous shooting. For a full day in Dhaka, carry 3–4 batteries and a power bank.
- **Charging** is easy — Bangladesh uses Type C, D, and G plugs at 220V. Bring a universal adapter.
- **Wi-Fi and data** are widely available. A local SIM (Grameenphone, Robi, Banglalink) gives you cheap data for uploading and cloud stitching.

### Language and Interaction

A few Bangla phrases go a long way:

- *"Ektu shoray jan"* — Please move aside a little
- *"Chobi tulte pari?"* — May I take a photo?
- *"Dhonnobad"* — Thank you

People in Dhaka are overwhelmingly warm and curious. A smile and a request usually gets you the shot.

### The Invisible Stick: What to Look For

Not all invisible sticks are created equal. The key factors for Dhaka:

- **Length:** 1–3 meters. Longer sticks give more elevation but are harder to control in crowds and wind.
- **Rigidity:** A wobbly stick creates motion blur and stitching errors. Carbon fiber is lighter and stiffer than aluminum.
- **Mount:** Standard 1/4"-20 thread. Some cameras (like Insta360) use a proprietary quick-release — check compatibility.
- **Weight:** Under 500g for a 2m stick is ideal for all-day shooting.
- **Grip:** Rubberized or foam grip for sweaty Dhaka summers.

Many photographers ask whether they should bring a **mirrorless** camera alongside their 360 rig. My answer: yes, if you have room. A mirrorless body with a wide lens gives you high-resolution stills that 360 cameras can't match, especially in low light. Use the 360 for immersive scenes and the mirrorless for detail shots. The two complement each other perfectly.

### Image Stabilization and 4K Video

If you're shooting **4K video** on your 360 camera, **image stabilization** is critical. Dhaka's streets are bumpy — rickshaws, CNGs, and uneven pavement will shake your footage. Both Insta360's FlowState and GoPro's HyperSmooth do an excellent job, but they work best when you:

- Walk smoothly, with bent knees
- Keep the stick vertical
- Avoid rapid pans
- Shoot at higher frame rates (60fps) for smoother slow-motion

## Post-Processing Workflow for Dhaka Shots

Once you're back at your hotel, the real work begins. Here's a streamlined workflow:

1. **Transfer files** to your laptop or phone via Wi-Fi or card reader.
2. **Stitch** in Insta360 Studio, GoPro Player, or Kandao Studio. Most modern cameras stitch in-camera, but desktop software gives you more control.
3. **Reframe** your 360 into standard 16:9 or 9:16 clips using keyframes. This is where you decide what the viewer sees.
4. **Color grade** — Dhaka's light is warm and hazy; a slight contrast boost and dehaze will make your shots pop.
5. **Export** at 4K or higher for social media, or full 8K for archival.

### Reframing Tips for Dhaka

- **Use the stick as a pivot point** — reframe around it for smooth reveals.
- **Lead with motion** — start on a rickshaw and pull back to reveal the street.
- **Embrace the chaos** — Dhaka's density is the story. Don't over-clean your shots.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best invisible stick for Dhaka photography?

The **Insta360 3m carbon fiber selfie stick** is the best all-rounder. It's rigid, lightweight, and pairs perfectly with Insta360 cameras. For tighter spaces like Shakhari Bazar, the 1.2m version is more manageable.

### Can I use an invisible stick with any 360 camera?

Yes, as long as the camera has a standard 1/4"-20 mount and stitches out the nadir (bottom) of the sphere. Insta360, GoPro Max, and Kandao cameras all support this.

### Is it safe to shoot 360 in Dhaka?

Generally yes, but be mindful of crowds, sensitive areas, and overhead wires. Keep your gear secure and respect local customs.

### Do I need a permit to shoot 360 in Dhaka?

For public spaces, no. For government buildings like the National Parliament House, shoot only from public areas and avoid the secured perimeter.

### What's the best time of year to shoot 360 in Dhaka?

November to February offers the best light and clearest skies. Avoid the monsoon (June–September) unless you're prepared for rain and humidity.
